Care work is team work. In this lesson you learn where to find your role and responsibilities, how to work well with colleagues, how to speak up when something is not fair, and how to keep getting better at your job.

1 Know your role: the documents that tell you **

How do you know what your job is? Read the documents your workplace gives you. Your assessment asks you to name two.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP7 S1 P1Position description (job description): the tasks you do, the rules you follow and your responsibilities.
  2. NP7 S1 P2Code of conduct: how every worker must behave.
  3. NP7 S1 P3Policy on worker rights, roles and responsibilities: what you can expect, and what is expected of you.
  4. NP7 S1 P4Daily work list or roster: who you support today, when, and what tasks you do.
  5. NP7 S1 P5Care plans (individual plans): what each person needs and how they like things done.

2 Working together with colleagues **

To collaborate means to work together towards the same goal. Your assessment asks you to tick three things that matter. They are below in the same words.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP7 S2 P1Agreeing on timeframes for completing activities: everyone knows what must be done, and by when.
  2. NP7 S2 P2Identifying lines of communication between other services: you know who to talk to, in your team and in other services, and how (phone, email, handover, the supervisor).
  3. NP7 S2 P3Using industry terminology correctly: everyone in the team uses the same work words with the same meaning.
  4. NP7 S2 P4Not right for collaborating: Allocate tasks to a colleague when they are not present; Only listen to the supervisor; Adopt others' communication mannerisms so that they feel comfortable.
  5. NP7 S2 P5Good teams also share information on time, help each other, and follow the communication protocols (the agreed way to pass on information).

3 Passive, aggressive, assertive **

There are three main communication styles. Only one of them works well at work.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP7 S3 P1Passive: you keep quiet to keep others happy. You do not say what you need. Problems stay and you feel frustrated.
  2. NP7 S3 P2Aggressive: you push your needs in a loud, rude or blaming way. You ignore other people's feelings. This causes arguments.
  3. NP7 S3 P3Assertive: you say clearly and calmly what you think and need, and you respect the other person. This is the style to use at work.
  4. NP7 S3 P4Assertive sentences often start with "I": "I noticed...", "I felt...", "I need..." instead of "You always..."

4 Maree and Louise: speaking up the right way **

The story. You work at a day respite centre with two colleagues, Maree and Louise. Three staff should share the work: set up activities, make and serve lunch, then tidy up. Maree and Louise are close friends. People say Maree has just separated from her husband, and she looks like she has been crying. All day they took long breaks and left you alone with the people. The last person left 15 minutes ago. You have not seen Maree or Louise for 30 minutes, and the room still needs tidying. You feel frustrated.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP7 S4 P1Use assertive communication. Stay calm and polite, and do not gossip about Maree's private life.
  2. NP7 S4 P2Ask Maree and Louise for a short meeting, in private.
  3. NP7 S4 P3Tell them what you saw: they were away for long periods during the day and during the clean-up.
  4. NP7 S4 P4Tell them how it affected you: you were left on your own and felt frustrated.
  5. NP7 S4 P5Tell them there are still jobs to finish, and ask them to help you now.
  6. NP7 S4 P6If it cannot be solved between you, speak to your supervisor or manager.

5 Solving conflict step by step

When there is a conflict at work, follow a process. Here are two simple ones.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP7 S5 P1Step 1. Set the scene: say what the conflict is and agree on a time and place to talk (a meeting, face-to-face, or online).
  2. NP7 S5 P2Step 2. Gather information: listen to both sides. What does each person need?
  3. NP7 S5 P3Step 3. Be respectful and open-minded: be honest, do not argue, listen without interrupting, stay assertive.
  4. NP7 S5 P4Step 4. Agree on the problem: find the real issue and the outcome everyone wants.
  5. NP7 S5 P5Step 5. Brainstorm solutions: break the problem into small parts and share ideas.
  6. NP7 S5 P6Step 6. Agree on a plan: each person knows what they will do. Talk about how to stop it happening again.
  7. NP7 S5 P7The 3-step approach: 1. Self-check: am I calm enough to solve this? 2. Honest communication: use "I" messages and listen to the other side. 3. Find a solution together.

6 Performance appraisals **

A performance appraisal is a meeting where your manager looks at your work with you: what you do well, what to improve, and your goals. Your assessment asks for five purposes and the two people who are there. The words below are the same as your assessment.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP7 S6 P1Purpose: Look at options for skill development training.
  2. NP7 S6 P2Purpose: Identify areas for improvement.
  3. NP7 S6 P3Purpose: Get feedback on what you are doing well.
  4. NP7 S6 P4Purpose: Set future goals.
  5. NP7 S6 P5Purpose: Reflect on work performance since the last appraisal.
  6. NP7 S6 P6Who attends: Supervisor / Manager and You. Clients and colleagues do not attend. (The manager may use feedback from others, but only you and the manager are in the meeting.)
  7. NP7 S6 P7Not the purpose: Opportunity to complain about the organisation; To meet with the supervisor if someone has made a complaint about you; To rate your popularity with clients; To terminate your employment.

7 Continuous improvement: getting better every day **

Continuous improvement means we keep looking for better ways to give care. Every worker can help. Your assessment asks you to tick four ways. They are below in the same words.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP7 S7 P1Speak up about work practices that could be changed for the better: if you see a better or safer way, say so.
  2. NP7 S7 P2Promote change to other colleagues: help your team try the new way.
  3. NP7 S7 P3Attend training so you can do your job better.
  4. NP7 S7 P4Seek advice from the supervisor on skill development opportunities.
  5. NP7 S7 P5Not these: Continue to do my work the way I want; Go along with what the supervisor decides; Displaying an indifferent attitude; Support others in adopting work practices that have been tried and tested by you. (Your own "tested" way is not a checked, approved practice.)

8 Inclusion and cultural safety at work **

A good workplace is safe and welcoming for everyone: staff and the people we support.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP7 S8 P1Examples of inclusion that build positive, safe relationships at work: having cross-cultural teams; asking everyone for ideas before decisions; inviting all staff to social outings; showing posters and materials from other cultures; celebrating cultural events; treating everybody equally; getting to know people's lives and backgrounds.
  2. NP7 S8 P2Cultural safety means people feel safe to be who they are, and their culture is respected. The person decides if they feel safe.
  3. NP7 S8 P3Four practices that make a workplace culturally safe for all (the same words as your assessment): All staff feeling comfortable contributing to discussions and decisions; Mandatory cultural awareness training for all staff; Displaying empathy and respect; Using negotiation and collaboration skills.
  4. NP7 S8 P4Not culturally safe: Having separate staff rooms for staff of different cultures; Using stereotyped slang terms during conversations; Promoting own agenda as best practice; Raising your voice.
  5. NP7 S8 P5In the practical skills day your teacher watches that you respect the person's beliefs, practices, values and views, even when they are different from yours.

9 Digital media at work, and the social media policy **

Workplaces use many kinds of digital media. Your assessment asks for three types and how each one is used.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP7 S9 P1Web (internet): to look up information, contact funding bodies, send reports, order supplies and download forms.
  2. NP7 S9 P2Email: to send information and messages to other people.
  3. NP7 S9 P3Social media: the organisation shares good news and its services, reaches many people at low cost, and finds new staff.
  4. NP7 S9 P4Podcasts and videos: information is recorded, so people can watch it later and nobody misses out. It is also cheap.
  5. NP7 S9 P5Tablets and apps: take notes in meetings and visits, and work away from the office. Apps make work faster.
  6. NP7 S9 P6Newsletters and broadcasts: to share information with many people.
  7. NP7 S9 P7Intranet (the organisation's own private website): staff find policies, procedures and forms, and share files between teams.
  8. NP7 S9 P8Why follow the organisation's social media policy? Because it gives clear rules on what is acceptable and not acceptable on social media for workers. It protects the people you support (privacy), you and the organisation.
