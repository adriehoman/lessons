2 AT2: the Diversity conversation, and the rules **

You have a conversation with a person who is not your family or a close friend. In class, your teacher puts you with a classmate you do not know well. You learn about their beliefs, practices, values and views, and they learn about yours.

The rules for AT2: work in pairs, and all pairs talk at the same time. The conversation takes 15 to 20 minutes. Then you have about 20 minutes to complete the Diversity Conversation form. Then your teacher asks you six questions (about 15 to 20 minutes). What your partner shares stays private.

The Diversity Conversation form

Have a conversation with a person who is not part of your immediate family or a close friend.

The aim: explore different beliefs, practices, values and views of the cultures in your community.

Conversation time: 15 to 20 minutes.

Key topics may include:

  • Religious beliefs
  • Beliefs about death and dying
  • Family roles and responsibilities
  • Disability
  • Ageing
  • Politics

Download the form from OneNote: open the Content Library, then the communication unit (CHCCOM005 CHCDIV001). Use it to write your notes, and keep it for your teacher's questions.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP13 S2 P1A conversation, not an interrogation. An interrogation is a list of questions, one after the other, like a police interview. A conversation goes both ways: you ask, you listen, you share something about yourself too.
  2. NP13 S2 P2Interrogation: "What is your religion? Do you pray? How many children do you have? What do you think about politics?"
  3. NP13 S2 P3Conversation: "In my family, my grandmother lived with us when she got older. What happens in your family when people get older?" Then listen, and ask about what they said: "That is interesting. Can you tell me more about that?"
  4. NP13 S2 P4Use open questions (what, how, tell me about). Nod, smile, and keep eye contact if that is comfortable for your partner.
  5. NP13 S2 P5Summarise what you heard: "So, in your culture, the oldest son looks after the parents. Have I got that right?"
  6. NP13 S2 P6Some topics are sensitive (death, politics, religion). Ask gently. Your partner can say "I would rather not talk about that", and that is OK. Change the topic politely.
  7. NP13 S2 P7Look for the things you have in common. You will be surprised.

3 AT2: what your teacher watches for **

Your teacher has a checklist for your conversation. These are the skills, in simple words. Click through the cards.

Listen to the explanation

Verbal communication

Show respect and empathy. Ask open questions to learn about their culture. Speak clearly at a good speed, with a calm tone. Be open-minded and non-judgemental. Face the person. Use simple words: no slang or jargon.

Non-verbal communication

Use open body language and a smile to build rapport and trust.

Respect their views

Respect the person's beliefs, practices, values and views, so they feel culturally safe.

Listen, clarify, confirm

Pay attention, ask when you are not sure, say back what you heard, and respond.

Notice and fix problems early

Notice a difficult topic (for example a religious practice), a language barrier (you may need an interpreter), or a misunderstanding (for example about family roles). Fix it with good communication techniques: rephrase, use pictures, use an interpreter.

Legal and ethical

Show you understand privacy and confidentiality, disclosure, discrimination, consent, professional boundaries, duty of care, and conflict of interest.

Clarify first, share on time

Ask about the instructions before you start, and share information on time.

Work words

Use industry terminology correctly.

Self-awareness

Manage your own feelings, your face, your body language and your words, so you do not upset the other person. Work within your own limits.

4 AT2: your teacher's six questions **

After the conversation and the form, your teacher asks you six questions. They are about your conversation, so use your own examples. Here is what to include.

Listen to the explanation

Question 1 How did you adapt your communication style so it worked well?

Think about:

  • Your verbal and non-verbal communication.
  • How you sat or stood (positioning).
  • Your listening skills, and how you paraphrased (said it back in your own words).

Question 2 Did any topics or comments challenge your own values? How did you accept the differences?

Think about:

  • Name the diversity you found: culture, background, religion, gender, age, language, family views, and so on.
  • Everyone has the right to their own view, even when it is different from yours.
  • Explain how you kept working without judging.

Question 3 What factors changed your communication style and techniques?

Think about:

  • Think about your partner: their age, language, culture, mood, or how much they wanted to share. What did you change because of that?

Question 4 How did your own culture, attitudes and beliefs affect the conversation? What did you have in common?

Think about:

  • Name any biases or views of your own that you noticed.
  • List the things you had in common.
  • How did it feel to find things in common (or not)?

Question 5 What did you learn from talking with someone you did not know well? How can it improve your self-awareness?

Think about:

  • What you learned about your partner (keep private details confidential).
  • What you learned in general: people can share interests you did not expect; you understand differences better; you learn about other cultures; you practise building rapport.

Question 6 At work, who would you refer breaches or unresolved conflict to?

Think about:

  • Your supervisor or manager.

5 AT3: the group discussion, and the rules **

In the group discussion you talk about two workplace stories: Scenario 1 (Zia) and Scenario 3 (Allie). Both stories fit aged care and disability.

The rules for AT3: your teacher puts you in a group of 3, 4 or 5, depending on the size of the class. All groups talk at the same time. You have about 15 minutes for each story. Everyone talks, everyone listens, and every person writes their own notes on the Student Task Sheet (Appendix A). Then your teacher asks you four questions, and you write a short reflection.
Important group rule: if one person in the group is not satisfactory, the whole group is not satisfactory. So work together. Invite the quiet person to speak: "What do you think?" Help each other to use the legal and ethical words. When one of us passes, we all pass.
Listen to the explanation
  1. NP13 S5 P1For each story, your group talks about three questions: What are the differences that could cause a misunderstanding? What problems could they cause at work? What strategies could fix them, and who could help?
  2. NP13 S5 P2Write the names of your group members on your task sheet.
  3. NP13 S5 P3Write your notes in your own words. Do not copy another person's notes.
  4. NP13 S5 P4Disagree politely. Deal with a different view calmly and quickly: "I see it differently. Can we look at what the person needs?"
  5. NP13 S5 P5Submit your Student Task Sheet in Connect.

6 What your teacher watches for **

Your teacher has a checklist. These are the skills, in simple words. Click through the cards.

Listen to the explanation

Find the problems

Find the cultural differences, misunderstandings and problems in the story. Think about ageism, human rights, staff morale, and staff who do not understand other cultures.

Plan strategies

Suggest ways to lower the risk and to break down communication or cultural barriers: education, good communication, and the organisation's practices.

Choose how to communicate

Say which communication style and techniques fit the story best (for example, assertive, a private meeting, training, written information).

Good verbal and non-verbal communication

Open body language and a smile. A calm tone, clear words, a good speed. Show empathy, and manage your own feelings and beliefs.

Be culturally safe

Respect each person's beliefs, practices, values and views. Be open-minded and do not judge. Build trust.

Use work words correctly

Use industry terms correctly when you speak, and write your notes clearly and neatly.

Ask and clarify

Ask your teacher questions before you start. Ask for feedback. Deal with disagreements in your group calmly and quickly.

Know the law and ethics

Talk about privacy and confidentiality, discrimination, duty of care, work health and safety, the code of conduct, abuse and mandatory reporting, people's rights, and work role boundaries.

Listen and share information

Pay attention. Clarify, confirm and respond. Share information clearly and on time, keep it private, and avoid slang and jargon.

Value diversity

Show respect for diversity, use interpreters when there is a language barrier, and build good working relationships.

8 Scenario 1: Zia **

The story (in simple words). Zia is a new support worker at your organisation. She came to Australia from Iran in 1993, and she is Muslim. Her religion asks her to wear a burka, to fast during Ramadan, to pray five times a day at set times, and to follow rules about food.

Listen to the explanation

Talk 1 What differences could cause a misunderstanding or conflict at work?

Think about:

  • How do people with dementia, or with low hearing or eyesight, understand you? What do they need to see?
  • How might a person feel during a long shift with no food or drink in the daytime?
  • What happens to the work when someone stops to pray at set times?
  • Some support work involves food. Which tasks could be hard for Zia?

Talk 2 What problems could happen at work if these differences are not handled well?

Think about:

  • How could the people Zia supports feel?
  • How could the other staff feel? Could anyone think it is unfair?
  • Could there be a safety risk (WHS) for Zia or the people she supports?
  • What could happen to teamwork and staff morale?

Talk 3 What strategies could fix this sensitively and include everyone? Who could help?

Think about:

  • What could the team learn about Zia's culture and religion? What could Zia learn about her role?
  • How could the roster and shift times help, for example during Ramadan?
  • How could the team share the tasks in a fair way?
  • Who should be involved: Zia, the supervisor, the team, the people she supports, HR?

9 Scenario 3: Allie **

The story (in simple words). Allie is from Africa and works at your organisation. He supports four women. Because of his cultural beliefs, Allie looks down on women, and he finds it hard to give them his best care. One woman was a competitive swimmer. She swims several days a week with a local ladies' swimming club, and she loves it. Today Allie is trying to stop her from going.

Listen to the explanation

Talk 1 What differences could cause a misunderstanding or conflict at work?

Think about:

  • How does Allie see women? How does that fit with the care he gives?
  • Whose goals and choices come first in consumer-directed care?
  • Is the woman getting all her needs met, including her social and emotional needs?

Talk 2 What problems could happen at work if this is not handled well?

Think about:

  • How could the four women feel about the way Allie treats them?
  • What could happen between Allie and the other staff?
  • What happens to the swimmer's health, goals and happiness if she stops swimming?
  • Which rights and which standards could be broken?

Talk 3 What strategies could fix this sensitively and include everyone? Who could help?

Think about:

  • What does Allie need to learn: the Quality Standards or NDIS Practice Standards, consumer-directed care, his job description?
  • Why must Allie read and follow each woman's care plan and goals?
  • Could the roster be changed in a fair way?
  • Who should be involved: Allie, the supervisor, the women and their families, the team?

10 AT3: the questions your teacher asks **

After the discussion your teacher asks you four questions about each story. Answer about Zia and about Allie.

Listen to the explanation

Question 1 Name two cultural, personal or individual differences that could cause a misunderstanding or conflict at work.

Think about:

  • A difference is something about the person: their beliefs, practices, or views.

Question 2 For each difference, explain what problems could happen at work if it is not handled well.

Think about:

  • A problem is what could go wrong: for the person, the people they support, the team, or safety.

Question 3 For each problem, describe one strategy to lower the risk of a misunderstanding or conflict.

Think about:

  • A strategy is a plan to make it better: learning, rosters, sharing tasks, care plans.
  • Give one strategy for each problem. Count them.

Question 4 Name two communication strategies or techniques you could use for the issues in each story.

Think about:

  • A communication strategy is how you share the message: a staff meeting, training, a private talk, written information, a negotiation with the supervisor.

12 Afternoon: Observation Task 9, hoist transfer

This afternoon you work in pairs to move a person with a non-weight-bearing hoist (for example from the bed to a chair). This is part of your CHCCCS031 CHCCCS040 practical assessment. Your teacher watches these steps.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP13 S12 P1Before you start: check the person's individual plan for special needs, and get the equipment ready.
  2. NP13 S12 P2Hand hygiene.
  3. NP13 S12 P3Tell the person who you are, and ask for consent to help.
  4. NP13 S12 P4Get the area ready: remove hazards; check the weight and height limits of the hoist and the sling; check the hoist and sling are in good order (no frayed edges, battery charged); make a clear path with enough room; set the bed height and put the bed brakes on.
  5. NP13 S12 P5Use good body mechanics the whole time.
  6. NP13 S12 P6Put the sling on: keep the person's dignity; use smooth, coordinated log rolls to place the sling; lie the person on their back, check the sling fits the body and the leg straps are right (manufacturer's instructions); ask the person to fold their arms across their chest; bring the hoist over and attach the sling safely.
  7. NP13 S12 P7Do the transfer: lift smoothly and together; keep checking the sling (it does not slip or pinch); move the hoist safely over the new place; lower smoothly; use the hand grips to position the person well.
  8. NP13 S12 P8Take the sling off: unhook the sling and move the hoist away; use log rolls to remove the sling; help the person get comfortable.
  9. NP13 S12 P9Talk to the person the whole time: reassure them and tell them what is happening.
  10. NP13 S12 P10Put the hoist and sling back in storage.
  11. NP13 S12 P11Hand hygiene.

13 Afternoon: Observation Task 10A, falls recovery with a hoist

A person has fallen. You work in pairs to move them from the floor to a chair with a non-weight-bearing hoist. Your teacher watches these steps.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP13 S13 P1Give first aid straight away if it is needed.
  2. NP13 S13 P2Call the supervisor or RN to check the person for injuries. Follow their directions. Do not move the person until they say it is safe.
  3. NP13 S13 P3Explain the task to the person and ask for consent.
  4. NP13 S13 P4Get the area ready: remove hazards; check the weight and height limits of the hoist and sling; check they are in good order (no frayed edges, battery charged); make a clear path with enough room.
  5. NP13 S13 P5Use good body mechanics the whole time (bend your knees, keep your back straight).
  6. NP13 S13 P6Put the sling on: keep the person's dignity; use coordinated log rolls to place the sling; check the sling fits and the leg straps are right; ask the person to fold their arms; bring the hoist over and attach the sling safely.
  7. NP13 S13 P7Do the transfer: lift smoothly and together; keep checking the sling; move the hoist safely over the chair; lower smoothly; use the hand grips to sit the person well in the chair.
  8. NP13 S13 P8Take the sling off safely and help the person get comfortable.
  9. NP13 S13 P9Reassure the person and tell them what is happening the whole time.
  10. NP13 S13 P10Put the hoist and sling back in storage.
  11. NP13 S13 P11Hand hygiene. Then report and document the fall (incident report and progress note: see Lesson 4).
