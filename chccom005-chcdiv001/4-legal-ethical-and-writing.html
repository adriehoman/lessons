In this lesson you learn the rules that guide your communication at work, and how to write it down the right way. Your notes and reports are legal documents: other people, and sometimes a court, may read them.

1 Legal and ethical: what is the difference? **

Two words you will meet in every unit.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP9 S1 P1Legal is about the law: what the law allows, or says you must do. Breaking the law can bring fines, court or losing your job.
  2. NP9 S1 P2Ethical is about what is right and fair, based on values. Something can be legal but still unethical.
  3. NP9 S1 P3Where can you find out more about the legal and ethical side of your sector? Your policy and procedure manual; your supervisor or manager; websites such as the Department of Health, Disability and Ageing, or Work Health and Safety Queensland; and training events.
  4. NP9 S1 P4Aged care workers follow the Aged Care Code of Conduct. Disability workers follow the NDIS Code of Conduct. Both are on the government websites.

2 Eleven key words **

These are the legal and ethical issues in your assessment. Each card shows the statement from your assessment and then what it means in simple words. Click through the cards.

Listen to the explanation

Duty of Care

Employers and employees in a workplace have a responsibility to take reasonable care not to cause harm to other people or property.

  • You must take care so that nobody gets hurt because of what you do, or do not do.

Discrimination

The unfair or unjust treatment of a person based on a personal attribute, including age, race or gender.

  • Treating someone worse because of who they are. It is against the law.

Mandatory Reporting

A legislative requirement is placed on some people to report signs of suspected abuse or neglect of vulnerable people, including frail, aged and children.

  • Some workers must, by law, report signs of abuse or neglect.

Translators and Interpreters

Professional used to change written text or spoken words from one language to another.

  • A translator works with writing. An interpreter works with speaking. Use a professional, not a family member, for important talks.

Informed Choice

Deciding to agree to a service/process after you have been provided with all the relevant information and had sufficient time to form an understanding.

  • The person gets all the information and enough time, then decides.

Job Description

Outlines the tasks you are to perform, the rules you are to abide by, and the responsibilities you have in the workplace.

  • The document that says what your job is.

Conflict of Interest

Occurs when workplace decisions or actions are influenced by, or perceived to be influenced by, personal gain.

  • When your own benefit (money, gifts, friends) could affect your work decisions, or looks like it could.

Ethics

Standards by which you are to act and behave in the workplace.

  • The standards of good behaviour at work.

Privacy and Confidentiality

Protection of personal information. Not repeating conversations. Ensuring that client files are locked in office.

  • Keep people's information and stories private.

Disclosure

Sharing of personal information or key information relevant to a specific purpose.

  • Sharing information only when there is a good reason, and only with the right people.

Child protection

A legislative process for protecting children from harm, abuse or neglect.

  • The legal process that keeps children safe.

Scope of Practice

What you are qualified to do, what you are competent to do, and what you are authorised to do.

  • You only do the tasks you are trained for, able to do, and allowed to do.

3 A legal problem or an ethical problem? **

In your assessment you read six situations. For each one you tick Unethical or Legal, and give a reason. Be careful: in this question, "Legal" means a legal problem: a law is broken. "Unethical" means it is wrong, but it breaks a work rule or a value, not a law.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP9 S3 P1Joan, an older worker, does not get a job because the job "suits a younger person". → Legal. Reason: this is discrimination because of her age.
  2. NP9 S3 P2John does not like helping people shower, so he talks another worker into doing his showers. → Unethical. Reason: John is not doing his work responsibilities.
  3. NP9 S3 P3Mary Jane is upset because the doctor made a treatment plan without asking her. → Legal. Reason: Mary Jane has the right to make her own choices and to be involved in decisions about her care.
  4. NP9 S3 P4Joe tells you about terrible things from the war. You plan to tell the other staff. → Legal. Reason: this is a breach of confidentiality (privacy).
  5. NP9 S3 P5Abdul is left out of a day outing because staff think his prayer times are too hard to plan around. → Legal. Reason: this is discrimination, and Abdul has the right to be included, or at least to be asked.
  6. NP9 S3 P6Sara feels sorry for Mrs Gordon and offers to visit her on weekends. → Unethical. Reason: this is a breach of work role boundaries (professional boundaries).
Try it Unethical or Legal, and why?

For each person, choose Unethical or Legal, then choose the reason.

4 Professional boundaries: the first meeting **

When you first meet a person who uses your service, you explain the boundaries of your working relationship. Your assessment asks you to tick five points. They are below in the same words.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP9 S4 P1The function of the service: what the service does.
  2. NP9 S4 P2Your role in the service: what you do, and what you do not do.
  3. NP9 S4 P3Responsibilities of the person seeking services: what the person needs to do.
  4. NP9 S4 P4Person's rights to privacy and confidentiality: how their information is kept safe.
  5. NP9 S4 P5What the person can expect from the service.
  6. NP9 S4 P6Not for the first meeting: Right to share information with other individuals; Ask the person for their current financial status; Support worker's annual wage; Size and structure of your family; Your health conditions. (Your own private life stays private. The person's information is not shared.)

6 Writing at work: the rules **

Progress notes and incident reports are legal documents. The person (or their attorney), auditors, and sometimes a court can read them. Follow these rules.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP9 S6 P1Start every entry with the date and time. Use the 24-hour clock: 11 pm is 2300, 11 am is 1100.
  2. NP9 S6 P2Objective, not subjective: write only facts, what you saw, heard, did or measured. No opinions, no guesses, no judging.
  3. NP9 S6 P3When the person tells you something, write it as "Mrs Collins stated..." and use her words in quote marks.
  4. NP9 S6 P4Factual, Accurate, Concise, Timely (FACT): true, correct, short and to the point, and written as soon as possible.
  5. NP9 S6 P5Use black ink, no white-out, correct spelling, neat writing, and no blank lines or gaps.
  6. NP9 S6 P6At the end, write your name and role, and sign. You are responsible for what you wrote.

7 The incident report, field by field **

Choose the case study for your sector. Here is the aged care one in simple words. Mrs Anna Collins (born 13/3/1934) lives at home and gets your service three days a week. Today you arrive at 11 am. She does not come to the door, but you hear her call. You use her hidden key and find her on the bathroom floor. She says she got up at 11 pm last night, the light blew, she slipped off the toilet seat in the dark, and she has been on the floor since then. She has a "horrid pain" in the top of her right leg. You see a large bruise there, and the leg is turned outward. You make her comfortable with a pillow and blankets, reassure her, call 000, then your supervisor, then her daughter Sally (next of kin).

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP9 S7 P1Your details: your name, your role (support worker), a phone number.
  2. NP9 S7 P2Date and time of injury: yesterday's date, 2300. The fall happened last night, not when you found her.
  3. NP9 S7 P3Injured person: Mrs Anna Collins. Relationship to you: client.
  4. NP9 S7 P4Where it happened: the bathroom at her home.
  5. NP9 S7 P5How it happened: write what Mrs Collins stated: the light blew, she slipped off the toilet seat in the dark, and she was on the floor until you came.
  6. NP9 S7 P6Nature of injury: tick Bruise and Other: right leg turned outward. Mark the top of the right leg on the body picture.
  7. NP9 S7 P7Actions taken: made comfortable with a pillow and blankets, reassured, ambulance called, supervisor and daughter told.
  8. NP9 S7 P8Witnesses: nil (nobody saw the fall).
  9. NP9 S7 P9When reported and to whom: today, about 1130, to your supervisor (name and position).
  10. NP9 S7 P10Next of kin told: Sally Collins, today at about 1145. Then sign and date the report.
  11. NP9 S7 P11Disability case study (Justice Ridge): same steps. At the lifestyle club after painting class, he tripped on an easel, grabbed the table and cut the palm of his right hand on a sharp metal edge, then fell on his right side. Tick Cut. First aid officer, ambulance, mother Samantha told. You saw it, so you are the witness.

8 The progress note **

In your assessment the progress note page already has some old entries from another worker. Write your own new entry underneath, about today's incident, and sign it with your own name.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP9 S8 P1Date and time: today's date and the time you write it, in the 24-hour clock (for example 1300).
  2. NP9 S8 P2What happened, in order: the person did not answer the door at 1100; you heard her call; you used the key and found her on the bathroom floor.
  3. NP9 S8 P3What she stated, in quote marks: when and how she fell (2300 last night, the light, the toilet seat).
  4. NP9 S8 P4What you saw: a large bruise on the top of the right leg; the leg turned outward; she said she had a lot of pain there.
  5. NP9 S8 P5What you did: made her comfortable (pillow, blanket); called the ambulance; told the supervisor; phoned her daughter Sally; she went to hospital; you locked the house; you completed the incident form.
  6. NP9 S8 P6Your name, role and signature at the end. No gaps, no white-out, no opinions.
  7. NP9 S8 P7The note must match your incident report. Do not copy the old entries, and do not write about a normal shift.
