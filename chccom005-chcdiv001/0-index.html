A Glossary: the words in plain English

These are words you will see in the lessons and in your assessment. The lessons link to this list.

Accommodate
To change the way you do something so that it suits a person's needs. For example, give a quiet room for prayer.
Augmentative communication device
A tool that helps a person who cannot speak (or speak clearly) to communicate, for example a tablet that speaks words or a picture board.
Auslan
Australian Sign Language. The language of the Deaf community in Australia. It uses hands, face and body.
Bias
A feeling or idea that makes you favour one person or group over another, often without noticing. Everyone has some bias.
Cultural safety
A place where people feel safe to be who they are, and their culture is respected. The person, not the worker, decides if they feel culturally safe.
Culture
The way of life of a group of people: their beliefs, values, customs, language, food, clothes, art and behaviour. Culture is learnt, shared, and it changes over time.
Diversity
Every person is different. Diversity means seeing and respecting these differences, for example age, culture, religion, language, gender, ability and sexual orientation.
Entitlement
Something a person has a right to have or do.
Ethnicity
The group a person belongs to because of shared ancestry, history, language or traditions, for example Vietnamese, Samoan or Aboriginal.
First Nations
The first peoples of a land. In Australia: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples.
Gender
How a person sees themselves: as a man, a woman, both, or neither. Gender can be different from the sex a person was given at birth.
Generation Alpha
Children born from about 2010 onwards. They have grown up with phones, tablets and the internet.
Human needs
The things a person must have to live and be well, for example food, water, shelter, clothing, sleep, safety and belonging.
Human rights
The basic rights and freedoms every person has because they are human, for example dignity, safety, privacy and fair treatment.
Immigration
People moving to live in a new country. Australia has a long history of immigration from many countries.
Inclusion
Making sure every person is welcome, can take part and has a voice, whatever their background.
Non-binary
A person who does not see themselves as only a man or only a woman. Many non-binary people use the pronouns they and them.
Non-verbal communication
Communication without words: face, eye contact, gestures, body position, touch, use of space and tone of voice.
Race
A way people group others by physical features, such as skin colour. Treating someone unfairly because of race is against the law in Australia.
Rapport
A feeling of trust and understanding between two people.
Reflection
Looking back at what you did and asking: what went well, what could I do better next time?
Self-awareness
Knowing your own thoughts, feelings, values, strengths and weaknesses, and how they show in your words and actions.
Sexual orientation
Who a person is attracted to, for example a man, a woman, or more than one gender. Examples: heterosexual (straight), gay, lesbian, bisexual.
Social awareness
Noticing how other people feel and what they need, and respecting their point of view.
Sorry Business
The time of mourning (grief) after a death in an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander community. It can include ceremonies and travel to family and Country.
Stereotype
A fixed idea that all people in a group are the same, for example "all older people are slow". Stereotypes are often wrong and hurtful.
Universal Declaration of Human Rights
A document written by the United Nations in 1948. It lists the rights and freedoms that belong to every person in the world.
