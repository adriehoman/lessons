Today you practise with two case studies from your assessment. A case study is a short story about a real-life situation at work. You read it, find the problems, and plan how to help. We finish the day together with We are stronger together.

1 Mary's story **

Read the story in your assessment first. It says: Mary is a proud Aboriginal woman who uses the respite service where you work. Her best friend Amelia has died. Mary is bereft (very sad and lost). She has asked to go back to her Country, because she feels isolated (alone) at the service.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S1 P1Country is very important to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people. Country means the land, the family, the culture, and the spirit. Going back to Country can help a person heal.
  2. NP11 S1 P2Grief and loss: Aboriginal cultures have their own practices after a death, for example Sorry Business. The family and community come together, and the person may need to travel for a funeral.
  3. NP11 S1 P3Your teacher's video tells a longer version of Mary's story: Mary left the centre without telling anyone, went to a funeral in the country, was missing for almost a week, and took no medication. She has diabetes and emphysema, and her family brings in cakes and biscuits.
  4. NP11 S1 P4Watch the video. Then think: what went wrong for Mary, and what could the service do better?

2 Mary: the key issues **

Your assessment asks for three key issues. Be careful: an issue is a problem the service needs to fix. Do not just write the story again ("Mary is sad, Mary wants to go home").

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S2 P1Poor lines of communication between Mary, her family and the staff: Mary did not feel she could tell staff what she needed.
  2. NP11 S2 P2Staff do not understand the cultural practices around grief and loss (such as Sorry Business and the need to go back to Country).
  3. NP11 S2 P3Mary feels isolated at the service: her cultural and social needs are not being met.
  4. NP11 S2 P4The service could not contact or find Mary when she left: a safety risk.
  5. NP11 S2 P5Mary did not take her medication with her: a serious health risk.
  6. NP11 S2 P6Mary and her family do not understand her diabetes and healthy food choices.
  7. NP11 S2 P7Choose three. Write each one as a problem for the service, in your own words.

3 Supporting Mary: six needs **

Your assessment has a table with six needs. For each need, write what the respite centre can do (a support strategy) and an outside organisation that can help with that need. Click through the cards.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S3 P1Check every row: the outside organisation must help with that need. A diabetes educator helps with the physical need, not with communication.
  2. NP11 S3 P2Do not copy one row into the next row. Each need gets its own answer.

Spiritual (for example, religion)

Read Mary's care plan. Ask Mary about her beliefs and what gives her strength. Give her a safe, quiet place for her spiritual practice, and help her find local activities.

  • Who can help: churches, spiritual groups and houses, retreats, libraries, discussion groups

Cultural (Mary's Aboriginal cultural heritage)

Make Mary feel culturally safe, so she can share her needs. Staff learn about Aboriginal culture and about Mary's own people and practices. Show Aboriginal art and the Aboriginal flag. Celebrate NAIDOC Week, National Reconciliation Week and National Sorry Day.

  • Who can help: community Elders, the local Aboriginal community, groups that run cultural events, a wellbeing centre

Emotional (for example, loss and grief)

Watch for signs of grief. Listen, and give Mary time and space. Do not stereotype. Offer counselling and extra support.

  • Who can help: psychologists and counsellors, grief support groups, books on grief, people who have had a similar loss

Physical (for example, health and wellbeing)

The registered nurse meets with Mary and her family: talk about leaving without telling staff, and give her a medication pack to take with her. Mary and her daughter make a self-management plan for her diabetes with the nurse. Refer Mary to a diabetes educator.

  • Who can help: a diabetes educator or diabetes association, an Aboriginal health service, walking or aqua-aerobics groups

Social (for example, inclusion, friends and family)

Ask Mary and her family what she likes. Help her stay in touch with family and Elders, and join events she enjoys. Remember special days and food needs.

  • Who can help: the local Aboriginal community and Elders, interest groups (art, history)

Communication

Learn about Aboriginal communication protocols. Avoid shame (do not embarrass Mary in front of others). Be honest and respectful. Check that you understand each other, and use the words Mary likes to use.

  • Who can help: Elders (they know the communication protocols), an interpreter service

Match each need to an outside organisation that can help.

4 Jane and Mei Lin's story **

Jane has worked in a family support service for five years. She mostly works with people from a similar culture to her own, and people like her. This week she fills in for another worker and visits Mei Lin.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S4 P1Mei Lin and her family came from China and speak only Cantonese. One child is learning English at school. Simple English words and gestures help with basic communication.
  2. NP11 S4 P2Cooking is important in Mei Lin's culture. She is proud to cook traditional meals with herbs and spices, and the smell fills the house.
  3. NP11 S4 P3Jane is not used to the smells and feels sick. She covers her nose and mouth, wears extra perfume, and sprays her own air freshener in the kitchen. She asks Mei Lin's husband to change the cooking times.
  4. NP11 S4 P4Jane feels she cannot give good care, and thinks a Chinese support worker would be better for Mei Lin.
  5. NP11 S4 P5Watch your teacher's short video about Jane and Mei Lin.

5 Jane: self-awareness, and a lack of it **

Self-awareness means you notice your own feelings, beliefs and habits, and how they affect other people.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S5 P1Jane shows self-awareness (one example): she knows that her own feelings about the smells are affecting the care she gives.
  2. NP11 S5 P2Jane shows a lack of self-awareness (give two):
  3. NP11 S5 P3She covers her nose and mouth when she goes into the kitchen.
  4. NP11 S5 P4She brings her own air freshener and sprays it in Mei Lin's home.
  5. NP11 S5 P5She does not understand Mei Lin's Chinese culture and what food means to her.
  6. NP11 S5 P6She thinks a Chinese worker would be better, instead of learning herself.
  7. NP11 S5 P7She expects Mei Lin to change her routine (her cooking times) just for Jane.

6 How could Mei Lin and her family feel? **

Your assessment asks for at least three ways Jane's actions could affect Mei Lin and her family. Imagine someone came into your kitchen and covered their nose.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S6 P1The family could feel offended: by Jane covering her nose and mouth, and by her strong perfume.
  2. NP11 S6 P2They could feel it is rude and wrong for Jane to spray air freshener in their home.
  3. NP11 S6 P3The family could feel disrespected.
  4. NP11 S6 P4The air freshener and perfume could cause allergies or other health problems.
  5. NP11 S6 P5Mei Lin could lose trust in the whole organisation.
  6. NP11 S6 P6Mei Lin could not trust new workers in the future.
  7. NP11 S6 P7Mei Lin could feel targeted, pushed aside, and discriminated against.
  8. NP11 S6 P8The family could stop using the service, because it does not meet their needs.
  9. NP11 S6 P9The family could feel cut off from the community and left out.

7 Help with communication: what can you ask for? **

The question asks what help you could ask for. That means help from someone else, not a skill you use yourself.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S7 P1An interpreter service: a trained professional who speaks Cantonese. In Australia you can call TIS National (Translating and Interpreting Service) on 131 450.
  2. NP11 S7 P2Support from your supervisor: they can arrange the interpreter and give you information about Mei Lin's needs.
  3. NP11 S7 P3"Use simple English words and gestures" is not the answer here. That is your own technique, and it is already in the story.
  4. NP11 S7 P4Do not ask the child or a family member to interpret important information. Use a professional.

8 A better experience for both Mei Lin and Jane **

Your assessment asks for three strategies: things that could have been done so that it went well for both Mei Lin and Jane.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S8 P1Give Jane information about Mei Lin's culture and needs before the first visit.
  2. NP11 S8 P2Write Mei Lin's needs in her care plan.
  3. NP11 S8 P3The supervisor gives Jane a proper handover, and checks that Jane is OK to take over the visits.
  4. NP11 S8 P4Jane does cross-cultural awareness training.
  5. NP11 S8 P5Jane tells her supervisor about the problem straight away.
  6. NP11 S8 P6Jane and Mei Lin talk together (with an interpreter) about their cultural differences.
  7. NP11 S8 P7Jane is open to learning about new cultures.
  8. NP11 S8 P8Do not copy your answer from the last question. The last question was about what happened (impacts). This question is about how to do it better (strategies).

9 We are stronger together

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S9 P1South Africa is one of the most diverse countries in the world. About 63 million people live there (2024). They come from many cultures: Black African peoples (such as Zulu, Xhosa and Sotho), white, coloured (mixed heritage), Indian and Asian South Africans.
  2. NP11 S9 P2South Africa has 12 official languages: Afrikaans, English, isiNdebele, isiXhosa, isiZulu, Sepedi, Sesotho, Setswana, siSwati, Tshivenda, Xitsonga, and since 2023 South African Sign Language. English is the language of business and government.
  3. NP11 S9 P3The first people of South Africa were the San and Khoekhoe.
  4. NP11 S9 P4South Africa has one of the highest crime rates in the world: about 50 murders every day.
  5. NP11 S9 P5The Rugby World Cup 2023 ran for 51 days (8 September to 28 October 2023). The whole country stood behind the Springboks and their captain Siya Kolisi.
  6. NP11 S9 P6During the Rugby World Cup, a security company (ADT) reported that crime went down by 70% in the west of Johannesburg and down by 60% in the east of the city. Violent crime and burglaries also dropped in Pretoria.
  7. NP11 S9 P7Your teacher's estimate: 51 days x 50 murders a day = 2,550. If crime went down by about half, about 1,275 lives were spared.
  8. NP11 S9 P8Why? They have a common goal and stand together.
  9. NP11 S9 P9This is our class motto: We have a common goal and we stand together.
  10. NP11 S9 P10Your task: listen to the song about Siya Kolisi. Then your teacher gives you one or two numbers. Find your number below and learn your word in Auslan. When the song plays again, show your word when you hear it.
