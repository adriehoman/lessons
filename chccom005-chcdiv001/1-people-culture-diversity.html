3 What is diversity? What is culture? **

Two words you will use all through this unit.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S3 P1Diversity means that every person is unique. We see the differences between people and we respect them.
  2. NP3 S3 P2People can be different in many ways: age, gender, culture, ethnicity, language, religion, beliefs, abilities, income, sexual orientation, and interests and hobbies.
  3. NP3 S3 P3Culture is the way of life of a group of people. It is the beliefs, values, customs, behaviour, language, food and clothes that the group shares.
  4. NP3 S3 P4Everyone has a culture. We learn it from our family and community. Culture also changes over time.
  5. NP3 S3 P5People from the same culture are still individuals. Never think that everyone from one country is the same. Ask the person what matters to them.

4 Key areas of diversity **

Look around your classroom or your workplace. Here are the key areas of diversity you will see: family, culture, race and ethnicity, language, disability, religion, gender, sexual orientation, interests and hobbies, and age groups. Click a card to see the next one.

Listen to the explanation

Family

How a family works: who makes decisions, who looks after older people, and what each person is expected to do.

Culture, race and ethnicity

Where a person or their family comes from, and the traditions they follow.

Language

The language a person speaks at home, and how well they speak English.

Disability

Physical, sensory (seeing, hearing), intellectual or psychosocial disability.

Religion and spiritual beliefs

Faith, prayer, holy days, food rules, and beliefs about life and death.

Gender

Men, women, and people who are non-binary, transgender or intersex.

Sexual orientation

Who a person loves or is attracted to: straight, gay, lesbian, bisexual.

Interests and hobbies

Sport, music, art, gardening, games: what a person enjoys doing.

Age (generation)

Every generation grew up with different music, technology, rules and life events.

5 Accommodating diversity: the need, and what we do **

To accommodate diversity, you think about a person's needs and then change what you do to fit them. Always name the need and what you do about it.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S5 P1Body language: a gesture or eye contact that is polite in one culture can be rude in another. Learn what is polite for the person and watch their reaction.
  2. NP3 S5 P2Religious practices: prayer times, fasting, food rules and holy days. Give time and a quiet place, and plan meals and activities around them.
  3. NP3 S5 P3Gender roles: some people want a worker of the same gender for personal care. Ask, and respect their choice.
  4. NP3 S5 P4Language: use simple words, pictures and gestures, and book an interpreter for important talks.
  5. NP3 S5 P5Different views: people may see the world differently from you. Listen and do not judge.
  6. NP3 S5 P6Physical and mental health: change the support so it fits the person's health, energy and feelings.
  7. NP3 S5 P7Topics people do not talk about (taboos), past trauma, and the hurt caused by discrimination, exclusion (being left out) and negative stereotypes: be gentle, include everyone, and never make jokes about a group.

6 Working with people who are different from you **

Working with colleagues from different backgrounds changes you too. Here are the good things it brings.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S6 P1You learn about other people: their lives, their families and their backgrounds.
  2. NP3 S6 P2You make new friends and your social circle grows.
  3. NP3 S6 P3You try new food, music and activities.
  4. NP3 S6 P4You grow as a person: you become more open and more patient.
  5. NP3 S6 P5You may learn some words of a new language.
  6. NP3 S6 P6The team has more ideas and better ways to solve problems, because everyone sees things differently.

7 How Australia has changed **

Australia today is very different from Australia 60 years ago. Three big factors changed the country.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S7 P1Advancement in technology: phones, computers and the internet changed how we work, shop, learn and talk to family overseas.
  2. NP3 S7 P2Increased population: many more people live in Australia now, mostly in the cities. This changed housing, transport and services.
  3. NP3 S7 P3Immigration: people came from all over the world. They brought new languages, religions, food and ideas.

8 Human needs and human rights **

Every person has needs and every person has rights. Your job is to support both.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S8 P1The Universal Declaration of Human Rights was written by the United Nations in 1948. It lists the basic rights and freedoms of every person on earth, wherever they live. Read the Universal Declaration
  2. NP3 S8 P2Examples of human rights: life, freedom, safety, privacy, free speech, being treated fairly, health care and education.
  3. NP3 S8 P3A need is something we must have to live: food, water, air, shelter, clothing and sleep. We also need safety, love and belonging.
  4. NP3 S8 P4A right is an entitlement: something a person is allowed to have because of who they are. Rights are written in laws.
  5. NP3 S8 P5How they connect: rights make sure people's needs are met. For example, the right to health care helps meet the need to be well.
  6. NP3 S8 P6In aged care, the Statement of Rights (part of the Aged Care Act 2024) lists the rights of older people, for example to be treated with dignity and to make their own choices.

9 Every person is unique **

Respect means you change your support to fit the person. Learn these six people and their support. The words below are the same words you will see in your assessment.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S9 P1A person who is non-verbal (dysarthria due to CP): Staff undergo training to effectively communicate using the person's augmentative communication device. (The person cannot speak clearly, so staff learn to use their talking device.)
  2. NP3 S9 P2A 'Sister of a Holy Order' residing in a residential aged care home: A female support worker providing assistance with personal hygiene tasks, as requested by the person. (A nun asked for a woman to help her shower, so we do that.)
  3. NP3 S9 P3A person who is non-binary: Staff use the person's preferred name and title when speaking with them and about them. (See non-binary. Use the name and words the person chose.)
  4. NP3 S9 P4A person from Generation Alpha: Supported with visual learning opportunities due to being raised with technology from an early age. (Young people who grew up with tablets learn well with videos and pictures. See Generation Alpha.)
  5. NP3 S9 P5A First Nations person: Staff respect Sorry Business and facilitate access/transport to family during these times. (When someone in their community dies, staff help them be with family. See Sorry Business.)
  6. NP3 S9 P6A Croatian family that has recently moved to the region: Staff assist the family to connect to a local cultural group through the community centre. (A new family may feel alone, so staff help them meet people from their culture.)

10 Self-awareness and reflection **

To work well with people who are different from you, you first need to know yourself.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S10 P1Self-awareness means you know your own thoughts, feelings, values, beliefs, strengths and weaknesses, and how they show in your words and actions.
  2. NP3 S10 P2Social awareness means you notice how other people feel and what they need.
  3. NP3 S10 P3Reflection means you look back at what you did and ask: What went well? What can I do better?
  4. NP3 S10 P4Self-awareness helps you to improve your self and social awareness by: Identifying and reflecting on your own perspectives and biases; Being aware of yourself and your social limitations; Being more inclusive in the workplace; Understanding how your culture and attitudes impact others. (See bias.)
  5. NP3 S10 P5Why self-awareness and reflection matter when you communicate at work: people feel valued and important; you judge and assume less; you listen better; you manage your emotions; you relate better to other people; and you know your strengths and weaknesses and how to manage them.
