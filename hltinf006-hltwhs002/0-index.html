A Glossary: the words in plain English

Every word here is one you will see in the lessons and in your assessment. The lessons link to this list.

Airborne transmission
Very small particles carrying germs float in the air for a long time and travel across a room.
Antimicrobial resistance
When germs change so that the medicines made to kill them (like antibiotics) stop working. Using antibiotics only when needed slows this down.
Aseptic technique
A way of doing a procedure (like a wound dressing) so that no germs get onto the clean area or into the person.
Bacteria
Single-cell germs that can copy themselves on their own and live on surfaces for days. Example: a urinary tract infection (UTI).
Colonisation
Germs live on or in the body but cause no illness. Many people carry germs on their skin and never get sick from them.
Contact transmission
A germ moves by touch. Direct contact is skin to skin. Indirect contact goes through something in between, like a bed rail or your hands.
Disease
An illness with signs and symptoms that can be seen, measured or felt. Some diseases are caused by infections; some are not.
Droplet transmission
Big wet drops from a cough, sneeze or talking carry germs about one metre before they fall.
Fungi
Small living things like yeasts and moulds. They like dark, damp places. Examples: thrush, tinea, ringworm.
Hazard
Anything that could cause harm to a person, property or the environment.
Host
The person (or animal) that a germ lives in or on.
Host cell
One of your own cells that a virus takes over to make copies of itself.
Immune system
The body's defence system that finds and fights germs.
Infection
Germs get inside the body and the immune system fights back. The person may or may not feel sick.
Infectious agent
Another name for a germ that can cause infection. Also called a pathogen.
Microorganism
A living thing too small to see without a microscope. Germs are microorganisms.
Mode of transmission
How a germ travels from one place or person to the next. It "hitches a ride".
Mucous membrane
The wet lining inside the eyes, nose and mouth (and other openings). Germs get in easily here.
NSQHS Standards
The National Safety and Quality Health Service Standards. Australia's national rules for safe, good quality health care. Standard 3 is about infection.
Parasites
Living things that live on or in a host and feed from it. Examples: tapeworm, scabies mites, the malaria parasite.
Pathogen
A germ that causes disease.
Penetrating injury
An injury where something sharp goes through the skin, for example a needlestick.
Portal of entry
The way a germ gets into a person: mouth, nose, eyes, a cut, a needle prick.
Portal of exit
The way a germ leaves its source: a cough, blood, faeces, urine, wound fluid.
PPE
Personal protective equipment: gloves, gowns and aprons, masks, eye protection.
Reservoir
A place where germs live and multiply: people, animals, soil, water, food, waste, blood and body fluids.
Respiratory etiquette
Good manners for coughing and sneezing: cover with a tissue or elbow, bin the tissue, turn away, clean your hands.
Risk
How likely it is that a hazard will cause harm, and how bad that harm could be.
Sharps
Anything that can cut or prick the skin: needles, lancets, razors, broken glass.
Source of infection
The person or thing carrying the germ. Also called the reservoir.
Standard precautions
The basic safety steps used with every person, every time: hand hygiene, PPE, safe sharps, cleaning, linen and waste handling, cough etiquette.
Susceptible host
A person who can easily catch an infection, for example an older person or someone with a long-term illness.
Vector-borne transmission
A germ is carried by an insect or animal (the vector), for example malaria carried by a mosquito.
Virus
The smallest germ. It cannot copy itself; it needs a host cell. Examples: the flu, COVID-19, HIV.
