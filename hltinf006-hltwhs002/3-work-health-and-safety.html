Gerry the Germ is not the only thing that can hurt you at work. A loose rug, a heavy transfer, a hot heater, a long week with no rest. This lesson is about the law that protects you, the hazards you will meet, and what you do about them.

1 Three layers of WHS law **

Work health and safety (WHS) law in Australia comes in three layers. Each layer has its own job.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S1 P1Model WHS Act: one fair and consistent set of rules that protects the health and safety of workers and workplaces right across Australia. (In Queensland it is the Work Health and Safety Act 2011.)
  2. NP11 S1 P2Model WHS Regulations: the detailed rules that sit under the Act. They say what must be done to follow the law, including licences for some activities and keeping records.
  3. NP11 S1 P3Model Codes of Practice: practical guides that show how to meet the standards in the Act and the Regulations. For example, the Hazardous Manual Tasks Code of Practice.
  4. NP11 S1 P4Think of it as: the Act says you must be safe, the Regulations say what that means, and the Codes say here is how.

2 Duty of care: what the worker and the employer must do **

The WHS Act gives duties to both sides. The employer (the law calls them the person conducting a business or undertaking, or PCBU) and the worker, which is you.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S2 P1Worker duty 1: take reasonable care of your own health and safety.
  2. NP11 S2 P2Worker duty 2: take reasonable care that what you do, or do not do, does not harm others.
  3. NP11 S2 P3Worker duty 3: follow any reasonable instruction about health and safety.
  4. NP11 S2 P4Worker duty 4: cooperate with the workplace WHS policies and procedures.
  5. NP11 S2 P5Employer duty 1: provide a safe work environment without risks to health and safety.
  6. NP11 S2 P6Employer duty 2: provide safe equipment, buildings and work areas, and safe systems of work (procedures).
  7. NP11 S2 P7Employer duty 3: make sure equipment, materials and substances are used, handled and stored safely.
  8. NP11 S2 P8Employer duty 4: provide facilities for workers (toilets, drinking water, rest areas).
  9. NP11 S2 P9Employer duty 5: give information, training, instruction and supervision.
  10. NP11 S2 P10Employer duty 6: monitor workers' health and workplace conditions to prevent injury and illness.
Watch out The assessment asks for duties from the Act. "Wash your hands" or "report hazards" are good habits, but they are not the duties of care. Use the words from this slide: reasonable care, follow instructions, safe workplace, training and supervision.

3 Policy or procedure? **

Every workplace has safety policies and safe work procedures. They are not the same thing.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S3 P1A policy tells people what the rules are and what is expected of them. Policy = what to do.
  2. NP11 S3 P2Why have a safety policy? It shows the employer is committed to safety and to preventing work-related injury and illness, and it sets the rules and standards everyone must follow.
  3. NP11 S3 P3A procedure explains how to do a task safely and correctly, step by step. Procedure = how to do it.
  4. NP11 S3 P4Why have safe work procedures? So every worker knows the safest way to do the job. A good procedure explains the task step by step, points out the risks, and says how to control them. It also supports training and supervision.

4 Hazards, risks and how to control them **

A hazard and a risk are not the same. Get these two words right and the rest of WHS makes sense.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S4 P1A hazard is anything that could cause harm: injury or illness to a person, or damage to property or the environment.
  2. NP11 S4 P2A risk is the chance that the hazard actually hurts someone, and how bad it would be.
  3. NP11 S4 P3The five hazard categories: Physical (spills, trip hazards, noise, manual handling), Ergonomic (poor lighting, badly set up workstation), Chemical (cleaning products, fumes), Biological (blood and body fluids, germs, animal droppings), Psychological (stress, bullying, violence, fatigue).
  4. NP11 S4 P4Common hazards in care work: lifting, supporting and moving people; moving equipment such as wheelchairs and trolleys; slips, trips and falls; work-related stress; occupational violence; bullying and harassment.
  5. NP11 S4 P5Once you know the hazard, you control it. The hierarchy of control ranks the options from best to last resort: eliminate, substitute, isolate, engineering, administrative, PPE.

5 Reporting: who to tell, why quickly, and what to write **

Finding a hazard is only half the job. Reporting it is the other half, and the paperwork protects you.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S5 P1Who to report a hazard, an incident or an unsafe practice to: your supervisor or team leader, the manager, the health and safety representative (HSR), the responsible authority in your organisation, and where it is right to do so, the person, their family or carer. Your colleagues and the person's GP may also need to know about an incident.
  2. NP11 S5 P2Why report quickly? Other people become aware of the risk. The hazard gets fixed sooner. You meet your duty of care and your legal obligations. And the chance of injury goes down.
  3. NP11 S5 P3Hazard form: when you find a hazard (for example a home with no smoke alarms), fill in a hazard form (paper or digital, whatever your workplace uses) as soon as possible. Written evidence means it can be investigated and fixed. Tell your line manager, supervisor or team leader straight away, and where appropriate the person and their family or carer.
  4. NP11 S5 P4Incident report: when someone is injured (for example a colleague hurts their back lowering a falling resident), complete an incident report, electronic or paper, as per workplace policy, as soon as possible after the incident.

6 Manual handling: hazardous manual tasks **

Moving people and things is the biggest cause of injury in care work. The Code of Practice has an exact definition, and the assessment wants it.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S6 P1A hazardous manual task is a task where a person must lift, lower, push, pull, carry or otherwise move, hold or restrain any person, animal or thing, and it involves one or more of: repetitive or sustained force, high or sudden force, repetitive movement, sustained or awkward posture, or exposure to vibration. (Hazardous Manual Tasks Code of Practice 2021, WorkSafe Queensland.)
  2. NP11 S6 P2Strategies to reduce manual handling hazards: use good manual handling technique; assess the weight and get help when a load would be risky; a no-lift approach; read the person's care plan for their mobility and transfer needs; use the right equipment and aids (hoist, slide sheet, walker); avoid twisting and keep your back straight with knees bent; regular manual handling training; report broken or missing equipment; exercise programs.
  3. NP11 S6 P3How muscles move you: muscle cells can contract, which means shorten or pull. When a muscle contracts it pulls on the bone it is attached to, and that is what makes the body move. A muscle can also contract by holding steady against a load without getting shorter, like holding a resident's arm still. Contraction means pulling the ends together, whether or not the muscle gets shorter.
  4. NP11 S6 P4Changes in a person's home to reduce injury: remove rugs that could trip someone; use mechanical aids such as a hoist, a wheelie walker or a commode chair; use a trolley or smaller boxes when carrying things in and out; change the layout with ramps, railings, handles and safer flooring edges.
Watch out For "what is a hazardous manual task", a one-line definition is not enough. Name the actions (lift, lower, push, pull, carry, hold, restrain) and the five risk factors (force, sudden force, repetitive movement, awkward posture, vibration).

7 Safety signs: read the picture **

Signs talk without words. The shape and colour tell you the type of message. The picture tells you the detail.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S7 P1Blue circle = you must do this: gloves, mask, eye protection, hand hygiene, cough etiquette.
  2. NP11 S7 P2Yellow triangle = warning, a danger is here: clinical waste, sharps, poison, radiation, cytotoxic waste.
  3. NP11 S7 P3Red = fire equipment (extinguisher, hose) or do not.
  4. NP11 S7 P4Green = safe: first aid, emergency exit.
  5. NP11 S7 P5Clinical waste is any waste with blood or body fluids. Clinical sharps waste means needles and blades: use and dispose of sharps safely. Cytotoxic waste comes from chemotherapy drugs and is handled with extra care.

8 Working in a person's home **

In community care the person's home is your workplace. Safety there is shared, and you are often working alone.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S8 P1The person receiving support has WHS responsibilities too: keep themselves safe (avoid actions that could injure them); keep others safe; follow safety instructions; help keep the home safe (put pets away, tell you about hazards like a wet floor); respect support workers; take responsibility for their choices; communicate clearly so care can be planned; allow access to the home.
  2. NP11 S8 P2You, the support worker: work safely at all times; keep others safe; follow WHS procedures and the care plan, and only do the tasks agreed in the service agreement; use PPE and equipment correctly; be properly trained; report hazards and incidents to your supervisor.
  3. NP11 S8 P3Other risks to your personal safety at work, in residential or community settings: catching an infection; injury during manual handling; stress from the nature of the work (shift work, long hours, palliative care, changed behaviours); verbal or physical aggression from a person or their family; car accidents travelling between visits; robbery when working in the community; being bitten or scratched by pets; poisoning or exposure to harmful substances.
  4. NP11 S8 P4Getting urgent help: in a residential setting use the call bell or emergency call system. In the community call 000.

9 Fire safety and smoke alarms **

House fires kill, and the people you support are the most at risk. Queensland has strict smoke alarm laws, and you need to know them.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S9 P1Queensland smoke alarm law, from 1 January 2017: alarms older than 10 years must be replaced with photoelectric alarms that meet Australian Standard AS 3786-2014 (the date is stamped on the back); an alarm that fails a test must be replaced immediately; a faulty hardwired alarm must be replaced with a hardwired photoelectric alarm.
  2. NP11 S9 P2From 1 January 2027: all existing homes, townhouses and units need photoelectric, interconnected alarms (if one sounds, they all sound), either hardwired (240 V) or with a non-removable 10-year battery.
  3. NP11 S9 P3Where: on every level; in every bedroom; in the hallway that connects bedrooms to the rest of the home (or, if no hallway, between the bedroom and the living areas); and if a level has no bedrooms, in the most likely path to the exit.
  4. NP11 S9 P4Maintenance: test once a month by pressing the test button (use a broom handle if you cannot reach); keep it clean by vacuuming around it, because dust stops it working; replace removable batteries once a year; never paint a smoke alarm.
  5. NP11 S9 P5High-risk groups: people aged 65 and over; people with disability; young children (curious about fire).
  6. NP11 S9 P6Risky behaviours: careless cigarettes, lighters and matches; unsafe candles and oil burners; overloaded power points and faulty electrical equipment; not following fire safety procedures; storing chemicals unsafely; doing electrical or repair work that needs a qualified tradesperson; leaving matches and lighters where children can reach them.
  7. NP11 S9 P7Help a person prevent fires at home: never leave cooking, candles or open flames unattended; never smoke in bed; keep flammable items away from the cooktop and heaters; put a screen in front of open fires; keep heaters away from curtains, tablecloths and bedding; watch loose clothing near heaters and stoves; keep heaters and cords out of walkways; clean the dryer filter every use; do not overload power points and switch off when not in use; have gas appliances checked.
  8. NP11 S9 P8R.A.C.E. when there is a fire: Remove people from immediate danger if it is safe; Alert by raising the alarm (break-glass alarm or call 000); Contain the fire by closing doors and windows; Extinguish only if you are trained and it is safe, otherwise Evacuate to the assembly area when directed and account for everyone in your care.

10 Safety is everybody's job **

You do not need a title to make the workplace safer. Here is how an ordinary worker contributes, and stays up to date.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S10 P1Report hazards, near misses and injuries.
  2. NP11 S10 P2Take part in workplace safety checks and inspections.
  3. NP11 S10 P3Report faulty tools, equipment or machinery.
  4. NP11 S10 P4Talk about safety regularly with managers and colleagues.
  5. NP11 S10 P5Keep your training and competency up to date.
  6. NP11 S10 P6Stay informed with current hazard information.
  7. NP11 S10 P7Join in regular review and consultation about safety.
  8. NP11 S10 P8Follow safe work procedures that describe the task, name the hazards, and say how to do the job to reduce the risk.
  9. NP11 S10 P9Attend training, use equipment properly, and follow safety policies and procedures.

11 Look after yourself: stress, fatigue and burnout **

Care work is rewarding and it is tiring. Burnout is a real WHS issue. The best workers are the ones who look after themselves.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S11 P1Strategies to reduce your risk of burnout: get plenty of rest; exercise regularly; eat regular, balanced meals; cut down on alcohol, caffeine, nicotine and other drugs; make time to relax; plan at least one enjoyable activity each day; spend time with people you care about; seek peer support at work; know your limits and keep strong boundaries at work; get professional support when you need it.
  2. NP11 S11 P2Support people and services: your supervisor, team leader or manager; your GP, who can give counselling and a Mental Health Care Plan to see a psychologist; online and phone services with a trained professional 24 hours a day.
  3. NP11 S11 P3Phone: 13 HEALTH (13 43 25 84); Lifeline 13 11 14; MensLine Australia 1300 789 978; Salvation Army 1300 36 36 22.
  4. NP11 S11 P4Online: Beyond Blue; Dear Mind (Queensland Health); Anxiety Treatment Australia; the Black Dog Institute Mental Health Workplace Toolkit.
