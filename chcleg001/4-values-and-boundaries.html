Some of the hardest parts of this job are not about laws. They are about values and boundaries. In this lesson you learn how to keep your own values out of the way, how to say a kind "no", and how to spot when something is unethical or illegal.

1 Your values, their values **

Values are the things you believe are important. Everyone has different values, and that is okay. At work, you do not push (impose) your values onto the people you support.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP9 S1 P1Your values may be different from the person's values, and pushing yours can cause conflict.
  2. NP9 S1 P2It is disrespectful. You accept the person's values, even when they are different or you do not agree.
  3. NP9 S1 P3People you support can be vulnerable. They may be influenced to take on your values instead of their own.
  4. NP9 S1 P4They may agree with you just to please you, so they lose their own choice.
  5. NP9 S1 P5It goes against person-centred support: the person is the expert on their own life.
Watch out Explain why in your own words. "It is not good" or "it is wrong" is not enough. Say what could happen: conflict, disrespect, influence, people-pleasing.

2 When values clash **

Your assessment example: you support a person who likes lunch, a beer and $20 on the pokies at the hotel every week. You are strongly against drinking and gambling. What do you do?

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP9 S2 P1Notice your own values and keep them out of your work. The outing is the person's choice and their right (dignity of risk).
  2. NP9 S2 P2Try to put your values aside and support the person in their best interests: their goals, their plan, their choice.
  3. NP9 S2 P3Talk to your supervisor about how you feel. This is professional, not weak.
  4. NP9 S2 P4If your values are so strong that you cannot give good support, your supervisor may change the worker for this person.
  5. NP9 S2 P5Do not lecture the person, refuse to go, or try to change their mind.
Watch out Your answer must be about your values (putting them aside), and it should reach your supervisor. Do not only write about dignity of risk.

3 Ethical dilemmas: a simple way to decide

An ethical dilemma is a problem where there is no easy right answer, and your values may pull you one way. Use these steps.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP9 S3 P1Step 1: Name the problem. What is the legal or ethical issue? Who could be hurt?
  2. NP9 S3 P2Step 2: Get the facts and write them down. What did you see and hear? Write it carefully and accurately.
  3. NP9 S3 P3Step 3: Get advice. Talk to your supervisor. If someone is in danger right now, call emergency services (000).
  4. NP9 S3 P4Step 4: Act and keep records. Follow the policy, keep a copy of your notes, and keep privacy: only share with people who need to know.

4 Professional boundaries **

Professional boundaries are the clear limits that keep your relationship with the person safe, respectful and professional, not personal.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP9 S4 P1You are a support worker, not a friend, partner or family member. The relationship is there for the person's needs, not yours.
  2. NP9 S4 P2A service user asks you on a date: say no, kindly and clearly: "Thank you, but I am your support worker, so I cannot go on a date with you." Explain it is because of professional boundaries, and tell your supervisor if needed.
  3. NP9 S4 P3Sara and Janet: Sara feels sorry for Janet, who lives alone, and offers to visit her on weekends. Kind, but it crosses the boundary: seeing a person outside work hours is against workplace policy. It is unethical (not illegal).
  4. NP9 S4 P4Other boundary breaks: giving your personal phone number, adding the person on social media, lending or borrowing money, sharing your personal problems.
  5. NP9 S4 P5If you are worried a person is lonely, tell your supervisor. The service can find proper supports, for example a community visitor or a social group.
Watch out For Sara, tick Unethical and explain the boundary: seeing a person outside work hours, as a friend, is against workplace policy.

5 Gifts

People sometimes want to give you a gift to say thank you. It feels nice, but it can cause problems.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP9 S5 P1Most organisations have a gift policy. Many say no gifts, or only a very small gift (like a card or chocolates) that is reported.
  2. NP9 S5 P2Money, jewellery, scratchies, or being put in a will, are not okay. They can be seen as financial abuse, even when the person really wants to give.
  3. NP9 S5 P3Say thank you kindly: "That is so kind. I am not allowed to accept gifts, but your thank you means a lot."
  4. NP9 S5 P4Always check the policy and tell your supervisor.

6 Conflict of interest **

A conflict of interest is when you, or someone close to you, could gain something personally from a decision at work, or when personal ties could affect your decisions.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP9 S6 P1A supervisor dates a support worker who reports to him.
  2. NP9 S6 P2A close family member is on the interview panel for a job you applied for.
  3. NP9 S6 P3A supervisor gives the cleaning contract to a company owned by a close friend.
  4. NP9 S6 P4An employee starts their own business that offers the same services as the organisation they work for full time.
  5. NP9 S6 P5These are not conflicts of interest: sharing a workspace with someone you do not like, or when your values and the person's values clash.
  6. NP9 S6 P6What you do: tell your supervisor about the situation. Do not keep it secret, and do not write it in the person's support plan.
  7. NP9 S6 P7A worker brings their eight-year-old child to work: this is a conflict of interest too. Is the worker focused on the person or on their child? For the person: they may feel exposed, lose privacy, and stop trusting the worker. There are also insurance problems if something happens.
Watch out In the quiz, "values that clash" is not a conflict of interest. That is a values conflict (Section 2).

7 Fit and safe for work **

The code of conduct expects every worker to come to work ready, safe and doing their own job. Here are three stories from your assessment.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP9 S7 P1John does not like helping people shower, so he talks a co-worker into doing his tasks. This is unethical: it is unprofessional to get others to do your work, and it breaks workplace policies and his job description.
  2. NP9 S7 P2Katie smells of alcohol at work and her mood changes after a break. She breaches the code of conduct and puts people at risk. She could face disciplinary action and lose her job. The right step for you: tell your supervisor.
  3. NP9 S7 P3Phillip and Jenny put Wilma into bed without the hoist the plan says to use. They lifted her by hand: unsafe manual handling. They broke policy, the support plan and the WHS code of practice. If anyone is hurt, WorkCover may not support them, they may not be supported in an investigation, and they could lose their jobs.
Watch out For Phillip and Jenny, name the unsafe lift: they moved Wilma without the hoist. "Did not follow procedures" on its own is not enough.

8 Unethical or legal concern? **

Ask yourself: does it break a law? Then it is a legal concern. Does it break workplace rules, the code of conduct or professional values, but no law? Then it is unethical.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP9 S8 P1Joan (too old for the job): legal concern. Age discrimination is against the Anti-Discrimination Act.
  2. NP9 S8 P2John (gets others to do his showers): unethical. It is unprofessional and breaks workplace policies.
  3. NP9 S8 P3Sara (visits Janet on weekends): unethical. It breaks professional boundaries (seeing a client outside work hours).
  4. NP9 S8 P4Abdul (left out because of prayer times): legal concern. Discrimination because of religion is against the Anti-Discrimination Act.
  5. NP9 S8 P5Tick one box only, then write a reason that fits that person's story. Do not copy one reason into every row.

9 Who gets hurt? Consequences **

When rules are broken, someone pays the price. Your assessment asks about three different groups. Read the question: who is it asking about?

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP9 S9 P1The organisation (Q17): legal action, fines, losing accreditation, sanctions from the Commission, damage to its reputation in the community. Example: if an organisation does not put new standards in place on time, the Commission does a risk assessment and can give sanctions, put in an administrator, or close the service.
  2. NP9 S9 P2The staff member (Q18): a warning, disciplinary action, losing their job, no WorkCover support if they broke the rules, not being supported in an investigation, or court.
  3. NP9 S9 P3The person using the service (Q19): harm or injury, loss of privacy and dignity, feeling unsafe or afraid of payback, losing trust in staff and the service, and not wanting to use the service any more.
  4. NP9 S9 P4Suzanne's complaint: the manager told the staff member that Suzanne complained. Her rights and privacy were not respected, she may fear payback from the worker, their relationship is damaged, and she may lose trust in the service or stop using it.
Watch out Match your answer to the group in the question. Q19 asks about the person, so do not write about the worker losing their job.
