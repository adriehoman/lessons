A Glossary: the words in plain English

These are words you will see in the lessons and in your assessment. The lessons link to this list.

Accountable
Having to explain and take responsibility for what you do.
Advocate
A person who supports someone to speak up for their rights, for example a worker from an advocacy service like OPAN.
Ageing in place
Helping an older person keep living in their own home and community for as long as possible.
Code of conduct
A document that sets out how all staff are expected to behave at work. The Aged Care and NDIS Codes of Conduct are backed by law.
Code of practice
A practical guide that explains how to follow the work health and safety laws, for example how to do manual tasks safely.
Common law
Law made by judges when they decide court cases. A person can take someone to court under common law, for example if they did not take enough care.
Conflict of interest
When a worker could gain something personally from a decision at work, or when personal ties could affect their decisions.
Continuing professional education
Learning that keeps your knowledge and skills up to date so you can do your job better. Also called professional development.
Dignity of risk
A person's right to take reasonable risks and make their own choices, whatever their age or ability.
Disclosure
Sharing personal or key information for a clear reason, with the right person, for example telling your supervisor about a risk.
Discrimination
Treating a person less fairly because of a personal attribute, for example their age, race, disability, sex or religion.
Duty of care
Your legal and moral duty to keep the people you support, and others, safe from harm.
Elder abuse
When an older person is harmed by someone they should be able to trust. It can be financial, physical, psychological, sexual or neglect.
Ethics
Ideas about what is right and fair. Ethics are guided by values. There is often more than one good way to act.
Human rights
Things every person is entitled to because they are human, for example dignity, safety, privacy and fair treatment.
Industrial award
A legal document that sets the minimum pay and conditions for a type of job, for example the Social, Community, Home Care and Disability Services Award.
Informed choice
A decision a person makes after they get all the information they need and enough time to understand it.
Informed consent
The person gets clear information they understand, knows the risks, and then agrees.
Legislation
A law made by Parliament. Also called an Act or statute law. (Qld) means a Queensland law. (Cth) means a Commonwealth law for all of Australia.
Mandatory reporting
Something the law says you MUST report, for example suspected abuse or neglect of a vulnerable person.
Neglect
Not giving a person the care, help or things they need, for example food, medicine or a way to call for help.
Policy
A written rule from the organisation that says WHAT must happen and WHY.
Privacy and confidentiality
Keeping personal information safe: not sharing it without consent, not repeating private talk, and locking files away.
Procedure
The step-by-step instructions that say HOW to follow a policy.
Professional boundaries
The clear limits that keep the relationship between a worker and a person safe, respectful and professional, not personal.
Regulatory framework
A set of rules, checks and bodies that make sure services follow the law.
Reportable incident
A serious incident that an NDIS provider must report to the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission.
Restrictive practice
Anything that limits a person's rights or freedom to move, for example taking away a walking frame or a call bell, locking a door or using medicine to control behaviour.
Scope of practice
The tasks you are trained and allowed to do in your job. Tasks outside it belong to someone else.
SIRS
Serious Incident Response Scheme: the system aged care providers use to report serious incidents to the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission.
Standards
A clear description of what good, safe service looks like. Services are checked (audited) against them.
Statement of Rights
The list of 13 rights of older people in the Aged Care Act 2024. It replaced the Charter of Aged Care Rights on 1 November 2025.
Values
The things a person believes are important, for example family, religion, honesty or independence.
Whistleblower
A person who reports something wrong or illegal at work. The law protects them from being punished for it.
Work health and safety
The duty to keep the workplace safe and to protect workers and others from hazards and risks. Often written WHS.
