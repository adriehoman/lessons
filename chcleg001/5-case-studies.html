1 How to answer a case study **

Watch the video as if you just walked into the room. You are a third person who sees what happens. Then answer the five questions.

Listen to the explanation

Question 1: Name it and describe it

  • What is the legal and/or ethical issue? Name it (for example: a boundary breach, outside scope of practice, neglect, possible abuse).
  • Then describe in detail what happened and why it is a problem: who could be hurt, and how.

Question 2: Two laws and two workplace guidelines

  • At least two pieces of legislation (full name, year, Qld or Cth). See Lesson 1, Section 3.
  • At least two organisational guidelines, for example policies and procedures, the code of conduct, the job description, the care plan, the standards.
  • Choose ones that fit this case study. Do not paste the same list into all three.

Question 3: What would you do?

  • Answer as a support worker, following policy and procedure.
  • Say what you do first (keep the person safe, stay inside your role), then who you tell (your supervisor) and what you write down.

Question 4: Two ways to help staff learn

  • Give two strategies you could suggest to your manager, so all staff know what to do next time.
  • Make them fit this situation (for example, training about the exact problem in the story).

Question 5: A professional email to your manager

  • Write a real email, not a list.
  • Include the date, the facts, what you did, and your name and role. See Section 2.
Watch out Each case study needs its own answers. Many papers lose marks because the same answer is pasted into all three.

2 How to write a professional email **

A work email about an incident is a legal document. Keep it short, factual and professional.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S2 P1To: your manager or supervisor. Subject: a short title, for example "Concern about a client visit, 12 March".
  2. NP11 S2 P2Greeting: "Dear [name],".
  3. NP11 S2 P3When and where: the real date (not "today"), the time and the place.
  4. NP11 S2 P4What happened: only the facts: what you saw and heard. Use the person's words in quotes if you can. No opinions, no blame, no gossip.
  5. NP11 S2 P5What you did: the actions you took to keep the person safe.
  6. NP11 S2 P6Who you told: for example "I phoned you at 10:15 am".
  7. NP11 S2 P7Sign-off: your full name, your role and the date.
  8. NP11 S2 P8Keep it private: send it only to the people who need to know, and do not copy in other staff.
Watch out The email must say what the problem was. An email that excuses the worker ("he was in pain, so I helped") does not report the issue.

3 Annette Curtain (Case Study 1) **

Miss Annette Curtain is 72. She is a widow and lives alone in her own home. She gets community aged care.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S3 P1Annette has very few friends. She feels lonely and loves it when the workers come and chat. Workers say it can be hard to leave her house.
  2. NP11 S3 P2One day you finish her ironing. She asks you to help her put an app on her phone.
  3. NP11 S3 P3While you help, a text message pops up on her phone. It is from another support worker: she will pick Annette up at 4 pm for an early dinner before a show tonight.
  4. NP11 S3 P4You know that this worker is not rostered on to work today.
  5. NP11 S3 P5Things to look for (no answers here): Whose phone number is on Annette's phone? Is this a work visit? What could go wrong for Annette, and for the worker? Which lessons could help you: think about boundaries (Lesson 4) and safety and insurance (Lesson 2).

4 Harry Hunter (Case Study 4) **

Mr Harry Hunter is 77. He lives at home with his wife Esme, who is his main carer. Harry gets palliative care.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S4 P1Harry stopped all his medicines except pain relief. He takes his own morphine (a liquid).
  2. NP11 S4 P2You visit once a week for 3 hours to give Esme a break (respite). You help Harry with personal hygiene and an activity he likes: sorting his stamp collection.
  3. NP11 S4 P3Today Harry is in a lot of pain. He does not want a shower. He asks you to get his morphine from the bathroom and pour him an extra 5 ml, more than his doctor prescribed.
  4. NP11 S4 P4In the story, the worker talks about calling the GP, agrees to skip the shower, then gets the morphine, pours 5 ml and leaves it on the table for Harry.
  5. NP11 S4 P5Things to look for (no answers here): Is giving or pouring morphine part of a support worker's job? Who decides how much medicine Harry has? Who should assess his pain? Which law controls strong medicines (Lesson 1, Section 3)?

5 Michael Shaw (Case Study 5) **

Mr Michael Shaw lives in an aged care home (residential aged care). He has moderate dementia.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S5 P1Michael just came back from hospital after an operation on his shoulder.
  2. NP11 S5 P2At night, once he is in bed, he often rings his call bell. When staff come, they find no reason for the call. The night staff get frustrated.
  3. NP11 S5 P3One night, when he rings again and again, two staff members go in. One distracts Michael. The other takes the call bell and puts it out of his reach, on his injured side.
  4. NP11 S5 P4Then they talk him into thinking he is tired and tell him it is time to sleep. Michael is calm and does what they say.
  5. NP11 S5 P5Things to look for (no answers here): Can Michael call for help now? Why might he be ringing the bell? What did you learn about restrictive practices (Lesson 3, Section 10) and about SIRS (Lesson 3, Section 8)? What would you do first?

6 Ideas to make work practice better **

Question 4 asks for two strategies to discuss with your manager. Here are types of strategies. Choose ones that fit each story, and say what about.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S6 P1Training or an information session about the exact problem (for example: medicines and scope of practice, professional boundaries, call bells and restrictive practices).
  2. NP11 S6 P2Read and talk about a policy at a staff meeting, or update the policy if it is not clear.
  3. NP11 S6 P3Written reminders: a memo, an email, a poster in the staff room, a note in the handover book.
  4. NP11 S6 P4Professional conversations: a mentor or buddy, or a team discussion about "what would we do?".
  5. NP11 S6 P5Check-ins: the supervisor checks that staff understand and follow the procedure.
Watch out "More training" on its own is too general. Say what the training is about and who it is for.
