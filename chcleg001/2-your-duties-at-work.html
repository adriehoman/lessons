1 Duty of care **

Duty of care is your legal and moral duty to keep the people you support, and others, safe from harm.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP5 S1 P1Duty of care means you take reasonable steps to stop harm you can see coming (foreseeable harm). For example: you wipe up a wet floor before someone slips.
  2. NP5 S1 P2Duty of care is about what you do and what you do not do. Walking past a hazard can be a breach.
  3. NP5 S1 P3Ways you meet your duty of care: turn up for work on time.
  4. NP5 S1 P4Find and report hazards.
  5. NP5 S1 P5Report and record any problems with the person, the environment or the service.
  6. NP5 S1 P6Keep the place safe and healthy.
  7. NP5 S1 P7Follow the individual plan (care plan, support plan or case plan).
  8. NP5 S1 P8Follow the reasonable directions of your supervisor and health professionals.
  9. NP5 S1 P9Work within policies and procedures.
  10. NP5 S1 P10Go to training and keep your skills up to date.
  11. NP5 S1 P11Work within your scope of practice: only do what you are trained and allowed to do.

2 Dignity of risk

Dignity of risk is a person's right to take reasonable risks, whatever their age or ability.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP5 S2 P1Taking some risks is part of a full life: walking to the shops, cooking a meal, going on a holiday, having a beer with friends.
  2. NP5 S2 P2Duty of care does not mean you make decisions for the person. It does not take away their human rights or their dignity of risk.
  3. NP5 S2 P3Your job is to help the person take the risk as safely as possible: talk about the risks, follow the plan, and report what happens.
  4. NP5 S2 P4Dignity of risk is not making the person do risky things. It is also not "only after a risk assessment". It is their right to choose.
  5. NP5 S2 P5If a choice could cause serious harm, talk to your supervisor. The team may do a risk assessment and update the plan together with the person.

3 Work health and safety: your responsibilities **

The Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (Qld) gives duties to your employer and to you. Here are your responsibilities as a worker.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP5 S3 P1Follow instructions, policies and procedures for health and safety.
  2. NP5 S3 P2Follow the code of conduct and standards, so people are emotionally safe too.
  3. NP5 S3 P3Use the PPE and equipment that is there for the task (gloves, hoists, slide sheets).
  4. NP5 S3 P4Do not put other people at risk.
  5. NP5 S3 P5Do not hurt yourself on purpose (wilfully).
  6. NP5 S3 P6Use safe manual handling: never lift a person on your own.
  7. NP5 S3 P7Find hazards and think about the risk (how likely and how bad), then report them.
  8. NP5 S3 P8Follow infection control: hand hygiene, cleaning, PPE.

4 When safety is ignored: what can happen **

If you do not follow the safety rules, the results can be serious, for people and for you.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP5 S4 P1Injury: to you, to the person you support, to a co-worker or to a visitor.
  2. NP5 S4 P2An official warning from your employer.
  3. NP5 S4 P3Losing your job.
  4. NP5 S4 P4Legal action: a court case, a fine, or being found personally responsible.
  5. NP5 S4 P5If you get hurt because you broke the safety rules, WorkCover and your employer may not support you.
Watch out When a question asks for two consequences, write two different ones, for example "the resident could be injured" and "I could lose my job".

5 Your rights at work

Your minimum pay and conditions come from awards, registered agreements and laws (such as the Fair Work Act and the National Employment Standards).

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP5 S5 P1Your industrial award (for example the Social, Community, Home Care and Disability Services Award) sets your minimum pay and conditions.
  2. NP5 S5 P2In Queensland, casual community services workers can also build up long service leave, through a portable long service leave scheme.
  3. NP5 S5 P3You can take compassionate leave when someone in your immediate family or household (including a step-parent) has a life-threatening illness or injury, or dies.
  4. NP5 S5 P4If your employer wants to change your regular roster, they must talk with you first (consult).
  5. NP5 S5 P5If your employer says you must go to training, you are paid for that time.
  6. NP5 S5 P6Workers' compensation covers all workers who are hurt at work, also casual and part-time workers.
  7. NP5 S5 P7You are free to join a union. Your employer cannot stop you or treat you badly because of it.

6 Keep learning: professional development **

Continuing professional education (also called professional development) keeps your knowledge and skills up to date.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP5 S6 P1Why it matters: so you give quality care to the people you support.
  2. NP5 S6 P2So you meet your organisation's standards and the industry standards.
  3. NP5 S6 P3Because laws, standards and best ways of working change. For example, the new Aged Care Act started in 2025.
  4. NP5 S6 P4Sharing what you learnt: talk about it at a staff meeting or handover.
  5. NP5 S6 P5Give a short presentation to your team.
  6. NP5 S6 P6Bring back brochures, posters or handouts and put them in the staff room.
  7. NP5 S6 P7Send a short email summary to your team and your supervisor.

7 When a person wants to complain **

Every person who uses a service has the right to complain, and to complain without fear. A complaint helps the service get better.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP5 S7 P1Listen and show empathy: "I am sorry this happened. Thank you for telling me."
  2. NP5 S7 P2Give the person the contact details of your supervisor or manager.
  3. NP5 S7 P3If they ask, help them write the complaint, or take their spoken or written complaint to your supervisor or manager.
  4. NP5 S7 P4A complaint does not have to be in writing. The organisation gives every person information (for example a brochure) on how to complain.
  5. NP5 S7 P5Every complaint is written in the complaints register, and the organisation answers it within its set timeframes. It is not "whenever the manager likes".
  6. NP5 S7 P6If it is not fixed inside the service, help the person get an advocate (for example OPAN), or contact the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission (the Complaints Commissioner) or the NDIS Commission.
  7. NP5 S7 P7Keep complaints private. The person must never be punished or treated badly for complaining.

8 Worried about a co-worker? **

Sometimes you see a co-worker do something that is against the law or not ethical. What do you do?

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP5 S8 P1Report it to your supervisor or manager.
  2. NP5 S8 P2If your supervisor is part of the problem, go to the next manager up.
  3. NP5 S8 P3Report the facts: what you saw and heard, when and where. Do not add your opinion.
  4. NP5 S8 P4Do not talk about it with other staff or the people you support. That is gossip, and it breaks privacy.
  5. NP5 S8 P5The law protects whistleblowers: you cannot be punished for reporting in good faith. In aged care, you can also tell the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission.
