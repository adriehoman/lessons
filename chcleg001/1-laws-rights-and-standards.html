1 What guides our work: law and ethics

Four things guide community services: laws, court decisions, funding rules and practice standards.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S1 P1Legislation is a law made by Parliament. It is also called an Act or statute law. (Qld) means a Queensland law. (Cth) means a Commonwealth law for all of Australia.
  2. NP3 S1 P2Laws do three things: they direct (say what people must do), protect (keep people safe) and inform (give information).
  3. NP3 S1 P3Common law is made by judges in court cases. For example, a person can take a worker to court if the worker did not take enough care.
  4. NP3 S1 P4When there is no law about something, governments and organisations write standards and guidelines. Then every service works in the same safe way.
  5. NP3 S1 P5Law is a set of rules everyone must follow. Breaking the law can mean fines, court or prison.
  6. NP3 S1 P6Ethics is about what is right and fair. Ethics are guided by your values and your purpose. There is often more than one good way to respond, and there is no fine for an ethical breach.

2 Human rights and human needs

Every person has human rights. Your job is to meet people's needs and protect their rights.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S2 P1The Universal Declaration of Human Rights was written by the United Nations in 1948. It says human rights belong to every person, no matter who they are or where they live.
  2. NP3 S2 P2Human rights include: being treated with dignity and respect, being safe, having privacy, making your own choices, and being treated fairly (no discrimination).
  3. NP3 S2 P3A need is something a person must have to live well, for example food, water, shelter, safety and health care.
  4. NP3 S2 P4A right is something a person is entitled to (allowed to have), for example privacy or the right to say no.
  5. NP3 S2 P5Needs and rights work together. A person does not have the right to get everything they want from a worker. And their rights are not more important than their needs. You support both.
  6. NP3 S2 P6In Queensland, the Human Rights Act 2019 (Qld) protects people's rights. Australia also signed the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities.

3 The Acts you need to know **

There are many laws for community services. Flip the cards to meet the ones you will use most. Learn the name, the year and (Qld) or (Cth).

Listen to the explanation

When you name a law in an answer, write the full name, the year and (Qld) or (Cth), for example Aged Care Act 2024 (Cth).

Aged Care Act 2024 (Cth)

The main law for aged care. It started on 1 November 2025 and puts the rights of older people first.

Disability Services Act 2006 (Qld)

The main Queensland law for disability services. It protects the rights of people with disability and makes services safe.

NDIS Act 2013 (Cth)

Set up the National Disability Insurance Scheme, which pays for supports so people with disability can reach their goals.

Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and Information Privacy Act 2009 (Qld)

Protect personal information: how it is collected, stored, used and shared.

Discrimination laws

Anti-Discrimination Act 1991 (Qld), Disability Discrimination Act 1992 (Cth), Age Discrimination Act 2004 (Cth). It is against the law to treat people unfairly because of attributes like age, disability, race, sex or religion.

Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (Qld)

Keeps workers and everyone at the workplace safe. You have duties under it too.

Guardianship and Administration Act 2000 (Qld)

Helps adults who cannot make some decisions for themselves. It explains who can decide for them, and how.

Mental Health Act 2016 (Qld)

Protects the rights of people with a mental illness, including when they get treatment they did not ask for.

Medicines and Poisons Act 2019 (Qld)

Controls who can give or handle medicines, especially strong ones like morphine. Older resources name the Health (Drugs and Poisons) Regulation 1996.

Human Rights Act 2019 (Qld)

Protects human rights in Queensland. Government services must respect them.

Watch out The old Aged Care Act 1997 is gone. Write Aged Care Act 2024 (Cth).

4 The Aged Care Act 2024

The Aged Care Act 2024 (Cth) is the main law for aged care. It started on 1 November 2025. New since 2025

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S4 P1It replaced the Aged Care Act 1997. The Department of Health, Disability and Ageing runs it. The government uses it to pay aged care providers and to control the fees they charge.
  2. NP3 S4 P2Objective 1: pay for aged care and help people get good quality services.
  3. NP3 S4 P3Objective 2: avoid doubling up on services (two services doing the same job).
  4. NP3 S4 P4Objective 3: help older people stay in their own home for longer (ageing in place).
  5. NP3 S4 P5Objective 4: help older people enjoy the same rights as everyone else in Australia.
  6. NP3 S4 P6The Act puts the rights of older people first. The Statement of Rights has 13 rights, for example: to make your own choices, to be treated with dignity and respect, to have your culture respected, to have your privacy, and to complain without fear. It replaced the Charter of Aged Care Rights. New since 2025
  7. NP3 S4 P7It also brings stronger standards, one assessment to find out what care a person needs, checks on workers (a police certificate or an NDIS Worker Check), and protection for whistleblowers who report problems. New since 2025

5 The Disability Services Act 2006 and the NDIS

For disability services in Queensland, your quiz names the Disability Services Act 2006 (Qld) as the main law.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S5 P1The Disability Services Act 2006 (Qld) aims to protect (safeguard) the rights of people with disability.
  2. NP3 S5 P2It makes services work under one national set of rules (a national regulatory framework).
  3. NP3 S5 P3It makes sure services are safe, accountable (they must answer for what they do), and meet each person's needs and goals.
  4. NP3 S5 P4It also covers worker screening, checks (audits) of funded services, and the rules for using restrictive practices.
  5. NP3 S5 P5The NDIS Act 2013 (Cth) set up the National Disability Insurance Scheme. The NDIS pays for "reasonable and necessary" supports so people with a permanent and significant disability can reach their own goals.
  6. NP3 S5 P6The NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission checks NDIS providers and receives their reportable incidents (Lesson 3).
Watch out In the quiz, "the primary legislation for disability services" is the Disability Services Act 2006 (Qld). It is not the Disability Discrimination Act and not the UN Convention.

6 Standards in aged care **

Aged care services must meet the Strengthened Aged Care Quality Standards. There are 7 standards. They started on 1 November 2025. Each one has things you do as a worker. Flip the cards.

Listen to the explanation

Standard 1: The Person

  • Ask and respect preferences: how they like to be called, their routines, their culture.
  • Support choice and independence: offer choices in meals, clothes, shower times and activities.
  • Keep dignity and privacy: close doors or curtains, explain what you are doing, ask for consent first.

Standard 2: The Organisation

  • Follow policies and procedures for care, safety, infection control and incidents.
  • Report risks, incidents and concerns straight away.
  • Take part in training, handovers and team meetings.

Standard 3: Care and Services

  • Read and follow the care plan and check for changes.
  • Communicate clearly at handover, and listen to the person and family.
  • Report changes in health, behaviour or wellbeing.

Standard 4: The Environment

  • Keep the place clean and safe: clear walkways, clean up spills.
  • Follow infection control: hand hygiene and PPE.
  • Report hazards like broken equipment or poor lighting.

Standard 5: Clinical Care

  • Watch for and report changes in health: pain, confusion, skin.
  • Follow safe care practices you are trained for.
  • Work within your role and ask when unsure.

Standard 6: Food and Nutrition

  • Follow dietary needs: allergies, texture-modified diets, cultural foods.
  • Help with eating respectfully and give enough time.
  • Report poor appetite, weight loss or swallowing problems.

Standard 7: The Residential Community

  • Encourage social activities based on the person's interests.
  • Support changes like a new room or coming back from hospital.
  • Build respectful relationships with residents, families and colleagues.

7 Standards in disability services **

Disability services meet the National Standards for Disability Services. There are 6 standards. Here is each one, with an example of what you do.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S7 P1Standard 1: Rights. The person's rights are respected. Example: I ask before I go into their room, and I keep their information private.
  2. NP3 S7 P2Standard 2: Participation and Inclusion. The person takes part in their community. Example: I help them get to their bowling club.
  3. NP3 S7 P3Standard 3: Individual Outcomes. Supports help the person reach their own goals. Example: I follow their plan to learn to cook a meal.
  4. NP3 S7 P4Standard 4: Feedback and Complaints. People can give feedback and complain safely. Example: I tell them how to make a complaint and help them do it.
  5. NP3 S7 P5Standard 5: Service Access. People can get the service fairly. Example: I give information in a way the person understands.
  6. NP3 S7 P6Standard 6: Service Management. The service is well run. Example: I follow the policies and go to training.

NDIS providers also follow the NDIS Practice Standards and the NDIS Code of Conduct.

Watch out In the quiz, the standards for disability services are the Disability Services Standards (National Standards for Disability Services). The Strengthened Aged Care Quality Standards are for aged care.

8 Where to find the legal rules of your job **

How do you know what the law expects from you? Look in these places.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S8 P1Your organisation's policy and procedure manual.
  2. NP3 S8 P2Your job description (what your role is and is not).
  3. NP3 S8 P3The code of conduct (how you must behave).
  4. NP3 S8 P4Your supervisor or manager: ask when you are not sure.
  5. NP3 S8 P5The industrial award or work agreement (your pay and conditions).
  6. NP3 S8 P6The laws themselves, for example the Aged Care Act 2024.
  7. NP3 S8 P7The standards for your sector (aged care or disability).
Watch out Give three different sources. "Policies, procedures and the policy manual" counts as one source, not three.

9 Policies and procedures

Policies and procedures keep everyone safe and help all staff work the same way.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S9 P1A policy says what must happen and why. A procedure gives the steps: how to do it.
  2. NP3 S9 P2They are written and reviewed together with the staff who use them (consultation). Not by management alone.
  3. NP3 S9 P3Every policy and procedure has a review date.
  4. NP3 S9 P4They all have the same format (they look the same), so they are easy to find and read.
  5. NP3 S9 P5They are updated straight away when a law or standard changes. They do not wait for the review date.
  6. NP3 S9 P6You must read them, follow them, and ask if you do not understand.

10 Codes of conduct and codes of practice

Two kinds of "code" guide your work. They sound alike, but they are different.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S10 P1A code of conduct sets out the behaviour expected from all staff. It is not about working conditions or pay.
  2. NP3 S10 P2The Aged Care Code of Conduct applies to every aged care worker. The NDIS Code of Conduct applies to NDIS workers. Both are backed by law.
  3. NP3 S10 P3For example, you must: treat people with dignity and respect, respect their privacy, give safe, good quality support, speak up promptly about concerns, help stop abuse and neglect, and act with honesty and integrity.
  4. NP3 S10 P4A code of practice is a practical guide that explains how to follow the work health and safety laws, for example how to do manual tasks or handle hazardous chemicals safely.
  5. NP3 S10 P5Breaking a code of conduct can mean warnings, losing your job, and for aged care, being banned from working in aged care.

11 Law or ethics?

Now put it together. Is each statement about law or about ethics?

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S11 P1Law: a set of rules and regulations; can lead to punishment, fines or prison; has enforceable standards; includes the Aged Care and NDIS Codes of Conduct.
  2. NP3 S11 P2Ethics: shaped by values and purpose; often more than one way to respond; no punishment or fines for a breach.
