1. Structural and systemic obstacles are things that make it hard for people to feel empowered and equal:

  • Structural Obstacles: These are rules and regulations from governments and organizations that limit equal access to resources and opportunities.
  • Systemic Obstacles: These are bigger processes in a system that keep inequality going, like long-lasting biases and imbalances of power.

2. Psychological and Physiological Obstacles

Psychological Barriers:

  • Internal factors rooted in mindset, beliefs, and perceptions.
  • Older people fearing ageing, retirement, and change may face mental health issues.
  • Low self-esteem, fear of failure, and learned hopelessness hinder independence.
  • Situations where others make decisions can lead to learned hopelessness.

Physiological Barriers:

  • Ageing brings memory loss, sensory changes, and impacts on bone and muscle strength.
  • Dementia, not a normal part of ageing, affects memory, communication, and decision-making.
  • Incontinence, regardless of age, can hinder social activities and impact self-esteem.
  • Sedative medications like antihistamines and antidepressants may slow down the body.
  • Polypharmacy, using multiple medications, can lead to adverse effects and impact cognitive function.

3. Cultural and spiritual obstacles

  • Cultural differences require awareness and understanding.
  • Providing support in diverse languages and culturally safe environments is crucial.
  • It's a way of life, balancing values with how life is lived.
  • Acknowledging beliefs, experiences, and backgrounds is key, regardless of gender, ethnicity, or beliefs.

4. Stereotyping, stigmas and myths

  • Stereotyping is when we simplify things by making generalizations about people.
  • We do this based on our experiences with others who share similar roles, genders, or ethnicities.
  • It's like a quick way our brains try to understand the world, even though it might not always be accurate.

5. Barriers to Empowerment: Examples **

  1. Economic Barriers:
    Example: Not having enough money or financial stability can limit access to education, personal growth opportunities, healthcare, and employment. This affects life choices, housing, and overall well-being.
  2. Stigma:
    Example: Negative attitudes and stereotypes can hinder support for certain groups. For instance, assuming people in wheelchairs aren't interested in sports or believing older individuals are not interested in learning.
  3. Ageism:
    Example: Treating someone unfairly due to their age, like not considering a highly qualified 65-year-old for a job or dismissing the views of an older person because they're considered "out of touch."
  4. Cultural Barriers:
    Example: Businesses and services lacking cultural awareness and competency, or having outdated policies that don't accommodate multicultural needs. This could include literature only in English or discrimination based on cultural practices.
  5. Physical Barriers:
    Example: Inaccessible building design and public spaces, limited public transport, and a lack of healthcare options can create barriers for people with mobility needs.
  6. Emotional Barriers:
    Example: Mental health disorders, cognitive impairments, and stress can pose emotional barriers to empowerment.
  7. Social Barriers:
    Example: Limited access to social events and activities, mobility issues, and a loss of contact with friends can create social barriers.

6. Internal Barriers to Empowerment: Overcoming Challenges **

  • Physical Barriers:
    • Example: Physical disability, hearing loss, or vision impairment affecting navigation and communication.
    • Strategy: Provide aids, assistive devices, and modify the environment for better accessibility.
  • Cognitive Barriers:
    • Example: Cognitive development or impairment, language barriers, limited education, neurological disorders.
    • Strategy: Implement inclusive, supported education, ensure information accessibility, promote skill development, collaborate with guardians, advocacy services, interpreters, or relevant agencies.
  • Social Barriers:
    • Example: Withdrawing from social interactions due to fear of judgment, lack of communication or social skills, limited funds or transport.
    • Strategy: Offer life skills and empowerment programs, enhance access to education, and promote skill development.

7. Staff expectations and practices

My beliefs and values greatly influence how I help people. This can be good or sometimes not so good. Here's how: **

Race: Good Influence: I like different cultures, and I make sure everyone feels respected and understood, no matter where they're from.

Not So Good Influence: Sometimes, without knowing it, I might treat people unfairly based on their race. It's important to watch out for that.

Gender: Good Influence: I believe everyone should be treated the same, no matter their gender. I make sure to be fair to everyone.

Not So Good Influence: Sometimes, I might treat people differently based on what I think about their gender. This is something I need to be aware of.

Religion: Good Influence: I respect all religions and make sure to include people's religious practices in what I do. I want everyone to feel welcome.

Not So Good Influence: Sometimes, I might let my own beliefs about religion affect how I help people. I need to make sure I don't do that.

Culture: Good Influence: I enjoy different cultures, and I adjust how I help people to fit with their cultural background. I want to make sure everyone feels understood.

Not So Good Influence: Sometimes, I might not understand someone's culture, and it can affect how I help them. I need to learn more and be careful not to stereotype.

Language: Good Influence: I know people speak different languages, so I use different ways to communicate. I want to make sure everyone understands.

Not So Good Influence: Sometimes, I might not communicate well because of language differences. I need to be more careful about that.

Sexuality: Good Influence: I accept everyone, no matter their sexual orientation. I want to create a safe and welcoming space for everyone.

Not So Good Influence: Sometimes, I might have ideas about sexuality that affect how I help people. I need to be aware of that and treat everyone the same.

I always think about how I can be better, understand different cultures, and learn from others. This helps me provide support that is fair, respectful, and considers everyone's values.

8. Building Relationships and Empowering Others: A Simple Guide

Dispelling Myths and Stereotypes:

  • Question your own beliefs and behaviors to build connections and empower others.
  • This self-awareness may feel uncomfortable but helps identify limitations, leading to seeking more information or training.

Values:

  • Identify your life principles, like peace, freedom, or religious beliefs.
  • Respect that others, including those you support, have their own values.

Avoiding Negative Influences:

  • Be cautious not to let your personal values affect your guidance to others.
  • Stay aware of biases or preconceived ideas from past experiences that may impact interactions.

Language:

  • Pay attention to how staff refer to the person they're supporting.
  • Avoid using diagnoses or slang, as it influences how others perceive the individual.

Elderspeak:

  • Watch out for elderspeak, a demeaning tone often used in aged care but can occur in disability support.
  • Elderspeak involves a sing-song voice, childish and rhyming and inappropriate terms, leading to disrespect and disempowerment.

9. Strategies to facilitate empowerment

9. Strategies to facilitate

9a. Positive Views on Disability:

Diverse Natural Variation:

  • Embrace disability as a natural human variation to reduce stigmas.

Human Rights Emphasis:

  • Highlight the importance of human rights for people with disabilities.

Advocacy Promotion:

  • Encourage advocacy in various forms.

Accessibility Tools:

  • Support industry growth in developing tools for better accessibility.

Collaborative Living:

  • Promote collaboration for inclusion and community participation.

Empowerment Strategies:

  • Adopt positive strategies for independence and empowerment.

9b. Positive Aging Essentials:

1. Positive Attitude:

  • Maintain a positive mindset to face aging changes resiliently.

2. Healthy Lifestyle:

  • Adopt a balanced diet, stay hydrated, and engage in regular, low-impact activities for overall well-being.

3. Meaningful Interactions:

  • Connect with friends, participate in community activities, and share skills with others for fulfilling daily interactions.

4. Coping Mechanisms:

  • Build coping mechanisms to find solutions when challenges arise.

5. Motivation and Encouragement:

  • Pursue goals with personal motivation and benefit from positive support and feedback from others.

9c. ** These strategies aim to empower individuals in exercising their rights while maintaining their independence.

1. Right to Privacy: This involves deciding how much personal information one shares with an organization and its staff. The strategies to support this right include:

  • Reminding the person of their rights and providing a copy of the Statements of Rights.
  • Ensuring the organization has policies and procedures to keep personal information confidential.
  • Encouraging the use of advocacy services if the person is unsure about expressing their rights.

2. Right to Make Decisions about Life: This pertains to the ability to be involved in the development of an individualized plan. Strategies for supporting this right include:

  • Reminding the person of their rights and providing a copy of the Statements of Rights.
  • Ensuring the organization has policies and procedures to maintain the confidentiality of personal information.
  • Encouraging the use of advocacy services if the person is not confident in expressing their rights.
  • Involving the person in the development of their plan using person-centered practices.
  • Seeking consent before starting a task on behalf of the person.
