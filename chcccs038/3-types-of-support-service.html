CHCCCS038 (3) Types of support service

The type of support provided will depend upon the person's needs and goals; and how they would like to achieve them.

1. My aged care

My Aged Care was established in 2013 and was designed to consolidate information regarding support services for older people, as well as education and resources for users, staff and organisations. You can visit their website to explore the options available.

Aged care services include:

  • home packages
  • rural and remote services
  • aged care facilities
  • aged care assessment team
  • Commonwealth respite and Carelink centres
  • transitional care.

2. NDIS Commission

The NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission was established in 2018 as a central agency for people with disability, staff and organisations to obtain direction and support. The breadth of support services provided under NDIS funding is expansive, depending upon the goals and needs of the person.

Services are commonly provided in:

  • individual homes
  • supported living centres
  • general community
  • respite centres
  • lifestyle centres.

3. Genetic Conditions **

Genetic conditions happen when there's a change in a person's genes or DNA. Sometimes, these conditions are passed down from parents (like Huntington's disease, cystic fibrosis, or fragile X syndrome), or they can happen without any family history (like Down syndrome). The support someone needs depends on how the condition affects them and what their goals and preferences are.

Diagnosis: Cystic Fibrosis

Genetic disorder affecting the respiratory and digestive systems. Thick, sticky mucus buildup in airways and lungs, impacting breathing.

  • Support Required:
    • Ongoing medication and treatment.
    • Healthy diet and exercise for overall strength.
  • Agency that could provide support: Cystic Fibrosis Australia.
  • Support Services: Information and resources. Support programs and events.

Diagnosis: Fragile X Syndrome

Genetic condition causing intellectual, behavioral, and physical symptoms. Varied symptoms in each person.

  • Support Required:
    • Therapies and interventions by allied health specialists including psychologists, occupational therapists and physiotherapists.
    • Early diagnosis and interventions are beneficial to the person.
  • Agency that could provide support: Fragile X Association of Australia.
  • Support Services: Multiple therapies and interventions. Information and support for families.

Diagnosis: Down Syndrome

Additional chromosome (21) impacting growth and development. Affects physical characteristics, intellectual development, increased health risks.

  • Support Required:
    • Speech, language, hearing and vision support.
    • Physiotherapy for mobility and strength
    • Additional time and support to gain competence and confidence with activities of daily living, social norms etc.
  • Agency that could provide support: Down Syndrome Australia.
  • Support Services: Prenatal services, new parent services. Support for professionals, education services.

Diagnosis: Huntington's Disease

A genetic test can confirm the presence of Huntington's Disease. Huntington's Disease is a genetic disorder that affects the brain, causing problems with movement, cognitive function, and emotions.

  • Support Required:
    • Ongoing medical care to manage symptoms.
    • Medications to control movement and psychiatric symptoms.
    • Mental health support for emotional well-being.
    • Genetic counseling for individuals and families.
    • Support for daily activities as the disease progresses.
  • Agency that could provide support: Huntington's Queensland
  • Support services the agency offers:
    • Information and education about Huntington's Disease.
    • Support groups for individuals and families.
    • Counseling services for emotional and psychological support.
    • Assistance in accessing medical and community services.
    • Advocacy to raise awareness and improve services for those affected by Huntington's Disease.

Physical Trauma

Physical trauma is a term that covers various events, injuries, and health issues affecting the body's muscles and bones. It can be caused by different things like:

  • Something passed down from family genes, such as muscular dystrophy.
  • Diseases affecting the nerves, like motor neurone disease.
  • Injuries from a hit or accident, like amputation or a brain injury.

Diagnosis: Muscular Dystrophy

There are 22 variations of this condition that involve weakness, or continual degeneration of muscles required for movement of body and bodily functions.

  • Support Required:
    • Assistance will be required with mobility, transfers, physical therapies and other activities of daily living (cooking, cleaning, showering included).
  • Agency that could provide support: Muscular Dystrophy Australia.
  • Support Services: Support programs. Advocacy and information.

Diagnosis: Motor Neurone Disease (MND)

Neurological disorder that onsets in adulthood and progressively stops the body from being able to control its muscles, resulting in the inability to walk, talk, swallow and breathe independently.

  • Support Required:
    • The use of aids to support independence as mobility is impacted
    • undertaking a variety of therapies to assist with maintaining muscle tone as long as possible; medications that can slow progression
    • eventual assistance with all activities of daily living, artificial feeding (e.g. nasogastric tube) and breathing aids (e.g. non-invasive ventilator).
  • Agency that could provide support: MND Australia.
  • Support Services:
    • Information and resources.
    • Support for carers.

Diagnosis: Acquired Brain Injury / Stroke **

Injury to the brain after birth. Cognitive and physical impairments. It will depend on the nature, severity and location of damage as to the supports required by the person. For example, spinal cord damage may result in the person being paralysed.

  • Support Required:
    • They would require ongoing physical therapy to maintain muscle tone and movement in affected limbs, assistance with daily living activities that requires mobility or transfers
  • Agency that could provide support: ABI Services. / Stroke Foundation
  • Support Services: Information and advocacy. Support groups and education. Education, research, advocacy, support groups

4. Psychological disabilities **

Psychological trauma can occur as a result of being unable to cope after a distressing experience, or the memories of. These could include natural disasters, accidents, or abuse. It can impact on a person's sense of security, trust in others and surroundings. It can lead to a diagnosis of PTSD.

Diagnosis: Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)

Mental health disorder following a traumatic event. Persistent distressing symptoms, flashbacks, and anxiety.

  • Support Required:
    • Psychotherapy and counseling.
    • Medications for symptom management.
  • Agency that could provide support: Phoenix Australia – Centre for Posttraumatic Mental Health.
  • Support Services: Trauma-informed care. Training and resources for professionals.

Diagnosis: Schizophrenia

Severe mental disorder affecting thinking, feeling, and behavior. Disorganized thoughts, hallucinations, and social withdrawal.

  • Support Required:
    • Antipsychotic medications.
    • Psychotherapy and community support.
  • Agency that could provide support: SANE Australia.
  • Support Services: Helpline and online forums. Resources for individuals and families. Education, online, face to face and peer support groups

Diagnosis: Psychosocial Disability

Impairment of social functioning due to mental health or emotional issues. Challenges in social interactions, relationships, and daily activities.

  • Support Required:
    • Psychosocial support programs.
    • Access to mental health services.
  • Agency that could provide support: Mind Australia.
  • Support Services: Community-based mental health services. Residential and outreach programs.

5. Chronic Diseases **

Chronic disease refers to an ongoing condition that requires regular treatment and impacts upon a person's ability to complete activities of daily living. Nearly half of the Australian population has at least one chronic disease

  • Not all chronic diseases are life-limiting.
  • Not all chronic diseases are debilitating in a way that creates disability.

Diagnosis: Diabetes

Metabolic disorder affecting blood sugar levels. Insulin deficiency or resistance leading to high blood sugar.

  • Support Required:
    • Medication and insulin therapy.
    • Lifestyle management and dietary changes.
  • Agency that could provide support: Diabetes Australia.
  • Support Services: Information and education. Support for self-management strategies.

Diagnosis: Asthma

Chronic respiratory condition causing airway inflammation. Recurrent episodes of wheezing, breathlessness, and coughing.

  • Support Required:
    • Inhaler medications and preventive measures.
    • Asthma action plans.
  • Agency that could provide support: National Asthma Council Australia.
  • Support Services: Education and resources. Helpline for advice.

Diagnosis: Heart Disease

Conditions affecting the heart and blood vessels. Coronary artery disease, heart failure, etc.

  • Support Required:
    • Medications and lifestyle changes.
    • Cardiac rehabilitation.
  • Agency that could provide support: Heart Foundation Australia.
  • Support Services: Healthy living resources. Peer support networks.

Diagnosis: Back Pain

Various causes, including muscle strain or structural issues. Pain or discomfort in the back.

  • Support Required:
    • Physiotherapy and pain management.
    • Exercise and posture correction.
  • Agency that could provide support: Back Pain Australia.
  • Support Services: Information and resources. Exercise programs.

Diagnosis: Arthritis

Inflammation of joints causing pain and stiffness. Osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, etc.

  • Support Required:
    • Medications and anti-inflammatory drugs.
    • Physiotherapy and joint protection.
  • Agency that could provide support: Arthritis Australia.
  • Support Services: Exercise and self-management programs. Information and advice.

Diagnosis: Haemophilia

Genetic bleeding disorder. Inability to form blood clots.

  • Support Required:
    • Clotting factor replacement therapy.
    • Bleeding management plans.
  • Agency that could provide support: Haemophilia Foundation Australia.
  • Support Services: Treatment information. Advocacy and support.

Diagnosis: Glaucoma

Eye condition causing optic nerve damage. Increased intraocular pressure.

  • Support Required:
    • Eye drops and medications.
    • Surgery in advanced cases.
  • Agency that could provide support: Glaucoma Australia.
  • Support Services: Information and awareness. Support for affected individuals.

Diagnosis: Cancer

Uncontrolled cell growth leading to tumor formation. Various types affecting different organs.

  • Support Required:
    • Surgery, chemotherapy, or radiation.
    • Palliative care and emotional support.
  • Agency that could provide support: Cancer Council Australia.
  • Support Services: Information and resources. Support for patients and families.

6. Seeking support

These are the people you should reach out to first:

  • Your peers
  • Registered Nurse
  • Coordinators – team leaders - supervisors

It's crucial to understand that this doesn't mean you're not important, skilled, or good at your job. It simply gives everyone a clear idea of their roles in the organization and where to get help when things go beyond what you're trained for. Talking to colleagues and supervisors is also a chance to discuss your experiences, get feedback on your work, and learn new skills for ongoing improvement.

7. Building relationships

7a. Strong communication skills are key to building relationships. People also appreciate a conversation that is not hurried and allows them to express their needs and fears. Using this information wisely means that support services are much more likely to fit the needs of the person.

  • Avoid assumptions or patronizing behavior.
  • Actively listen, use open body language, and take your time during conversations.
  • Find a quiet and comfortable place for discussions, minimizing background noise.
  • Adapt your communication style to suit the person's needs.
  • Employ various communication techniques to keep the conversation flowing.
  • Stay person-centered; don't presume choices for the person.
  • Confirm accurate understanding by checking that you've heard and understood the information correctly.

8. Language to support empowerment

Words are powerful because they have the ability to shape perceptions, ignite emotions and inspire actions. In the disability and aged care sectors, words hold particular significance and power due to the impact on the person, their goals, and society as a whole. Words influence:

  • a person's identity
  • dignity and respect
  • advocacy and empowerment
  • shape public perceptions
  • communication and accessibility.

9. Advocacy

Advocacy means actively supporting a cause or person to create positive change, raise awareness, and influence decisions. Advocacy services offer:

  • Information for understanding rights and making informed choices.
  • Education on disability and aged care sectors.
  • Assistance for those feeling pressured, dissatisfied, or facing abuse.
  • Support in addressing discrimination, legal matters, and ensuring basic human needs are met.
