1. Human Rights are like the basic rules that guide how we help older people and those with disabilities. They're important because they make sure that everyone gets fair and respectful support. This means considering their rights at every step - from picking the right services to creating a plan just for them, deciding what help they need, and how they get it. Human Rights also affect how staff members and the people they're helping interact with each other. So basically, they're the foundation for a good support system. *CHCCCS038*

2. Legal and Ethical Definitions

Legal and Ethical Examples

Code of Conduct for Aged Care

Definition: A set of rules about how staff should act and behave at work.

Example: Staff wear their uniform, speak politely, and follow the organisation’s values.

Confidentiality

Definition: Keeping private information safe and not sharing it with others unless needed for care.

Example: Client files are kept in locked cabinets or password-protected systems, and staff don’t repeat private conversations.

Dignity of Risk

Definition: Allowing people to make their own choices, even if there is some risk, because it supports independence and self-esteem.

Example: A client chooses to walk outside without a walking aid, and staff support them while monitoring safety.

Disclosure

Definition: Sharing important information only with the right people and for the right reasons.

Example: A support worker reports a client’s health concern to a supervisor so the client can get proper care.

Duty of Care

Definition: The responsibility to keep others safe and well.

Example: Staff clean up spills quickly to prevent falls and always follow workplace policies and procedures.

Human Rights

Definition: The basic rights and freedoms that everyone has, no matter their background, culture, or beliefs.

Example: Staff respect client choices, such as what food they want to eat or when they want to go to bed, in line with the Charter of Aged Care Rights.

SIRS (Serious Incident Response Scheme)

Definition: A legal requirement for staff to report any signs of suspected abuse or neglect of vulnerable people.

Example: Staff report indicators of abuse to management within 24 hours. Management investigates and follows SIRS procedures.

Work Health and Safety (WHS)

Definition: The responsibility to keep the workplace safe and protect staff, clients, and others from hazards and risks.

Example: Staff identify and report hazards so they can be fixed, reducing risks in the workplace.
