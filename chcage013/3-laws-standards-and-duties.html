In Lessons 1 and 2 you learned about aged care and the person at the centre. This lesson is about the rules that protect older people. A law (also called an Act or legislation) is a rule made by the government that everyone must follow. Standards describe what good care looks like. Your workplace turns these into policies and procedures, and you follow them every day.

1 Who keeps aged care safe? **

The Royal Commission found that care for older Australians was not always safe. Safeguarding bodies are organisations that help make care safer.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP8 S1 P1Safeguarding bodies help aged care providers give safe, quality care.
  2. NP8 S1 P2They help providers understand the risks and put policies and procedures in place that follow the laws and standards.
  3. NP8 S1 P3Examples: the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission (the national regulator for aged care), WorkSafe Queensland (work health and safety) and the Office of the Health Ombudsman (complaints about health services in Queensland).

2 The Aged Care Act 2024 **

The Aged Care Act 2024 is the main law for aged care that the Australian Government pays for. It started on 1 November 2025. It replaced the Aged Care Act 1997.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP8 S2 P1It sets the rules for which providers are allowed to give government-funded aged care (provider approval).
  2. NP8 S2 P2It sets the rules for funding: subsidies the government pays, and fees the person pays.
  3. NP8 S2 P3It protects the rights of older people and the quality of care they receive. Its Statement of Rights is part of the law.
  4. NP8 S2 P4It sets the standards for the industry and how the industry is regulated (checked).
  5. NP8 S2 P5It says what happens when a provider does not follow the rules (non-compliance).
Watch out Write about the Aged Care Act 2024, not the 1997 Act. The 1997 Act is old.

3 The Work Health and Safety Act 2011 **

The Work Health and Safety (WHS) Act 2011 protects people at work. In Queensland, WorkSafe Queensland makes sure people follow it.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP8 S3 P1Its main purpose: a national framework (one set of rules for Australia) to protect workers and other people from harm at work.
  2. NP8 S3 P2It does this by finding risks, then removing them or making them smaller (eliminating or minimising risks).
  3. NP8 S3 P3It promotes education and training about work health and safety.
  4. NP8 S3 P4It explains how the law is enforced (how people are made to follow it).
  5. NP8 S3 P5It supports continuous improvement of safety standards.
  6. NP8 S3 P6It supports workplace representation: workers and employers cooperate, consult (talk together) and solve safety problems.
  7. NP8 S3 P7WorkSafe Queensland checks that workplaces follow WHS laws, teaches employers and workers about their duties, and investigates serious injuries and deaths at work.

4 The Office of the Health Ombudsman **

An ombudsman is an independent person or office that deals with complaints. The Office of the Health Ombudsman (OHO) deals with complaints about health services in Queensland.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP8 S4 P1The OHO was set up by the Health Ombudsman Act 2013. It is independent (it does not belong to a hospital or provider).
  2. NP8 S4 P2Anyone can contact the OHO if they have a complaint about a health service they received in Queensland.
  3. NP8 S4 P3It protects public health and safety.
  4. NP8 S4 P4It makes sure health practitioners (for example nurses and doctors) work in a safe, competent and professional way.
  5. NP8 S4 P5It promotes quality services from health service organisations.
  6. NP8 S4 P6It receives and manages complaints, and it acts on them (for example it investigates).
Watch out Do not stop at "it takes complaints". Also say it protects public safety, checks that health workers are safe and professional, and acts on complaints.

5 The 7 Strengthened Aged Care Quality Standards **

The Strengthened Aged Care Quality Standards started on 1 November 2025. They describe what safe, good quality care looks like. The Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission checks that providers meet them.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP8 S5 P1Standard 1: The Person. Know the people you care for and support their choices. (Person-centred care; dignity, respect and privacy; choice and independence.)
  2. NP8 S5 P2Standard 2: The Organisation. The organisation helps workers do their job well. (Policies, training, managing risks, incidents and complaints.)
  3. NP8 S5 P3Standard 3: Care and Services. Know who you care for and what matters to them. (Assessment and planning, communication, safe delivery of care.)
  4. NP8 S5 P4Standard 4: The Environment. Make the place safe and supportive. (Safe homes and buildings, infection prevention and control.)
  5. NP8 S5 P5Standard 5: Clinical Care. Understand the person's clinical (health) needs. (Medicines, skin care, dementia, palliative care.)
  6. NP8 S5 P6Standard 6: Food and Nutrition. Make sure people enjoy tasty, healthy food every day. (Food and drink choices, help with eating, the dining experience.)
  7. NP8 S5 P7Standard 7: The Residential Community. Work together to build a connected community in residential care. (Daily living, moving in or out.)

Read the standards: Strengthened Aged Care Quality Standards and the poster for workers. Older resources called Standard 1 "The Individual". It is now The Person.

6 The Standards in your daily work **

Each standard has things you can do in your job. Flip the cards. In your answer, choose one standard and write three things you do as a support worker.

Listen to the explanation

Standard 1: The Person

  • Ask and respect preferences: how they like to be called, their routines, their culture.
  • Support choice and independence: offer choices in meals, clothes, shower times and activities.
  • Keep dignity and privacy: close doors or curtains, explain what you are doing, ask for consent first.

Standard 2: The Organisation

  • Follow policies and procedures for care, safety, infection control and incidents.
  • Report risks, incidents and concerns straight away using the reporting system.
  • Take part in training, handovers and team meetings.

Standard 3: Care and Services

  • Read and follow the care plan before you give care, and check for changes.
  • Communicate clearly: share information at handover and listen to residents and families.
  • Give care that matches the plan, and report changes in health, behaviour or wellbeing.

Standard 4: The Environment

  • Keep the place clean and safe: clear walkways, clean up spills, store equipment safely.
  • Follow infection control: hand hygiene, PPE, cleaning procedures.
  • Report hazards like broken equipment, poor lighting or broken furniture.

Standard 5: Clinical Care

  • Watch for and report changes in health: pain, confusion, mobility, skin.
  • Follow safe care practices: support safe medicine routines (only what you are trained and allowed to do), help prevent pressure injuries.
  • Work within your role: only do tasks you are trained and allowed to do, and ask when unsure.

Standard 6: Food and Nutrition

  • Follow dietary needs: allergies, texture-modified diets, cultural foods.
  • Help with eating respectfully: encourage independence and give enough time.
  • Report concerns: poor appetite, weight loss, swallowing problems or dehydration.

Standard 7: The Residential Community

  • Encourage social activities based on the person's interests.
  • Support changes: give comfort and information when someone moves room or comes back from hospital.
  • Build respectful relationships with residents, families and colleagues.
Watch out Write what you do as a support worker ("I read the care plan..."), not what the organisation does. All three actions must belong to the standard you chose.

7 Privacy and confidentiality **

Privacy means keeping a person's body and personal space private. Confidentiality means keeping their information secret from people who do not need it.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP8 S7 P1Two laws protect personal information: the Privacy Act 1988 (Australia) and the Information Privacy Act 2009 (Queensland).
  2. NP8 S7 P2Keep records in a locked place (or a password-protected computer system).
  3. NP8 S7 P3Do not talk about a person's information with anyone who is not involved in their care, at work or outside work. That includes social media.
  4. NP8 S7 P4Protect the person's dignity during personal care: close doors and curtains, cover them, explain what you are doing.
  5. NP8 S7 P5Sharing a person's information without their consent breaks privacy and confidentiality.

8 Consent: informed and uninformed **

Consent means the person agrees to something. In aged care you must have consent before you start any care or task.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP8 S8 P1Informed consent: you give the person all the information about the task. They show they understand (for example by telling you back), and then they say yes, in words or in their own way of communicating.
  2. NP8 S8 P2Uninformed consent: the person says yes but without all the facts being explained. This is not real consent.
  3. NP8 S8 P3Ask for consent every time, for every task, even small ones like helping with a cardigan.
  4. NP8 S8 P4The person can change their mind at any time.
  5. NP8 S8 P5If the person cannot give consent, their substitute decision maker (for example a guardian) may decide for them. Check the care plan.

9 Duty of care and mandatory reporting **

Two more legal ideas you must follow at work.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP8 S9 P1Duty of care: you have a legal duty to give care in a way that does not cause harm. Example: you walk beside a person with a high falls risk when they go from their bedroom to the dining room.
  2. NP8 S9 P2If a person is hurt because a worker was careless (did something wrong, or did not do something they should), that is negligence, and the person can be paid compensation.
  3. NP8 S9 P3Mandatory reporting: "mandatory" means you must. You must report actual or suspected abuse of an older person. Example: you think someone used unreasonable force on a resident, so you tell your supervisor straight away and follow the workplace's Incident Management System (IMS).
  4. NP8 S9 P4You will learn more about abuse and reporting in Lesson 4.

10 The Code of Conduct for Aged Care

The Code of Conduct for Aged Care (from 1 December 2022) tells providers and workers how to behave. It protects older people and helps them trust their service.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP8 S10 P1Respect people's right to speak up, make their own choices and run their own lives.
  2. NP8 S10 P2Treat people with dignity and respect, and value their diversity.
  3. NP8 S10 P3Respect people's privacy.
  4. NP8 S10 P4Give care safely and competently (with care and skill).
  5. NP8 S10 P5Be honest, truthful and open (act with integrity).
  6. NP8 S10 P6Speak up quickly about anything that could affect the quality or safety of care.
  7. NP8 S10 P7Give care that is free from violence, discrimination, exploitation, neglect, abuse and sexual misconduct.
  8. NP8 S10 P8Take reasonable steps to prevent and respond to these harms.

On Wednesday, professional conduct also means: arrive on time, dress in your uniform with closed-in shoes, and treat the person with respect from the moment you knock on the door.
