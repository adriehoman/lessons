A Glossary: the words in plain English

These are words you will see in the lessons and in your assessment. The lessons link to this list.

Ageism
Treating someone unfairly, or thinking less of them, only because of their age.
Assistive technology
Tools and devices that help a person do things for themselves, for example a walking frame, a shower chair, a pill alarm or a tablet with large buttons.
Culturally competent
Able to work and live well with people from other cultures: you know your own culture, respect other cultures and value the differences.
Delegation
When the registered nurse gives the responsibility for a task to another worker. The RN stays accountable and must meet five requirements first.
Dementia
A group of conditions where memory, thinking and everyday skills slowly get worse. It is not a normal part of ageing.
Demographics
Facts about the people in a population, for example how many people there are of each age. "Changing demographics" means the mix of ages is changing: there are more older people.
Dignity of risk
Letting a person make their own choice about an activity that is good for them but has some risk. It respects their right to live their own life.
Duty of care
Your legal duty to give care in a way that does not cause harm to the person.
Elder abuse
When an older person is harmed by someone in a relationship of trust, for example a family member, friend or carer. It can be financial, physical, psychological, sexual, neglect or systems abuse.
Employee Assistance Program (EAP)
A free and private counselling service that many employers pay for, to help workers with stress or personal problems.
Empowerment
Giving a person the power to decide: they have choice and control over the decisions in their life.
Episodic
Comes and goes. An episodic condition has times when it is worse (episodes) and times when it is better.
Frail
Weak and easily hurt or made ill, often because of age and several health conditions at once.
Hazard
Something that could cause harm, for example a wet floor, a loose rug or a strong cleaning chemical.
Incident report
A form you fill in after something worrying happens at work. It has the facts only: what, when, where and who. Only your supervisor sees it.
Informed consent
The person gets all the information, shows they understand it, and then agrees.
Interdisciplinary team
Health workers from different professions working together for the person, for example physiotherapist, podiatrist, occupational therapist, speech pathologist, social worker and dietician.
Mandatory reporting
Something you must report by law or policy, for example suspected abuse of an older person.
Paternalism
Making decisions for someone and taking away their freedom, saying it is "for their own good", like a strict parent.
Person-centred approach
Seeing each person as an individual and working with them to make decisions about their care. Also called person-centred care.
Person-centred care
Care that is built around the person: their needs, their choices and what matters to them, not around the routine of the service.
Providers
The organisations that run aged care services, for example an aged care home or a home care company.
Psychosocial disability
The disability some people experience because of a mental health condition. It makes it hard to take part fully in everyday life. Not everyone with a mental health condition has one.
Respite care
A short stay or short period of care. It gives the family carer a break or helps the person recover.
Restrictive practice
Anything that limits a person's rights or their freedom to move. The five types are chemical, environmental, mechanical, physical and seclusion.
Rights-based approach
Remembering that every person keeps their rights at any age, and helping them make informed choices.
Risk
The chance (likelihood) that harm will happen because of a hazard, and how bad the harm could be.
Safeguarding bodies
Organisations that help keep aged care safe, for example the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission, WorkSafe Queensland and the Office of the Health Ombudsman.
Scope of practice
Everything you are trained and allowed to do in your job. Tasks outside it belong to someone else.
Self-determination
Deciding things for yourself and running your own life.
Social role valorisation
Helping people who are disadvantaged to have valued roles in the community, so they and others see what they can give. Often shortened to SRV.
Stereotyping
Believing that everyone in a group is the same, for example "all old people are sick". It ignores the real person.
Stigma
Seeing a person in a negative way because of one thing about them, like their age, so they become a label instead of a person.
Uninformed consent
The person says yes without being given all the facts. It is not real consent.
Value judgement
An opinion based on your own beliefs about what is good or bad, not on facts.
