Solomon does not live in a bubble. Around him there is a service that supports him, standards that service must meet, money that pays for it, agencies and networks in the community, a team of people, and tools that help him stay independent. In this lesson you learn how they all fit together, so you can answer the questions about the sector, the team and assistive technology.

1 Service delivery models: how support reaches the person

A Service delivery model is the way a program delivers support to a person. Aged care is for people 65 and older (50 and older for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people). The disability sector (the NDIS) is for people under 65.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP7 S1 P1Support at Home (since 1 November 2025): help for older people to stay living in their own home. It replaced Home Care Packages and Short-Term Restorative Care.
  2. NP7 S1 P2What Support at Home can give: personal care (showering, dressing), domestic help (cleaning, laundry, meals, shopping), nursing and allied health, transport, social support, respite for carers, and equipment and small home changes.
  3. NP7 S1 P3Short-term pathways in Support at Home: Restorative Care (get strength and skills back after illness), End-of-Life care at home, and Assistive Technology and Home Modifications (AT-HM).
  4. NP7 S1 P4Other aged care programs: the Commonwealth Home Support Programme (CHSP) for a little help at home, and residential aged care for people who can no longer live at home.
  5. NP7 S1 P5For Solomon: he lives in his own home and wants to stay there. So the best model is Support at Home. It can give him personal support, domestic help, transport and social support in his own home, and he can also choose respite and activity services.
  6. NP7 S1 P6In your answer: name ONE model (Support at Home), then explain the types of support it offers.

2 The standards every aged care service must meet

Services must meet industry standards. In aged care these are the Strengthened Aged Care Quality Standards (you can write "Aged Care Quality Standards"). There are seven standards. Learn the headings exactly.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP7 S2 P1Standard 1: The Person
  2. NP7 S2 P2Standard 2: The Organisation
  3. NP7 S2 P3Standard 3: Care and Services
  4. NP7 S2 P4Standard 4: The Environment
  5. NP7 S2 P5Standard 5: Clinical Care
  6. NP7 S2 P6Standard 6: Food and Nutrition
  7. NP7 S2 P7Standard 7: The Residential Community
  8. NP7 S2 P8The disability sector has different standards: the NDIS Practice Standards and Quality Indicators, with modules, not standards. You do NOT need these for Solomon.

3 Who pays? Funding for Solomon

Support costs money. A funding source is where the money comes from. Your assessment asks for one funding source that Solomon could apply for.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP7 S3 P1For Solomon: the Support at Home Program. The Australian Government funds it. Solomon pays a contribution based on his income and assets, and he is wealthy, so he may pay more.
  2. NP7 S3 P2How to get it: call or go online to My Aged Care. An assessor visits and checks his needs. If he is approved, he gets a budget and chooses an approved provider.
  3. NP7 S3 P3Other funding you may hear about: AN-ACC pays residential aged care homes; Medicare pays part of doctor and health visits; Centrelink pays pensions; the Department of Veterans' Affairs (DVA) helps veterans; some people are self-funded and pay for everything themselves.
  4. NP7 S3 P4The disability sector is funded by the NDIS (for people under 65). Not for Solomon.

4 Agencies and networks in your region

Your service cannot do everything. An external agency is another organisation that gives a different kind of help. A Network is a group of services, workers and people who share information, resources and ideas.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP7 S4 P1Why networks matter: they help services work together, share information and resources, do projects together, and make support easier for the person.
  2. NP7 S4 P2Aged care networks and agencies: My Aged Care, the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission, COTA (Council on the Ageing), the Older Persons Advocacy Network (OPAN) and ADA Australia (advocacy in Queensland), the Elder Abuse Prevention Unit, Dementia Australia, GPs and allied health, Queensland Health.
  3. NP7 S4 P3For Solomon, for example: the Stroke Foundation (StrokeLine, information and support after a stroke); a community transport service so he can get to appointments and the synagogue; the local Jewish community and synagogue for faith and friends; COTA Queensland for social groups and information.
  4. NP7 S4 P4In your answer: one agency and one network, near where you live (Springfield, Ipswich or your suburb), and how each one helps Solomon.
  5. NP7 S4 P5Tip: the agency must be a different type of service to the one you work for (a home care provider).

5 Cecilia and Ruth: who is in the team?

This case study is in Section 2 of your assessment, for everyone. Cecilia is 34 and has Down syndrome and a moderate intellectual disability. She lives with her Great Aunt Ruth, who is 93. Ruth now needs dialysis three long days a week, and she feels she cannot support Cecilia the way she wants to. The team meets to plan.

Section 2 case study

Cecilia, 34, and Ruth, 93

  • Cecilia has had support from disability services for 15 years. Support workers visit 6 hours a day, 2 days a week.
  • Ruth has chronic kidney disease. Dialysis means up to 5 hours a day, 3 times a week, plus travel, and she will be very tired.
  • The team: Cecilia, Ruth, their GPs and allied health professionals, the supervisors of the disability service, and you, the support worker.
Listen to the explanation

Cecilia

Role: the focus person, at the centre of the planning.

  • Share her wants, needs, likes and limits (as well as she can at the time).
  • Be as independent as she can.
  • Try not to hurt others with her actions.

Ruth

Role: a focus person in her own right, AND Cecilia's full-time support person.

  • Be honest about what she can and cannot do to support Cecilia.
  • Have time to look after her own health.
  • Be a responsible guardian for Cecilia.

GP and allied health professionals

Role: give medical advice and treatment options to Cecilia and Ruth, for a good quality of life for both.

  • See the whole picture for Ruth, with empathy.
  • Link them to other services that can help both of them.

Support worker (you)

Role: part-time support for Cecilia, and so also for Ruth.

  • Give support that helps Cecilia reach the goals in her individual plan.
  • Report any changes or anything unusual to the supervisor.

Supervisor

Role: coordinates between outside agencies, the person and the family; guides and supports the paid workers; helps the family find their way through the sector.

  • Keep the focus on the person.
  • Make sure Cecilia is supported in a way that respects her rights, her needs and her choices.

6 How a good team communicates

A team only works if information gets to the right person, at the right time, in the right way, and everyone has a say.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP7 S6 P1Be respectful to every team member.
  2. NP7 S6 P2Share information on time (timely), for example at handover and in team meetings. Some can be verbal, but key information must be recorded in the progress notes or the person's file.
  3. NP7 S6 P3Make sure every member knows about any change.
  4. NP7 S6 P4Cecilia and Ruth are the key people. Include them in every decision. They have the final say.
  5. NP7 S6 P5Ways to communicate: phone, email, a communication book, Cecilia's file.
  6. NP7 S6 P6Example (put this in your answer): the doctor changes Cecilia's medication. Ruth has signed a consent form so the doctor and the service can share information. The doctor emails the service and the pharmacy. The supervisor writes it in Cecilia's file. The support worker reads the file and looks for the new chart and blister pack. The pharmacy delivers the new chart and pack to Ruth. The support worker talks with Ruth about side effects to watch for, then signs the chart after helping Cecilia take the new tablet at lunch.

7 Assistive technology: the categories

Assistive technology is any tool, equipment or change that helps a person do something they could not do, or could not do safely, on their own. Your assessment lists the categories below (exact names). Give one different example for each.

Listen to the explanation

Carer support

Helps the worker or carer move and support the person safely.

  • Weight bearing lifter (hoist)
  • Slide sheets

Cognition and memory loss

Helps a person remember and stay safe.

  • Alert buttons
  • Pill dispensers and reminders
  • Night and day clocks
  • Lights that turn on by themselves

Continence and hygiene

Helps with toileting and staying clean and dry.

  • Continence pads, aids and sheets
  • Commodes

Daily living activities

Helps with everyday jobs around the home.

  • Long handled claw (pick-up stick)
  • Non-slip mat to open jars
  • Long handled shoehorn

Eating and drinking

Helps a person eat and drink by themselves.

  • One way straws
  • Curved or built-up cutlery
  • Non-slip mats
  • Lip plates

Education and employment

Helps a person learn and work.

  • Literacy software
  • Magnification software
  • Text-to-voice software
  • Speaking to type (dictation)
  • Big, colourful keyboards

Home and other environment

Changes to the home and places the person goes.

  • Ramps
  • Roll-in showers
  • Automatic or wider doors
  • Stair lifts
  • Voice controlled systems

Mobility and transfers

Helps a person move around and get from one place to another.

  • Rollators and walkers
  • Walking sticks
  • Mobility scooters
  • Grab rails in the bathroom
  • Raised toilet seats

Pressure area management

Helps protect the skin from pressure injuries.

  • Monkey bar (trapeze) over the bed
  • Rope bed ladder
  • Heel troughs and splints
  • Pressure relieving mattresses

Recreation and leisure

Helps a person enjoy hobbies and fun.

  • Card holders and large print cards
  • Large dice
  • Raised garden beds and adapted gardening tools
  • Beach wheelchairs and mats

Self-care

Helps a person wash and care for themselves.

  • Long handled sponges
  • No-touch soap dispensers and taps
  • Shower chairs

Vision and hearing loss

Helps a person see and hear.

  • Hearing aids
  • Magnifiers
  • Text-to-voice apps
  • Braille displays and screen readers
  • Phones with big buttons

8 Choosing technology for Solomon

Your assessment asks for two pieces of technology from your list: one that helps Solomon be independent, and one that helps him join in the community. For each: name it, then explain how it helps Solomon.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP7 S8 P1Remember Solomon: a stroke, weakness on his left side, an electric wheelchair, help to transfer, soft foods, slightly slurred speech. He is clever, loves his farm and his faith.
  2. NP7 S8 P2Ideas for independence: built-up or curved cutlery and a lip plate so he can feed himself with one hand; a long handled sponge and shower chair so he can wash more himself; grab rails for safer transfers; a communication app if his speech is hard to understand.
  3. NP7 S8 P3Ideas for joining in: a wheelchair accessible vehicle with a ramp, or community transport, so he can get to the synagogue, the farm markets and his friends; a tablet or phone for video calls with his children overseas; raised garden beds so he can keep growing produce.
  4. NP7 S8 P4Explain the benefit: what will Solomon be able to do, and why does it matter to him? Example: "A wheelchair accessible taxi lets Solomon go to the synagogue on Shabbat, so he keeps his faith community and his friends."

9 How do you know what equipment to use, and how?

On a shift you use lots of equipment: a hoist, a shower chair, a urine bottle, cutlery. How do you know which one, how to use it, and how much help the person needs? Your assessment asks this about Juan (you meet him in Lesson 5).

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP7 S9 P1Read the individual plan (care plan) and make sure you understand it. It says what equipment the person uses and how much help they need.
  2. NP7 S9 P2Ask your supervisor if anything is not clear.
  3. NP7 S9 P3Follow your organisation's policies and procedures: they set how support must be given.
  4. NP7 S9 P4Go to in-service training about equipment and manual handling.
  5. NP7 S9 P5Complete your yearly manual handling competencies.
  6. NP7 S9 P6Never swap in different equipment or guess. If the equipment looks broken or unsafe, do not use it: tag it and report it.
