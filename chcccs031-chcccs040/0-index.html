A Glossary: the words in plain English

These are words you will see in the lessons and in your assessment. The lessons link to this list.

ADLs
Activities of daily living: the everyday tasks of looking after yourself, such as showering, dressing, eating and using the toilet.
Anxiety
Strong worry or fear that does not go away and makes daily life hard.
Assistive technology
Any tool, equipment or change that helps a person do something more easily or more safely, from a curved spoon to a hoist or a ramp.
Bipolar disorder
A mental health condition with big mood changes: very high moods (mania) and very low moods (depression).
Confidentiality
Keeping a person's personal information private. You only share it with people who need it for their care, or with the person's permission.
Conflict
In Erikson's stages, the main challenge a person faces at an age, for example trust or mistrust for a baby.
Depression
Feeling very sad or empty for a long time, and losing interest in things the person used to enjoy.
Down syndrome
A genetic condition a person is born with. It can affect learning and health. Every person with Down syndrome is different.
Dysphagia
Difficulty swallowing. Food or drink can go into the lungs. A speech pathologist decides the safe food texture and drink thickness.
Enduring Power of Attorney
A legal document. It names a person who can make some decisions for someone if they cannot make them any more.
Hemiplegia
Weakness or paralysis on one side of the body, often after a stroke.
IDDSI
The International Dysphagia Diet Standardisation Initiative: the numbered levels for food textures (3 to 7) and drink thickness (0 to 4).
Intellectual disability
A disability that affects how a person learns, understands and solves problems. It can be mild, moderate or severe.
Kosher
Food that follows Jewish food laws.
Life domain
One area of a person's life that affects health, for example exercise, hygiene or food.
Mental health condition
An illness that affects how a person thinks, feels and acts, for example depression or anxiety.
Modified foods
Food made softer or smooth so a person with swallowing problems can eat it safely.
NDIS
The National Disability Insurance Scheme. It funds support for people with disability under 65.
Need
Something a person must have to live and be well, for example food, water, safety and love.
Network
A group of services, workers and people who share information, resources and ideas, so they can support people better.
Occupational therapist
A health professional (OT) who helps people do everyday activities, for example with special equipment or by changing the home.
Progress note
A short written record in the person's file of what you saw, did and were told during your shift.
Protective factor
Something in a person's life that helps keep them well, for example family, friends or a strong faith.
Psychosocial
About the mind (psycho) and about people around us (social). Erikson's stages are psychosocial: they are about how we grow inside and with others.
Respite
A short break. For example, the person goes to a respite centre for a day so they can socialise and their carer can rest.
Risk factor
Something in a person's life that makes a problem more likely, for example being alone or losing a loved one.
Schizophrenia
A mental health condition that changes how a person sees what is real. They may hear voices or believe things that are not true.
Self-actualisation
The top of Maslow's hierarchy: becoming the best you can be, using your strengths and feeling your life has purpose.
Service delivery model
The way a program delivers support to a person, for example Support at Home (help in your own home) or residential aged care.
Shabbat
The Jewish day of rest, from Friday sunset to Saturday night.
Stroke
When blood stops flowing to part of the brain. It can affect movement, speech, swallowing and memory.
Synagogue
A Jewish place of worship.
Uridome
A soft sheath that fits over the penis and drains urine into a bag strapped to the leg.
Want
Something a person would like to have, but can live without.
Wellbeing
How well a person is in body, mind and life: feeling healthy, safe, connected and happy with their life.
