Appendix 1 Abuse

Objectives / Aim

KAMA Community will provide equitable, high quality service to people from all backgrounds regardless of cultural or religious identity. KAMA Community values and respects the rights of all people to live free of abuse. In doing so will

•Meet the United Nations Convention of the Rights of older persons and persons with a disability. One right is to live free from abuse which includes mental and physical abuse, neglect, and exploitation. Where this right has been violated, to have equal recognition by law and access to justice.

•Protecting the health, wellbeing, and safety of clients.

•Provide procedures for staff to follow for preventing and responding to abuse.

•Provide a Code of Conduct to ensure a zero-tolerance approach to abuse is adhered to across all services.

•Create and provide staff with abuse management training and information to enable compliance.

•Create and maintain abuse report documents maintaining privacy, confidentiality, and disclosure laws.

•Development of a coordinated approach to manage suspected or actual abuse promoting the rights of the client and or their significant other (also known as a substitute decision maker).

Scope

This policy applies across all KAMA Community sites and sets out required action by all staff.

Background

KAMA Community is a community agency fundamentally concerned with developing and providing programs, activities and services that respond to the needs of the most disadvantaged and isolated people in the community.

Procedures

All staff are to adhere to the following

•Staff are to attend Abuse Management Training

•Staff are expected to use effective communication and active listening skills when talking to clients.

•Assess persons or maintain an awareness of suspicions of abuse

•Staff must report any suspicions of abuse.

•Staff must report any form of abuse to senior person on duty or supervisor.

•Staff must complete suspected abuse or incident of abuse form.

Staff must maintain the rights of the clients and or their significant other when managing a complaint. The following must be applied.

Risk of serious abuse

When there is a risk or there is a suspicion a person may experience serious physical abuse, sexual abuse, or serious damage to property the following must occur.

•Mandatory/ compulsory reporting is to be completed to senior person on duty or supervisor.

•Police must be notified without seeking permission from the clients or their substitute decision maker.

•Organisational suspicions of abuse form must be completed

Incident of serious/reportable abuse

When a person has experienced physical or sexual abuse or abuse that comes under the guidelines of reportable abuse incidents the following must occur.

•Mandatory/ compulsory reporting is to be completed to senior person on duty or supervisor.

•Police must be notified without seeking permission from the clients or their substitute decision maker.

•Organisational abuse form must be completed

•Reportable Incidents must be reported to the Serious Incident Reporting Scheme or National Disability Insurance Scheme Quality and Safe Guard Commission.

Concerns about financial and personal health matters by the Enduring Power of Attorney

•If the client has capacity the client can be given information about revocation of the Enduring Power of Attorney

•If the client does not have capacity, then a referral should be made to the Office of Adult Guardian for investigation.

Culture Considerations

•If the client who is at risk of or has endured abuse and usually uses family as an interpreter, in this situation use an accredited interpreter service. Contact a cultural advisor.

Maintaining the person’s rights

•Ensure the person or their substitute decision maker is informed about their rights to have the abuse incident reported.

•Store reports to ensure person’s privacy and confidentiality is maintained

Support the person who has experienced an abuse incident

•Staff must ensure the immediate safety of the person.

•Immediately notify their supervisor of the abuse incident.

•People must be provided information about dignity of risk.

•Support the person and or their support person through supportive communication.

•Provide safe guarding options.

•Provide information about abuse support services.

•Staff must follow code of conduct.

•Staff must maintain duty of care principles.

Staff support

Staff are encouraged to seek support to assist them, to manage the physiological impact, supporting a client who has experienced an abusive incident had on them.

Related Documents

•SIRS Manual Form (Aged Care)

We follow SIRS guidelines as outlined in the Aged Care ACT 2024 that is regulated by Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission, and ‘Reportable Incidents’ as outlined in the National Disability Insurance Scheme ACT 2013 that is regulated by the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission.

Appendix 2 Bed Bath

Policy

This procedure should be used when assisting a person complete a bed bath.

Purpose

To provide assistance where needed for a person to maintain their personal hygiene and general health.

Procedure

Before commencing ensure to read the person’s individual plan. This will provide you with essential information regarding:

Personal preferences
Cultural and religious requirements
Special requirements		Equipment required
Level of assistance required

1.Identify yourself to the person, obtain consent to assist with task, explain procedure to be followed to check for accuracy and suitability to person’s needs

2.Gather all required linens and equipment and place at bedside, including soiled laundry skip o Prompt and encourage person to select clothes, gather and place in a clean, accessible location

3.Consult with person regarding toiletries required during process, gather and place in a clean, accessible location

4.Ensure privacy by closing doors and curtains

5.Wash hands and apply PPE as required

6.Adjust bed to a suitable height

7.*NB, person may choose to use toilet facilities first. Ask, and provide assistance where needed.

8.*Follow the guidelines established in the individual plan to guide your participation in the activity.

9.Assist the person to reposition so that they are lying in the middle of the bed. Remove all extra pillows, the person may feel comfortable with 1 or 2 pillows under their head.

10.Remove upper bed clothes, place directly in soiled linen skip. Place a blanket over the person for warmth and dignity.

11.Offer the person a warm wet washer to wash face before commencing.

12.Look for any abnormalities as you begin to wash, and dry the person in the following order

•Water only

•Eyes. Select one part of the wash cloth to wipe from the inner to the outer section of the eye. Choose a different part of the wash cloth to tend to the other eye.

•Rinse washer

•Face, ears and neck

•Adding soap to washer

•Arms and hands (including arm pits)

•Chest, abdomen and back

•Remove clothing from bottom half of body, and place directly in soiled laundry skip.  Legs and feet

•(remove continence pad if in-situ) genitals and groin (reposition continence pad)

•Assist person to reposition on one side

•Wash perianal area including buttocks

13.Bed Linen should be changed during the process. Follow the procedure for changing an occupied bed

14.Assist the person with application of chosen lotions, or need for medicated creams.

15.Assist the person to dress in chosen clothing

16.Assist the person to groom hair, tend to oral hygiene (brush teeth or complete mouth toilet) and apply sunscreen, makeup and accessories.

17.Assist the person to reposition for comfort and ensure call bell and personal items are within reach

18.Assist the person to fit hearing aids, glasses or other assistive devices.

19.Tidy room and clean equipment according to organisational policy

20.Remove PPE and wash hands

21.Report and document any changes to care needs as appropriate

Appendix 3 Bed Making

Policy

This procedure should be used by all staff when changing linen on an occupied, or unoccupied bed.

Definitions

•Bed Cradle - curved, semi-circular device made of metal or plastic pipe that can be placed between the sheets.

•Mitred corner - anchoring sheet under mattress.

•Toe pleat - specifically folding the edge of the top sheet in a fan formation.

Purpose

Regular linen changes promote comfort, ensure a clean environment and provide a quality surface that aids in reducing incidents of pressure ulcers.

Procedure

Before commencing ensure to read the person’s individualised plan to be aware of special requirements and equipment to be used.

* Clean and soiled linen should not touch staff clothing when being handled.

Unoccupied bed

Stage 1

1.Obtain consent from person to perform task

2.Wash and dry hands

3.Collect all required clean linen and place it clean chair or table near bed

Stage 2

1.Put on required PPE

2.Bring soiled linen skip inside person’s room

3.Remove excess furniture, over table from near the bed. Check bed for any personal items and place in a secure location

4.Adjust height of bed and check bed brakes are on

5.Remove pillow cases and put in soiled linen skip. Place pillows on a clean chair or table

6.Remove quilt and blankets separately, folding individually and place on clean chair or table

7.Remove top sheet, rolling it loosely and place it directly into soiled linen skip

8.Remove continence sheet (if applicable), and place directly into soiled linen skip

9.Sanitise waterproof sheeting (if applicable), allow to dry

10.Remove bottom bed linen, rolling it loosely and place it directly into soiled linen skip

11.Remove PPE and sanitise hands

Stage 3

1.Place bottom sheet on the bed, and unfold so that the centre of the bottom sheet runs the length of the bed, ensuring there is ample sheet to tuck in at top of mattress

2.Tuck sheet in using mitred corners at the top of the bed first, and then the bottom of the bed. Ensure that there are no wrinkles

3.Replace waterproof sheeting and continence sheet (if applicable), ensure it is situated to match the person’s sleeping position (approximately 30cm from head of bed)

4.Place top sheet on the bed and unfold so that the centre of the top sheet runs the length of the bed. The top edge only needs to meet the top of the mattress, ensuring that there is ample sheet to tuck in at the bottom of mattress

5.Replace remaining blankets and quilt, mitring bottom corners allowing linen to hang freely

6.If toe pleats are required, this is to occur with all top linens (sheets and blankets)

7.Turn top sheet back over blankets

8.Replace quilt, mitres bottom corners allowing linen to hang freely

9.Apply new cases to pillow and arrange accordingly

10.Readjust height of bed as required for the person

11.Turn bed down if the person is returning to bed soon

12.Replace any furniture and remove linen skip

13.Wash and dry hands.

Occupied Bed

Stage 1

1.Obtain consent from person to perform task

2.Wash and dry hands

3.Collect all required clean linen and place it on clean chair or table near bed

Stage 2

1.Put on required PPE

2.Bring soiled linen skip inside person’s room

3.Remove excess furniture, over table from near the bed

4.Explain each step of the procedure to the person to ensure understanding and consent.

5.Ensure the person’s privacy is maintained at all times

6.Offer the person a bed pan or urinal before commencing the procedure

7.Check bed for any personal items and place in a secure location

8.Adjust height of bed and check bed brakes are on

9.Explain to person that excess pillows need to be removed

10.Remove pillow cases and put in soiled linen skip. Place pillows on a clean chair or table

11.Remove quilt and blankets separately, folding individually and place on clean chair or table

12.Remove top sheet, rolling it loosely and place it directly into soiled linen skip

13.Cover person with blanket, towel or sheet for dignity and warmth

Occupied Bed (continued)

Stage 3

1.Continue to work with the person, informing them of each step of the procedure

2.Assist the person to reposition so that they are lying on their side

3.On the vacant side of the bed, loosen all bottom sheets and roll length ways towards the centre of the bed, up close to the person’s body.

4.If continence sheet and waterproof sheet are present then

•Loosen all sheets

•Roll continence sheet towards centre of bed first

•Lightly fold waterproof sheet

•Then roll bottom sheet in towards centre of the bed

5.Place bottom sheet on the vacant side of the mattress, and unfold so that the centre of the bottom sheet will run the length of the bed, ensuring there is ample sheet to tuck in at top of mattress

6.Tuck sheet in on that side, using mitred corners at the top of the bed first, and then the bottom of the bed. Straighten the remainder of the sheet to ensure no wrinkles

7.Lie the waterproof sheeting across the new bottom sheet, place the new continence sheet (if applicable), and tuck in firmly on that side.

8.Roll up remainder of continence sheet towards the centre of the bed

9.This leaves two portions of rolled linen – soiled and clean.

10.Assist the person to roll over the ‘small bump’ of linens

11.Remove the soiled linens one at a time, rolled loosely and placed directly into the soiled linen skip

12.Unroll the clean linen, starting with bottom sheet. Ensure that bottom sheet is taut and tucked in with mitred corners starting at the top of the mattress

13.Smooth out the waterproof sheet, and roll out the continence sheet, tucking them in firmly (if applicable)

Stage 4

1.When situating the top linens, never cover the face or head of the person.

2.Remove the person’s dignity cover

3.Place top sheet on the bed, and unfold so that the centre of the top sheet runs the length of the bed. The top edge only needs to meet the top of the mattress, ensuring that there is ample sheet to tuck in at the bottom of mattress.

4.Replace remaining blankets and quilt, mitring bottom corners allowing linen to hang freely

5.If toe pleats are required, this is to occur with all top linens (sheets and blankets)

6.Turn top sheet back over blankets

7.Apply new cases to pillows and arrange accordingly. Change pillow case on pillow being used

8.Assist the person to reposition to ensure comfortable

9.Replace call bell and any personal items that are needed

10.Readjust height of bed as required for the person

11.Replace any furniture and remove linen skip

12.Wash and dry hands.

Appendix 4 Communication – Progress Notes

Policy

In line with KAMA Community privacy policy, our practice will to be professionally and respectfully maintain client private information in relation to delivery of service. Documents will be of consistent quality and structure, emphasising the good recording of information to meet regulatory requirements.

Scope

This policy applies across all KAMA Community sites and sets out required action by all staff. Providing guiding principles for good written documentation.

Background

KAMA Community Centre is a community agency fundamentally concerned with social justice, encompassing issues of equity and inclusion for all in the community. Cultural or ethnic identity should not be a disadvantage to community participation or access to services.

Legislation

Privacy Act 1988

The Queensland Information Privacy Act 2009

Policy

Progress Notes are important because it provides the accountability needed to show compliance with professional, operational and legislative requirements. It is used to record client preferences, needs and goals, to establish a standard of support and interaction. Staff record work activities completed, required resources, concerns and changes observed. Progress Notes also demonstrates allocation of staff and provides accountability for funding.

Progress Notes should be completed in a timely manner, same shift preferable; or within one day of interaction.

Procedures

•Principles of report writing

•To ensure effective quality documentation follow these basic guides

•Progress Notes are to be person-focussed

•Meet evidence-based practices by using objective writing style

•Are non-discriminatory and culturally inclusive

•Record information that is relevant, and in an active first person voice

•Should be done by the person who completed the task or participated in the event.

•Circumstances when to complete Progress Notes

The examples provided are not exclusive to situations where progress notes are required.

•Information regarding support provided to complete tasks

•Administrative tasks

•Medical records, referrals, clinical assessments or interactions

•Behaviour observations

•Case Management, including, reviews and casework

•Child protection, elder abuse, or abuse of people with disability

•Counselling

•Legal matters

•Methodology

Entries should include the:

•location and purpose of interaction

•summary of events, what was witnessed, completed, seen and/or heard

•all relating factors

•actions performed by person and/or staff

•actions unable to be performed by person and/or staff

•what strategies were used

•what goals were determined

•what actions need to take place

•who this information was reported to

•any referrals that may need to be made

•When completing electronic documents

•Individual ID and password used to access database

•Completed in a timely manner

•Time, date and signature are automatically logged

•When completing written documents

•Record chronologically and are completed in a timely manner

•Time and date of writing to be provided

•Entries must be legible and completed in black ink only

•Errors to be crossed with a single line and initialled (No white out or other correction fluid to be used)

•Entries must be signed, with full name included

Related Documents:

•Progress Note Template

•Case Management Plan Performa

•Client Consent Form

•Critical Incident Report Form

•Privacy Policy

Appendix 5 Manual Handling of People

Policy

This contains multiple procedures that relate to providing assistance to people to transfer safely. The high risk tasks included in the Manual Handling of People are performed by trained staff, using equipment and techniques assessed by Health Professionals as required to meet that person’s needs.

Purpose

The aim is to ensure that people are being safely supported with their mobility and transfer needs in accordance with legislation and sector practices. In doing so, uphold the health and safety of the staff who are supporting them.

Definitions

•Slide Sheet - polyester sheet that is placed between a person and a flat surface to facilitate safe transfers/repositioning.

•Sling -canvas device designed to use with specific mechanical hoists to facilitate the safe transfer from one location to another.

•Mechanical Hoist - battery operated machine used to facilitate transfer of people from one location to another. Can be weight bearing (person must have some strength in lower and upper limbs to sustain a supported standing position) or non-weight bearing (the person is suspended in a sling).

•Detachable Handle - assistive device that can be used in car doors to provide extra stability.

Procedure

Before commencing ensure to read the person’s individual plan. This will provide you with essential information regarding

Personal preferences
Cultural and religious requirements
Special requirements		Equipment required
Level of assistance required

Staff must have completed training and organisation competency before using mechanical hoists.

Assist a person to stand

*NB level of assistance will depend upon the person. Staff may only be required to provide verbal prompts on how to stand. It should be reported when exertion is required to assist a person to stand. The person will be referred to specialist for assessment and revision of manual handling plan.

1.Wash hands and seek assistance where required

2.Identify yourself to the person, obtain consent to assist with task, explain procedure to be followed to check for accuracy and suitability to person’s needs

3.Gather necessary equipment (mobility aid)

4.Prompt the person to shuffle their bottom forward in the chair so that their feet can sit flat on the floor and they are in an upright position.

5.Prompt the person to

•place their hands on the arms of the chair

•lean forward

•in a rocking motion of 1, 2, 3, push forward

•using their leg muscles and forward momentum to raise their centre of gravity and stand up

6.Have the person’s mobility aid in reach, and provide this to them to get stability

Assist a person to sit

*NB level of assistance will depend upon the person. Staff may only be required to provide verbal prompts on how to sit. It should be reported when exertion is required to assist a person to sit, or the person is ‘falling’ rather than sitting. The person will be referred to specialist for assessment and revision of manual handling plan.

1.Identify yourself to the person, obtain consent to assist with task, explain procedure to be followed to check for accuracy and suitability to person’s needs

2.Prompt the person to position themselves in front of the chair, with the back of their legs against the seat.

3.Prompt the person to reach down with one hand and grab onto the arm of the chair. This will put the person in a semi-bent position. Head forward, bottom out.

4.As the person reaches out with other hand to hold the arm of the chair, encourage them to push their bottom further out and sit back in the chair

5.Assist the person to get comfortable

6.Situate the person’s personal belongings in reach.

7.Tend to hand hygiene

Assist a person to transfer in and out of a car

1.Wash hands and seek assistance where required

2.Identify yourself to the person, obtain consent to assist with task, explain procedure to be followed to check for accuracy and suitability to person’s needs

3.Gather necessary equipment (slide sheet, towel, detachable handle)

4.To get in a car

•Open car door

•If person is using a wheelie walker

•Prompt person to approach open door, and transfer hands to car door for support

•When person is standing safely, remove wheelie walker

•If person is using a wheelchair

•Prompt them to raise their feet, raise and retract foot pedals.

•Position wheelchair angularly in open door

•Prompt person to get to standing position. Hold onto door, or use detachable handle, for leverage and security.

•When person is standing safely, remove wheelchair

•Prompt person to turn so that their back is against the car opening

•The person may choose to hold onto the open window, or detachable handle as support. Prompt the person to lean forward, and then lower their bottom onto the seat. Assist as needed

•Once seated, encourage the person to lift their right leg and place it into the car. Assist as needed

•Adjust their seating to comfortable, and then encourage the person to lift their left leg into the car. Assist as needed

•Prompt and assist the person to get comfortable. Assist as needed

•Prompt or assist the person to do up seat belt and close car door.

5.To get out of a car

•Open car door

•Prompt or assist the person to undo seat belt.

•Prompt person to adjust their position, and lift left leg out of the car and place it on the ground. Assist as needed

•Prompt the person to lift their right leg out of the car and place it on the ground. Assist as needed

•Prompt the person to lean forward, and using the door (or detachable handle) as leverage, stand up. Assist as needed.

•Provide person with their mobility device or if person is using a wheelchair

•Angle wheelchair into door opening.

•Prompt person to place left hand on left arm of the wheelchair and shuffle step so that the backs of their legs are against the wheelchair seat

•Prompt person to place right hand on the right arm of the wheelchair.

•Prompt person to slightly lean forward, push bottom back and sit down.

•Prompt them to raise their feet, and reverse wheelchair out from car.

•Prompt person to raise their feet and place on foot pedals.

6.Report any significant observations.

Repositioning using a slide sheet

1.Complete hand hygiene

2.Identify yourself to the person, obtain consent to assist with task, explain procedure to be followed to check for accuracy and suitability to person’s needs

3.Collect and prepare equipment for use. Slide sheet and 2nd staff member

4.Close doors and curtains for privacy.

5.Remove excess furniture, over table and any personal items that are on the bed.

6.One staff member each side of the bed

7.Adjust bed to suitable height (where possible to flat) and ensure that brakes are on

8.Lower blankets and top sheet to foot of bed. Maintain the person’s dignity by ensuring that body is covered appropriately.

9.Work as a team of three (3) providing clear instructions of each step, and not performing a manual task until each person is ready

10.Position the slide sheet

•Assist the person to roll onto side using log roll

•Depending on person’s requirement

•When using one slide sheet, fold it in half width ways

•When using two slide sheets, place flat together

•Staff Member A (the staff member standing behind the person)

•Take the sides of the slide sheet with the open edges, and (by pressing hands down into the mattress, and up under the person’s body) position edges under the bony prominences (shoulder and hip).

•This will leave the folded edge facing the opposite direction the person is facing. (when using one sheet)

•Gather half of the remaining slide sheet and roll it towards the centre of the bed, against the person’s back. This will create a small hump.

•Assist the person to roll onto the other side, over the small hump

•Staff Member B will then unravel the slide sheet, lying it flat against the bed. The open edges of the slide sheet are with Staff Member B:

•Assist the person to roll onto their back

•Both staff are to ensure that the slide sheet is flat under the person. Check that the person’s hair is not caught, or that the slide sheet will friction against their neck or thighs.

11.Execute the turn

•Staff adopt a stance where legs are within shoulder width, one foot in front of the other

•Staff Member B gather the edges of the top slide sheet until your hands are close to the person’s shoulder and hip, maintaining a firm grasp.

•Staff Member A to place open palmed hands on the person’s hip and shoulder

•Encourage person to keep arms crossed on their chest, and where possible tuck chin into chest

•Using a fluid, coordinated movement. Advise using counting of 1, 2, 3 (or similar):

•Staff Member B will rock back, forward and back

•Staff Member A will rock forward, back and forward

•On the third movement:

•Staff Member B will pull back and slightly down with their hands, bringing the slide sheet across the bed

•Staff Member A will push forward aiding with the movement of the person across the bed

•The person should not raise off the bed, it is a slide across

•Check person’s position, and skin for any signs of pressure damage

12.Remove the slide sheet

•If using one slide sheet:

•Staff Member A, ensure that the edges of the slide sheet are not caught in the person’s clothing, hair or body parts, roll any excess slide sheet towards their body

•Staff Member B, grasp the bottom slide sheet at the hip and shoulder, and pull alternating left hand, then right hand. Scissoring the slide sheet out from under the person

•If using two slide sheets:

•Staff Member A, ensure that the edges of the bottom slide sheet are not caught in the person’s clothing, hair or body parts, roll any excess slide sheet towards their body

•Staff Member B, grasp the bottom slide sheet at the hip and shoulder, and pull alternating left hand, then right hand. Scissoring the slide sheet out from under the person

•Staff Member A, takes the outer edge of the remaining slide sheet and folds under, between the slide sheet and the bed.

•Staff Member B, takes the edge and uses it to pull through the slide sheet on itself, not the person

•Put slide sheet away.

13.Check positioning of their shoulder, knees, feet and arms. Adjust pillows, and reposition bedding to ensure the person is comfortable.

14.Return call bell, personal items and other furniture that had been moved

15.Adjust bed to appropriate height.

16.Tend to hand hygiene

17.Document and report where required

Assist a person to transfer using weight bearing mechanical hoist

1.Tend to hand hygiene

2.Identify yourself to the person, obtain consent to assist with task, explain procedure to be followed to check for accuracy and suitability to person’s needs

3.Gather necessary equipment, as outlined in the individual plan, and a second staff member. Two people are always required for this procedure

4.Prepare the environment. Ensure that

•all hazards have been identified and addressed

•there is a clear logical path from location A to location B (for example, bed to wheelchair)

•If location B is a wheelchair or other mobile device, ensure it is prepared to receive the person and brakes are on

•there is sufficient room to operate the hoist

5.Position sling

•Work as a team of three (3) providing clear instructions of each step, and not performing a manual task until each person is ready

•Prompt the person to sit forward in the chair, or to a sitting position on the bed. Assist as needed

•Stand on one side of the person with co-worker on opposite side (Staff Member A, Staff Member B)

•Lower the bed to appropriate level so that person’s feet are touching the floor

•Maintain person’s dignity and comfort by ensuring correct alignment of clothes during the procedure

•Place the sling around the person, and position according to manufacturer’s instructions.

•Sash belts are firm against a person’s hips.

•Rolled cuff goes around the person’s mid back and under arms.

6.Attach the sling

•Encourage person lift their feet and align hoist to person and bed

•Observe to ensure that hoist does not hit person when being positioned

•Prompt person to correctly position feet on the foot plate

•Attach sling straps according to manufacturer’s instructions

7.Execute the transfer

•Using a fluid, coordinated movement. Advise using counting of 1, 2, 3 (or similar)

•Prompt person to hold onto machine in required position

•Staff Member A to work controls and commence lift. Observe for any tightening of sling or strap on the person’s skin.

•Staff Member B to provide reassurance to person, and observe that sling is conforming to the person’s body and maintaining their weight as it supports the person from the surface.

•As person’s bottom raises from surface, ensure that their knees are against the hoist guard.

•Staff Member B to provide support as Staff Member A manoeuvres hoist away from location A towards location B.

•Position person appropriately over location B.

•Using a fluid, coordinated movement. Advise using counting of 1, 2, 3 (or similar)

•Staff Member A to commence to lower the hoist arm.

•Prompt person to push their bottom out

•Staff Member B to observe for correct positioning as person lowers, any hazards that appear and comfort of the person

9.Assist person to reach required venue, ensuring access to personal items and call bell as appropriate

10.Return hoist and sling to appropriate place

11.Return battery to battery charger

12.Tend to hand hygiene

13.Document and report any relevant information noted throughout the procedure

Appendix 7 Medication Administration

Policy

This policy and procedure relate to the safe storage and administration of oral medication and relating practices to uphold the safety and rights of people using the service.

Purpose

The aim is to ensure that staff only assist where the person lacks capacity to do a specific task, when a person has consented to being assisted with the task, and that specific staff member has the authority to assist.

Kama Community upholds the person’s rights to refuse medication.

Authority to administer medications

Registered Nurses (RN) and Endorsed Enrolled Nurses (EN) are qualified and work under a Scope of Practice that guides the safe administration of medication. Qualified support workers who have undertaken medication training and deemed medication competent for the organisation, can be delegated medication administration tasks by, and under the supervision of the RN or supervisor.

Safe disposal of medication

The person has the right to refuse medication, and incidents can occur where a medication has been dispensed, but will not be taken by the person. In these instances, unused S4 medication is to be discarded in the yellow bin in the medication trolley. The person’s medication chart must be signed accordingly. Discarded medications are returned to the pharmacy each week. Unused S8 medications are to be placed in the sharps container by a supervised RN (residential setting). Both staff must sign the medication chart to verify that the S8 medication was disposed of. Staff in a community setting will require the person or family member to co-sign discarded S8 medication.

Medication Incident Form

A medication incident form must be completed if staff make or find an error during any stage of the medication administration procedure. The completed form is submitted to the supervisor in a timely manner for recording and any actioning.

Safe storage of medication

Medication room, medications and medication trolleys must be locked when unattended. People will be assisted to store medications safely in their own home using a locked box. Key’s to medication are available to authorised staff, who are responsible for the security of the medication during that shift.

Procedure for assisting with pre-packaged oral medications

1.Confirm (with RN/supervisor) the authority to proceed with medication actions.

2.Collect required equipment. For example medication chart, pre-packaged medication, pil bob, hand sanitiser, medicine cup, cup of water

3.Tend to hand hygiene

4.Introduce yourself and obtain consent from the person to assist with the task.

5.Complete the Rights

•Right Person – name on medication chart matches name on pre-packaged medication

•Right Medicine –medication name, strength and form on medication chart matches information on pre-packaged medication

•Right Dose – medication dose on medication chart matches information on pre-packaged medication

•Right Route – information on medication chart matches information on pre-packaged medication

•Right Time – information on medication chart matches information on pre-packaged medication.

6.Ensure that the correct number of each medication as listed on the pre-packaged medication match the number of each medication in the required time slot.

7.Confirm identify of the person (match name and picture from chart to person), confirm consent, and the level of assistance required.

8.Conduct pre-condition check on person. Including physical and behavioural changes

9.Use pilbob to dispense correct time slot medication from packaging. Double check that correct number of pills are dispensed.

10.Prompt/assist person to administer medication by the correct method and route.

11.Confirm medication has been ingested and sign medication chart.

12.Dispose of waste items. Clean and return equipment after use.

13.Tend to hand hygiene.

14.Conduct post-dose condition of person. Including physical and behavioural changes

15.Return pre-packaged medication and medication chart to correct storage location.

16.Seek assistance from supervisor if any irregularities or errors occur

•an electric toothbrush

•Toothpaste selection

•Use a small amount of fluoride toothpaste for natural teeth, or

•denture paste for dentures

d.Encourage the person to relax their lips and cheeks

Appendix 10 Privacy

Objectives /Aim

KAMA Community will provide equitable, high quality service to people from all backgrounds regardless of cultural or religious identity. KAMA Community values and respects the privacy of the people with deal with, and are committed to protecting your privacy and complying with the Privacy Act 1988 (cth), Queensland Information Privacy Act 2009 and other applicable privacy laws and regulations.

Scope

This policy applies across all KAMA Community sites and sets out required action by all staff.

Background

KAMA Community is a community agency fundamentally concerned with developing and providing programs, activities and services that respond to the needs of the most disadvantaged and isolated people in the community.

Definitions

A privacy statement is a document that declares the intentions of the organisation in relation to client information and data, how personal information is stored, how clients can access this information and the purposes for which personal information is used and disclosed.

Legislation

We apply the Queensland Information Privacy Act 2009 and Privacy Act 1988 that includes health-specific privacy principles which guide how we collect and manage personal information, including health information.

“Personal information” means any information or opinion, whether true or not, and whether recorded in a material form or not, about an identified individual or an individual who is reasonably identifiable. At KAMA Community Centre, we describe information about you using three terms – personal information, sensitive information and health information.

Personal information: is information that identifies a living person.

Sensitive information: is a subset of personal information, and includes information relating to health, race, ethnicity, religious beliefs, sexual preferences or practices and criminal records.

Health information: is information or an opinion about an individual’s physical or mental health or disability, health preferences including future provision of health services, use of health services, bodily donations and genetics

Policy

Why we collect information

Specific Personal Information is collected so that we can provide you with the best and most suitable assistance and support. We only collect information that is necessary for our functions, activities and level of activity to be provided, to ensure that we support you efficiently and effectively.

Information collected

•Name

•Date of Birth

•Telephone number

•Name and phone number for emergency contact

At times we are required to collect government identifiers such as Medicare, Pension of Veterans Affairs numbers. We will only use or disclose this information in accordance with the Act.

Additional information collected will is reliant on forms to be completed. This directly relates to the level and type of assistance required. This will also include a Consent Form, where you will authorise to whom, and under what circumstances, we can communicate with others regarding your needs.

Anonymity

Please understand that we will only be able to provide limited services if you choose to remain anonymous, or use a pseudonym.

How your personal information is used:

•Enabling better coordination of support between team members

•Providing information to a responsible person (eg a parent, guardian) if you are incapable of communicating

•Service monitoring, planning, evaluation and complaint handling

•Quality assurance processes (Audits and accreditation)

•Provision of reminders for appointments or follow-up support

•Advanced computer technology with firewall and intrusion identifiers to store Encrypted digital files

•Individual staff login with passwords

•Inclusion of Code of Conduct with ongoing privacy and confidentiality training for staff

Client Rights to Access

Applications can be made to access information that KAMA has collected about you. Information will be supplied within 30 days of receiving request.

Removal of Client Information

KAMA will need to maintain your information for the duration of the professional relationship, and where application time outlined in the legislation for record keeping obligations.

3.Consult with person regarding toiletries required during process, and prompt and encourage to set up all required equipment in the bathroom. Assist where needed

4.Consult with person regarding ventilation in bathroom, as well as room temperature. Adjust as needed

5.Ensure privacy by closing doors and curtains

6.Apply PPE as required

•*NB, person may choose to use toilet first. Ask, and provide assistance where needed.

•*NB, mobility and transfer needs of the individual person to be considered

7.Prompt or assist person to mobilise to bathroom.

8.Prompt, encourage or assist person to turn on water. Check water temperature, before asking person to check (forearm or thigh is a suitable location to test). Adjust as needed.

13.Continue to encourage the person to do as much as possible while taking into account their preferences

14.If you are required to assist - Ensure face, neck, arms, torso, legs, feet, back and genital areas are washed and rinsed thoroughly, checking under skin folds for any signs of excoriation or changes in skin integrity

•Changes in skin integrity can include rashes, scratch marks, skin tears, bruising, and lesions

15.Consult with person as to other hygiene needs, such as hair washing. Assist where needed.

•When washing a person’s hair a good practice is to hold a washer over eyes to prevent shampoo getting in them

18.Prompt and encourage the person to dress. Assist where needed.

19.Prompt and encourage the person to groom hair, brush teeth, and apply sunscreen, makeup and accessories. Assist where needed.

20.Prompt or assist the person back to room ensuring they are comfortable and has call bell and personal items within easy reach

21.Leave bathroom clean and tidy, disposing of soiled linen

22.Clean equipment according to organisational policy

23.Remove PPE and wash hands

24.Report and document any changes to care needs as appropriate

Appendix 13 Toileting Assistance

Policy

This contains multiple procedures that relate to assisting a person to maintain their urinary and bowel continence.

Purpose

The aim is to provide dignity, enhance comfort and promote social inclusion by tending to a person’s individual toileting needs.

Continence management program

A continence management program is developed for each person to meet their individual needs.

Follow requirements for:

•Type of continence pad to be used for day and night

•Toileting schedules

•Habits and practices used to promote bowel health and regularity

•Equipment used to promote continence eg commode

•Continence sheets and related bedding

•Continence aids such as catheters

Procedure

Before commencing ensure to read the person’s individual plan. This will provide you with essential information regarding:

1.Continence management plan

2.Personal preferences

3.Cultural and religious requirements

4.Special aids and schedules

5.Equipment required

6.Level of assistance required

Assist with toileting – toilet or commode

1.Identify yourself to the person, obtain consent to assist with task, explain procedure to be followed to check for accuracy and suitability to person’s needs

2.Wash hands and apply PPE as required

3.Collect and prepare equipment for use. Commode, mobile shower chair, toilet paper, continence aid, plastic bag

4.Follow procedure:

•Prompt the person to mobilise to toilet (if using toilet). Assist where needed.

•Prompt the person to adjust clothing and sit on commode or toilet. Assist where required.

•If continence aid is soiled, remove and replace as per instructions below

•Ensure that person is sitting comfortably

•Place towel over lap to provide dignity

•Provide appropriate privacy to tend to toileting needs.

•If leaving person alone, ensure that call bell is within reach.

•Prompt the person to tend to toilet hygiene. Assist where needed.

•Assist person to stand. Position pad if needed.

•Prompt the person to adjust underwear and clothing. Assist where required.

5.Remove PPE and wash hands

6.Clean room and equipment. Dispose of waste appropriately

7.Assist person to relocate to chosen destination

Assist with toileting – bed pan or urine bottle

1.Identify yourself to the person, obtain consent to assist with task, explain procedure to be followed to check for accuracy and suitability to person’s needs

2.Collect and prepare equipment for use. Bedpan or urine bottle (with covers), toilet paper

3.Ensure privacy by closing doors and curtains

4.Wash hands and apply PPE as required

5.Adjust bed to suitable height and position

6.Assist person to adjust bed sheets and clothing to facilitate toilet use.

7.Follow procedure:

•Bed pan:

•Encourage person to bend knees and push down with feet to raise their bottom off bed OR, assist person to roll onto their side to position bed pan

•Assist person to get a comfortable position using pillows, or raising bed head

•Discreetly cover person’s body

•Provide appropriate privacy to tend to toilet needs

•Prompt person to clean with toilet paper. Assist where required

•Encourage person to bed knees and push down on feet to raise their bottom off the bed, OR, carefully assist the person to roll slightly in order to remove bed pan

•Urine bottle:

•Assist the person to position penis in urine bottle

•Ensure that bottle is positioned to capture urine without leakage

•Discreetly cover person’s body

•Provide appropriate privacy to tend to toilet needs

8.Provide person with a warm, wet wash cloth to tend to hand hygiene. Assist where needed

9.Assist the person to adjust clothing and become comfortable

10.Readjust bed height to suit the person

11.Assess contents of bed pan/urine bottle. Measure where required

12.Dispose of appropriately

13.Take bad pan/urine bottle to dirty utility room for sterilising

14.Remove PPE and wash hands

15.Report any significant changes such as mouth ulcers or fungal lesions.

Assist with changing of continence pad

1.Identify yourself to the person, obtain consent to assist with task, explain procedure to be followed to check for accuracy and suitability to person’s needs

2.Collect equipment for use. This will vary for each person and could include: bowl with warm water, wash cloth, towel, incontinence aid, toilet paper, plastic bag for soiled continence aid.

3.Ensure privacy by closing doors and curtains

4.Wash hands and apply PPE as required

5.Follow directions if in bed:

•Adjust height of bed

•Keep person informed of each step along the way ensuring that they understand and provide consent to complete

•Lower sheets and clothing to access continence pad.

•Remove pad by rolling it in upon itself, and place directly into plastic bag.

•Remove any solid matter using toilet roll, and place directly into plastic bag.

•Use warm water and cloth to wash groin, perianal area and buttocks

•Pat dry thoroughly.

•Position pad properly, assisting the person to roll from side to side to ensure a proper fit.

•Replace clothing and bed linens

•Adjust height of bed, ensure person is comfortably positioned with all necessary items in reach

•Remove PPE and wash hands

•Clean room and equipment. Dispose of waste appropriately

6.Follow directions if person is using toilet:

•Keep person informed of each step along the way ensuring that they understand and provide consent to complete

•Prompt the person to remove lower clothing. Assist where required

•Remove soiled pad by rolling it in upon itself, and place directly into plastic bag.

•Hand person a warm wet cloth to wash groin, perianal area and buttocks. Assist where needed.

•Hand the person a towel to dry. Assist where needed.

•Assist person to stand. Position pad from front to back, ensuring that the pad sits properly against the skin. Assist the person to adjust underwear

•Assist the person to redress where needed

•Remove PPE and wash hands

•Clean room and equipment. Dispose of waste appropriately

•Assist person to relocate to chosen destination

Change catheter drainage bag

1.Identify yourself to the person, obtain consent to assist with task, explain procedure to be followed to check for accuracy and suitability to person’s needs

2.Collect and prepare equipment for use. This could include: bowl, clamp, drainage bag, bin/plastic bag

3.Ensure privacy by closing doors and curtains

4.Adjust the bed to suitable height

5.Wash hands and apply PPE as required

6.Follow directions to empty the drainage bag first

7.Follow directions to change drainage bag:

•Have new sterile bag positioned within reach

•Hold catheter tubing at point of connection to drainage bag

•Pinch/clamp the top side of the connection and disconnect existing bag. Placing directly into plastic bag

•Remove cover from connector on new bag, and immediately connect to catheter tubing.

•Ensure that connection is firm

8.Release hold on catheter tubing. Ensure that tubing is free draining with no kinks and the drainage bag is:

•is secured to the person's leg, or

•attached to the hanger and suspended from chair or bed, at a height that is below the bladder

9.Remove and clean all equipment and the environment

10.Remove PPE and wash hands

11.Clean area, reopen curtains and return equipment

12.Document as required. Report any significant findings.

Emptying catheter drainage bag

1.Identify yourself to the person, obtain consent to assist with task, explain procedure to be followed to check for accuracy and suitability to person’s needs

2.Wash hands and apply PPE as required

3.Collect and prepare equipment for use, eg container, urine bottle

4.Ensure client's privacy (shut doors, curtains and draw screens around the bed when appropriate)

5.Follow procedure

6.Adjust the bed to suitable height:

•Check that the catheter and tubing are secure on leg, bed or chair

•Place container or urine bottle under valve of drainage bag before opening the valve

•Close the valve after allowing bag to drain

7.Readjust bed height ensuring the catheter bag is not on the floor and is not above the client's bladder. Both of these positions increase the risk of infection

8.Assess and measure volume if required

9.Dispose of urine accordingly

10.Clean area and returns equipment

11.Remove PPE and wash hands

12.Reopen the curtains

13.Complete required documentation and report any significant findings
