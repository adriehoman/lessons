It is 7.45 in the morning and you are Juan's support worker for the next three hours. In this lesson you follow the shift step by step, the same way your assessment does: the urine bottle, breakfast, shaving, the shower, getting dressed, his denture, and getting ready to go out. At every step ask: What can Juan do himself? How do I keep him safe? How do I protect his privacy?

1 Juan's morning: getting ready **

You will help Juan from 7.45 am to 10.30 am. Read about him first. Everything you do this morning comes from his plan and his choices.

Your client this morning

Juan

  • Only the two smallest fingers on his left hand work (a partial amputation).
  • His legs are weak and he struggles to stand alone.
  • With the physiotherapist he chose a weight bearing hoist for transfers and a motorised wheelchair to get around.
  • He likes to use the urine bottle before he gets out of bed, have breakfast, then shave and shower after breakfast, on a mobile shower chair.
  • At 10.30 a wheelchair taxi takes him to lunch with friends at the local club, with some shopping first.
Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S1 P1Get the equipment ready first: urine bottle, gloves, hoist and sling, wheelchair, shower chair, toiletries, towels, clothes he chooses.
  2. NP11 S1 P2Check each item: clean, and no damage that could hurt him or you.
  3. NP11 S1 P3Place it where it is easy to reach for you and for Juan, in the order you will use it.

2 Helping Juan use the urine bottle **

Juan wants to use the urine bottle before he gets out of bed. Your assessment asks for a detailed explanation, including safe manual handling. Every step counts.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S2 P1Knock, introduce yourself, explain what you will do, and get Juan's consent. Tell him each step as you go.
  2. NP11 S2 P2Privacy: close the door and curtains.
  3. NP11 S2 P3Hand hygiene, then put on gloves.
  4. NP11 S2 P4Position him safely: raise the bed to a safe working height, help him sit up or roll a little using a slide sheet if he needs it. Never drag him.
  5. NP11 S2 P5Give him the bottle. Help him place it correctly only as much as he needs (remember his left hand). Cover him with the sheet.
  6. NP11 S2 P6Make sure the call bell is in reach, then leave the room to give him privacy, if it is safe.
  7. NP11 S2 P7When he calls: put on gloves, remove the bottle carefully, cover it, and help him get comfortable.
  8. NP11 S2 P8Look at the urine (colour, amount, smell), empty it in the toilet, clean the bottle and put it back in its place.
  9. NP11 S2 P9Remove your gloves, do hand hygiene, and offer Juan a hand wash. Lower the bed. Record and report anything unusual.

3 What is normal? Stools and urine

When you help with toileting, you look at what comes out. You are often the first person to notice a change. The Bristol Stool Chart gives every type a number, so everyone uses the same words. Press Play all and watch the person on the toilet.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S3 P1Stools (poo): types 3 and 4 are normal. Types 1 and 2 mean constipation. Types 6 and 7 mean diarrhoea. Report constipation, diarrhoea, and any blood, black or very pale stools.
  2. NP11 S3 P2Stools change with food and drink, how the bowel works, illness, medication and the person's habits.
  3. NP11 S3 P3Urine (wee): an adult passes about 1 to 1.5 litres a day. Normal urine is straw coloured, clear, with only a slight smell.
  4. NP11 S3 P4The colour shows how much the person drinks and how the kidneys work. Dark, cloudy, smelly, red or brown urine must be reported.
  5. NP11 S3 P5Write what you see in the progress notes or bowel chart, and tell your supervisor about anything unusual.

5 Swallowing safely: dysphagia and IDDSI

Dysphagia means difficulty swallowing. It is common after a stroke (remember Solomon) and in older age. Food or drink can go into the lungs and cause choking or pneumonia.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S5 P1A speech pathologist assesses the person and decides the safe food texture and drink thickness.
  2. NP11 S5 P2The levels come from IDDSI, a worldwide framework: drinks from 0 (thin) to 4 (extremely thick), foods from 3 (liquidised) to 7 (regular).
  3. NP11 S5 P3Always give exactly the texture and thickness in the plan. Never "a little bit" of something else.
  4. NP11 S5 P4Sit the person upright, offer small mouthfuls, go slowly, and stay with them. Watch for coughing, a wet voice or choking, and report it.
  5. NP11 S5 P5Remember Solomon: his food must be kosher AND the right texture.

6 A pleasant mealtime, and aids for eating **

Juan has breakfast in the dining room. Your assessment asks how to make the dining room pleasant, and which aids help Juan eat and drink by himself.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S6 P1Temperature: not too hot, not too cold, so he is comfortable.
  2. NP11 S6 P2Smells: pleasant, no bad odours.
  3. NP11 S6 P3Light: bright enough to see the food.
  4. NP11 S6 P4Table: clean and tidy, or a clean tablecloth.
  5. NP11 S6 P5Place setting: cutlery, cup and condiments set up within easy reach, in the right places for him.
  6. NP11 S6 P6Noise: turn off loud, annoying noise. Friendly chatter or soft music can be welcoming.
  7. NP11 S6 P7Aids for Juan: modified cutlery (thick or bent handles) that is easier to hold, a non-slip mat to stop the plate moving, a lip plate (raised edge) to push food onto the spoon.

7 Setting up so Juan can shave himself **

After breakfast you help Juan onto his mobile shower chair. He likes to shave before his shower. Your assessment asks how you set up the space and equipment so he can shave by himself.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S7 P1Gather everything he needs: razor, shaving cream or gel, a mirror, a face washer, a towel.
  2. NP11 S7 P2Place it within reach, in the order he will use it, for example on the bench next to the sink or behind the sink.
  3. NP11 S7 P3Position Juan in front of the sink so he does not have to bend or stretch to reach the sink, the taps or his things.
  4. NP11 S7 P4Put a towel over his lap, so it is easy for him to reach.
  5. NP11 S7 P5Then step back: let him do it. Help only if he asks or needs it.

8 The shower: the help Juan needs, and his privacy **

Your assessment asks what physical help Juan may need for each part of the shower, and how you protect his privacy. He does most things himself.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S8 P1Privacy: close the doors and curtains.
  2. NP11 S8 P2Put towels over his body when he is not in the shower.
  3. NP11 S8 P3Do not stare, and do not talk about his body parts.

A: Undressing

Help with buttons or zips. Help to lower his pants while he stands in the hoist.

B: Washing during the shower

Help to wash his back, feet and lower legs, and some areas of his buttocks while he stands in the hoist.

C: Drying after the shower

Help to dry his feet and lower legs, and parts of his lower back and buttocks while he stands in the hoist.

D: Dressing

Help with buttons or zips, putting on his pants and doing them up, and his socks and shoes.

Grooming is how a person presents themselves to the world. Your assessment asks for four grooming actions, and how you help Juan choose what to wear.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S10 P1Grooming actions: putting on moisturiser, powder, makeup, styling hair, perfume or cologne, putting on jewellery, trimming facial hair, filing fingernails.

11 Dentures and the mouth **

Juan sleeps with his top denture in and cleans around it after every meal. Today the top denture falls down onto his tongue when he opens his mouth. He says, "It's fine. I'll put more denture glue on." He agrees to see a specialist.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S11 P1Impact of a loose denture: it rubs on the gums, causing pain and ulcers. The pain can make eating hard and limit the foods he can eat.
  2. NP11 S11 P2Signs of a poorly fitting denture: a clicking sound when talking, the top denture dropping when he opens his mouth or talks, a pained face when eating, regular sore gums, trouble swallowing or speaking.
  3. NP11 S11 P3Gums shrink over time after teeth are taken out, so dentures can become loose.
  4. NP11 S11 P4When you clean a denture, look for: cracks, chips and other imperfections that could cause pain, and make sure all food is removed.
  5. NP11 S11 P5Natural teeth: use a toothbrush with a small head and soft bristles. Brush gently in small circles, covering the front and back of all teeth, top and bottom. Gently brush along the gum line.

12 Reporting the denture problem: write it AND say it **

Juan agreed to see a specialist. Your organisation can only help him get a referral if you tell them. Your assessment asks what you document and verbally report.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S12 P1Write a progress note: what you saw (the top denture falls onto his tongue when he opens his mouth) and the conversation with Juan (he said it is fine and he would use more glue; he agreed to a specialist appointment).
  2. NP11 S12 P2Tell your supervisor (verbal report), so they can follow up with Juan and make the appointment.
  3. NP11 S12 P3Write facts, in plain words, with the date, time and your name. Do not guess or give opinions.

14 Leo asks about Juan **

The taxi driver is your friend Leo. A week later at a party he asks: "What happened to his left hand? Can he walk? Has he ever crashed the wheelchair?" Your assessment asks how you answer politely, and how you say it.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP11 S14 P1How you say it: a calm, friendly face and a gentle voice. You are not angry with Leo, you are protecting Juan.
  2. NP11 S14 P2What you say: "Sorry Leo, I can't share any personal information about Juan without his permission. It would hurt Juan and the trust we have built, it could cause problems in his life, and I could lose my job."
  3. NP11 S14 P3This is confidentiality. Keeping it is part of your professional duty and your organisation's privacy policy. It applies everywhere, at work and outside work, including on social media.
