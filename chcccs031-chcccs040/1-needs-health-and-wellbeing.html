Before you can support a person, you need to understand what every person needs to be well. In this lesson you meet Solomon, the person in your case study, then learn about needs, how people grow through life, and what keeps the body and mind healthy.

2 Needs and wants: the five categories of human needs **

A Need is something you must have. A Want is something you would like to have, but you can live without it. Your assessment uses five categories of human needs.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S2 P1Physical: what the body needs to stay alive and healthy: air, water, food, shelter, sleep, exercise, hygiene.
  2. NP3 S2 P2Psychological: what the mind needs: meaning and purpose, feeling you have done something good in the world, feeling capable and independent.
  3. NP3 S2 P3Spiritual: faith, or a belief in something bigger than us, that gives life meaning. It may or may not be a religion.
  4. NP3 S2 P4Cultural: belonging to your culture: its customs, rituals, language, food and celebrations, with people who share them.
  5. NP3 S2 P5Sexual: close and loving relationships, feeling connected to another person, and showing who you are (for example by clothes and behaviour).
  6. NP3 S2 P6Many lists also add social needs: friends, family and belonging. Social needs often sit inside the other categories.

Physical

3 Maslow's hierarchy of needs: self-actualisation **

Abraham Maslow put human needs in a pyramid. The needs at the bottom (food, safety) usually come first. The top is Self-actualisation.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S3 P1The levels, from the bottom: physiological (food, water, sleep), safety, love and belonging, esteem (respect and feeling good about yourself), and self-actualisation at the top.
  2. NP3 S3 P2Self-actualisation means reaching your full potential: you reach the goals you work towards.
  3. NP3 S3 P3You know and use your strengths and abilities, and you find a purpose in life.
  4. NP3 S3 P4It is being the best version of yourself.
  5. NP3 S3 P5The person feels fulfilled, content (happy with life) and has inner peace.
  6. NP3 S3 P6It is easier to reach the top when the needs lower down are met first. Not everyone gets there.

4 Erikson's stages: how people grow through life **

Erik Erikson described eight stages of life, from baby to old age. They are Psychosocial stages: about how we grow inside, and with other people.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S4 P1Why the stages matter (purpose): at each age range, a person meets a main challenge, called a psychosocial crisis or Conflict.
  2. NP3 S4 P2A good outcome helps the person grow and feel strong.
  3. NP3 S4 P3A poor outcome can leave a conflict that is not resolved, and the person may have difficulties later in life.
  4. NP3 S4 P4Knowing a person's stage helps you give the right kind of support for their age and life.

Stage 1 (birth to 1): trust vs mistrust

A baby learns to trust when they are cared for and their needs are met. Neglect can lead to mistrust.

Stage 2 (1 to 3): autonomy vs shame/doubt

A young child wants to do things alone. Encouragement builds independence. Too much criticism brings shame and doubt.

Stage 3 (3 to 6): initiative vs guilt

Children play, imagine and try new ideas. If this is stopped, they may feel guilty.

Stage 4 (6 to 11): industry vs inferiority

Children build skills at school and with friends. Without support they can feel not good enough.

Stage 5 (12 to 18): identity vs role confusion

Teenagers explore who they are and what they believe. They can feel confused about their place.

Stage 6 (18 to 40): intimacy vs isolation

Young adults build close, long-term relationships. Without them, they can feel alone.

Stage 7 (40 to 65): generativity vs stagnation

Adults give back through work, family or the community. Without this, they can feel stuck.

Stage 8 (65 and older): integrity vs despair

Older people look back on their life. They feel fulfilled, or they feel regret.

6 Seven categories of wellbeing, and the support you can give **

Wellbeing has many parts. For each part, think: what support could a support worker give Solomon, or which outside service could help him?

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S6 P1Financial: since Hannah died, Solomon may need to update his Will or name a new Enduring Power of Attorney. Help him make the phone call to his lawyer, and tell your team leader so transport can be booked.
  2. NP3 S6 P2Physical: help with ADLs (personal care, transfers, moving around), transport and exercise.
  3. NP3 S6 P3Social: he may feel alone on the farm. Help him get ready for outings, suggest a Respite centre each week, and encourage family visits or video calls.
  4. NP3 S6 P4Cultural: Solomon is from Israel and follows Judaism, for example Shabbat, reading the Torah and Kosher food. Help find and prepare suitable meals, get ready for religious events, and contact the local Synagogue for information.
  5. NP3 S6 P5Career and occupation: he is used to being busy and running a business. With your manager, link him to an Occupational therapist for technology (computer control, communication apps) so he can stay involved.
  6. NP3 S6 P6Spiritual: help him stay connected with his synagogue: transport, religious programs on TV or online, and getting ready (special clothes, religious items).
  7. NP3 S6 P7Psychological: he may still be grieving Hannah and his loss of independence. Report the signs. Counselling may help (suggest a referral). More time with family or spiritual guidance may also help. Show respect, empathy and companionship.

7 Good health: one strategy for each life domain **

Each Life domain needs its own strategy. Match it to Solomon's age, health, faith and culture.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S7 P1Exercise: a physiotherapy program and breathing exercises, to help Solomon after his stroke.
  2. NP3 S7 P2Lifestyle practices: avoid illegal and recreational drugs, and drinking too much alcohol.
  3. NP3 S7 P3Mental health: relax, think positive, talk through problems with friends, get help when needed (spiritual or medical), and sleep well.
  4. NP3 S7 P4Hygiene: shower every day, change clothes every day, and wash hands often.
  5. NP3 S7 P5Nutrition and hydration: eat a balanced diet, with food of the right texture (Solomon needs modified food), and drink 8 to 10 glasses of water a day.
  6. NP3 S7 P6Oral health: brush teeth regularly, look after dentures, avoid sweet and processed snacks, drink water, and see the dentist regularly.

9 Common mental health conditions and daily life **

A Mental health condition can change how a person thinks, feels and joins in with life. Your assessment asks for two conditions and how each affects daily life.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S9 P1Depression: long-lasting sadness or emptiness. The person loses interest in activities, friends and daily tasks, and may pull away from people.
  2. NP3 S9 P2Anxiety: strong worry and fear that does not stop. The person may avoid new things, outings or meeting people.
  3. NP3 S9 P3Bipolar disorder: big mood swings, from very high to very low. The mood can change whether the person wants to join in.
  4. NP3 S9 P4Schizophrenia: changes in how the person sees what is real, and in behaviour. Medicine helps, but problems can come back if the medicine is not taken.
  5. NP3 S9 P5Other conditions you may meet: post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), obsessive compulsive disorder (OCD), eating disorders, substance use disorder, psychosis.

10 Risk factors and protective factors **

Imagine Solomon has one of these conditions. A Risk factor makes things harder. A Protective factor helps. Take them from his case study, not from a general list.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP3 S10 P1Risk factors for Solomon: no family living close by; grieving the loss of his wife; coping with big changes to his body after the stroke.
  2. NP3 S10 P2Protective factors for Solomon: his faith community; support workers visiting most days; his family connections; his education and intelligence; money to pay for therapy and medical care.
  3. NP3 S10 P3General examples of risk factors: family violence, few friends or family, low self-esteem, poverty, homelessness, not being able to reach services. General protective factors: positive role models, good health, education, a job, a stable home, friends, community ties. Use these only to help you find the ones in Solomon's story.
