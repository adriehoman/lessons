You are a support worker at a Respite Centre. Twelve people are at the centre today for a cultural craft and food sharing day. It is 11.45 am and you are helping people with their 12 pm medication. As a support worker you assist with pre-packaged medication (blister packs). You follow the chart and the procedure exactly, every time.

1 Your role, the Rights, and the RIGHT person

A support worker assists a person to take their pre-packaged medication, only when the plan says so and only after training. Before you give anything, you check the Rights.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP13 S1 P1How to confirm the RIGHT person: check the chart for their name, date of birth and photo.
  2. NP13 S1 P2Then ask the person their name and date of birth, and compare the photo with the person in front of you.
  3. NP13 S1 P3Twelve people are at the centre today, so this check is very important. Never just call out a name.

Right person

The medication is for this person. Check the name, date of birth and photo.

Right medication

The medication on the chart matches the pack.

Right dose

The right number and strength of tablets.

Right time

The time on the chart, for example 12 pm. Not early, not late.

Right route

How it goes into the body, for example oral (by mouth).

Right documentation

Sign the chart after the person has taken it.

Right to refuse

The person can say no. You respect it and report it.

2 Reading the chart: times and route

This is Roselea Montgomery's medication chart. Look closely. Each medication has a dose, a route and a frequency (how often).

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP13 S2 P1Paracetamol is taken TDS (three times a day): at 0800, 1200 and 1700 (8 am, 12 pm and 5 pm: breakfast, lunch and dinner). 24-hour time is best.
  2. NP13 S2 P2Route of the 12 pm medications: oral (O, by mouth).
  3. NP13 S2 P3Useful abbreviations: BD = twice a day; TDS = three times a day; NOCTE = at night; O = oral.
  4. NP13 S2 P4Always read the times from the chart. Do not guess from the meal times.

3 Pain at 10.30: can she have the 12 pm tablets now?

Roselea says she has pain at 10.30 am. Can you give her the 12 pm paracetamol from her pack now?

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP13 S3 P1No. It is too close to the dose she had at breakfast (8 am), so it is not safe to give it again now.
  2. NP13 S3 P2No. Support workers do not have the authority to give medication at any time other than the prescribed time.
  3. NP13 S3 P3What you do: tell the nurse or your supervisor. A doctor must approve a different pain medicine, OR a qualified nurse can assess her and may give a PRN ("when needed") medication.
  4. NP13 S3 P4Comfort her in the meantime and write it in her notes.

4 Checking the blister pack before you open it

Before you take anything out of the pack, you check that the pack is right for this person at this time.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP13 S4 P1Match the details: the medications, the dose and the times on the chart match what is listed on the back of the blister pack.
  2. NP13 S4 P2Check each pill: the description of each pill on the back of the pack matches the pills in the time slot you are giving.
  3. NP13 S4 P3Check the number: there is the correct number of each medication for the dose.
  4. NP13 S4 P4Count: the total number of pills in the time slot matches the total listed on the chart.
  5. NP13 S4 P5Also check the pack is not damaged or opened, has the right name, and is in date.

The front of Roselea's pack

Her name, photo, pharmacy, and the time slots for every day of the week.

The back of Roselea's pack

The list of each medication, the dose, the time it is given, and a description of each tablet.

5 "What is Metformin?" Staying in your scope

You offer Roselea her tablets. She asks: "What is Metformin? Why am I taking it?" You want to help, but you must stay inside your scope.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP13 S5 P1Refer her to the registered nurse on duty if there is one, or to her pharmacist or GP (for example, she can ask at her next appointment).
  2. NP13 S5 P2If your organisation has them, use MIMS or the medicine information sheet from the pharmacist to give simple, correct information.
  3. NP13 S5 P3Keep it short and simple, for example: "Metformin is a common medicine for type 2 diabetes. It helps keep your blood sugar under control." Do not give in-depth medical advice.
  4. NP13 S5 P4Mild side effects of Metformin: feeling sick (nausea), headaches, diarrhoea, stomach aches, loss of appetite, a metallic taste in the mouth, feeling tired (lethargy), indigestion, gas.
  5. NP13 S5 P5Rare side effects of Metformin: low vitamin B, severe tiredness, shallow or fast breathing, a slow pulse, yellow skin or yellow whites of the eyes, trouble swallowing, loss of strength, behaviour changes (acting as if drunk).
  6. NP13 S5 P6If a person has an adverse (bad) reaction: get help from your supervisor or the RN straight away, get medical attention if needed, write it in the progress notes, and complete a medication incident form.

6 When the person says no

Roselea thanks you for explaining Metformin. But she says she does not want any medication today. That is her right.

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP13 S6 P1Dispose of the medication safely, following your organisation's procedure.
  2. NP13 S6 P2On the medication chart, write R (refused) and your initials.
  3. NP13 S6 P3Tell your supervisor (verbal report) and write it in her file.
  4. NP13 S6 P4Never force, trick or hide medication in food or drink.

7 Jacob's pack: spot the faults

Next you pick up the blister pack for Jacob Edwards. Look at the front and the back. What is wrong?

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP13 S7 P1What is wrong: the packaging has been tampered with (opened and damaged blisters); the pack was made in 2012, so the medication is out of date; the names are different on the front (Jacob) and the back (Joseph).
  2. NP13 S7 P2What you do: do not give the medication.
  3. NP13 S7 P3Ask Jacob if he brought the right pack from home.
  4. NP13 S7 P4Tell your supervisor straight away, write it in his file, and complete a medication incident form.
  5. NP13 S7 P5Your organisation may then arrange, with Jacob and his family, another pack from home, a new pack from the pharmacy, or a GP appointment.

The front of the pack

Look at the name, the date and the blisters.

The back of the pack

Look at the name and the foil.

8 Jasper cannot swallow: respond, record, report

Jasper puts the tablets in his mouth and sips water. He moves his head side to side and tilts it back, again and again. Then he spits the tablets into his hand: "It won't go down."

Listen to the explanation
  1. NP13 S8 P1Dispose of the medication safely, following your organisation's procedure.
  2. NP13 S8 P2Help Jasper take a drink of water to clear any tablet residue from his mouth, so he is comfortable.
  3. NP13 S8 P3Complete the medication chart and a medication incident form.
  4. NP13 S8 P4Report all of this to your supervisor, so Jasper can be supported.
  5. NP13 S8 P5The doctor is contacted to review his medication and check his health (he may have a swallowing problem: remember dysphagia).
  6. NP13 S8 P6Never put the spat-out tablets back in his mouth or in the pack.
